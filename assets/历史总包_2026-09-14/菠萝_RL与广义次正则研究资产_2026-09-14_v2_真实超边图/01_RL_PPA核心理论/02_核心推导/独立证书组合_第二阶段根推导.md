# Phase 2 root derivations: separated verification and natural coverage

## A. Separation principle

Let (F:\mathbb R^n\rightrightarrows\mathbb R^n), (S=F^{-1}(0)\cap D) be closed, and fix (\lambda>0). Suppose two logically independent certificates have been established on compatible local domains.

**RL-side certificate.** Every controlled PPA step (x^+\in J_{\lambda F}(x)) and every required (p\in P_S(x)) lie in a graph patch on which

\[
\|(x^+-p)-\lambda((x-x^+)/\lambda-0)\|
\le L\|(x^+-p)+\lambda((x-x^+)/\lambda-0)\|^\gamma .
\]

Consequently,

\[
s:=\|x-x^+\|\le \frac{d+Ld^\gamma}{2},
\qquad d=d(x,S).
\]

The same RL-side analysis separately certifies existence/selection of the controlled resolvent step.

**Error-bound-side certificate.** On the output region, independently of how the RL certificate was obtained,

\[
d(y,S)\le K d(0,F(y))^q.
\]

The only interface requirements are the same restricted operator/zero set (S), norm, step size (\lambda), output domain, and PPA selection. No common native information source is required.

Since ((x-x^+)/\lambda\in F(x^+)),

\[
d_+:=d(x^+,S)
\le K(s/\lambda)^q
\le K\left(\frac{d+Ld^\gamma}{2\lambda}\right)^q.
\tag{A.1}
\]

For (0<\gamma<1), this yields:

- if (\gamma q>1), (d_+=O(d^{\gamma q})), hence local superlinear distance convergence;
- if (\gamma q=1), the asymptotic Q-factor certificate is
  \[
  \kappa_0=K\left(\frac{L}{2\lambda}\right)^q,
  \]
  and strict local contraction follows when (\kappa_0<1);
- if (\gamma q<1), these two aggregate certificates alone are inconclusive.

Thus the two verification pipelines are modular and meet only at (A.1).

## B. A natural conic generalized-equation class

Let (C\subset\mathbb R^r) be a nonempty closed convex cone, (a,b,\lambda>0), (0<\alpha<1), and (e\in\mathbb R^m) be a unit vector. Define

\[
F(t,z)=\bigl(-b\|z\|^\alpha e,\;az+N_C(z)\bigr),
\qquad (t,z)\in\mathbb R^m\times\mathbb R^r.
\tag{B.1}
\]

This is a triangular conic generalized equation: the normal subsystem is the standard strongly monotone variational inequality (0\in az+N_C(z)), while the neutral/tangential variable is driven by a sublinear amplitude of the active normal state. The map is genuinely multivalued on the boundary of (C), and

\[
S=F^{-1}(0)=\mathbb R^m\times\{0\}.
\]

Indeed, (0\in az+N_C(z)) and (z\in C) imply, after pairing with (z), (a\|z\|^2=0).

Let

\[
K_C(q)=P_C\left(\frac{q}{1+\lambda a}\right).
\]

The Minty equation has the unique global solution

\[
J_{\lambda F}(p,q)
=\left(p+\lambda b\|K_C(q)\|^\alpha e,\;K_C(q)\right).
\tag{B.2}
\]

The map (K_C) is (k=(1+\lambda a)^{-1})-Lipschitz. Moreover (R_C=2K_C-I) is nonexpansive: firm nonexpansiveness of (P_C), with (c=(1+\lambda a)^{-1}\in(0,1)), gives

\[
\|2c(P_Cx-P_Cy)-(x-y)\|^2\le\|x-y\|^2.
\]

Therefore on every Minty input set of diameter at most (\delta), the reflected resolvent satisfies RL with

\[
\gamma=\alpha,
\qquad
L_\delta=2\lambda b(1+\lambda a)^{-\alpha}+\delta^{1-\alpha}.
\tag{B.3}
\]

If (C) contains a nonzero ray, comparison along that ray proves that the best local power exponent at every solution input is exactly (\alpha).

Independently, direct residual growth gives

\[
d(0,F(t,z))\ge b\|z\|^\alpha,
\qquad
d((t,z),S)=\|z\|,
\]

and hence

\[
d((t,z),S)\le b^{-1/\alpha}d(0,F(t,z))^{1/\alpha}.
\tag{B.4}
\]

Thus (q=1/\alpha), (K=b^{-1/\alpha}). Combining the independently verified (B.3) and (B.4),

\[
K\left(\frac{L_0}{2\lambda}\right)^{1/\alpha}
=\frac1{1+\lambda a}<1,
\qquad
L_0=2\lambda b(1+\lambda a)^{-\alpha}.
\]

The actual normal-distance factor from (B.2) is at most ((1+\lambda a)^{-1}), with equality on rays on which the projection acts identically. The RL step bound also makes the tangential forcing summable, hence gives convergence of the full orbit to an individual point of (S).

## C. Smooth-manifold obstruction

Let (F:\mathbb R^n\to\mathbb R^n) be (C^1) near (p\), and suppose (S=F^{-1}(0)) is a (C^1) manifold near (p\), (F|_S=0\). Assume the full local inverse (J=(I+\lambda F)^{-1}) is single-valued on an input neighborhood and satisfies

\[
d(Jx,S)\le\kappa d(x,S),\qquad \kappa<1.
\tag{C.1}
\]

Then (I+\lambda DF(p)) is nonsingular. Otherwise take (v\ne0) with ((I+\lambda DF(p))v=0). Since (DF(p)w=0) for (w\in T_S(p)), such (v) cannot lie in (T_S(p)). Hence

\[
d(p+tv,S)=\Theta(|t|),
\]

whereas

\[
(I+\lambda F)(p+tv)=p+o(t).
\]

Putting (x_t=(I+\lambda F)(p+tv)) into (C.1) gives a contradiction. Thus the classical inverse theorem makes (J), and therefore the reflected resolvent, locally Lipschitz. Genuine (\gamma<1) plus local distance contraction must therefore exploit nonsmooth/multivalued geometry, a singular solution set, an incomplete inverse image, or a weaker selection/coverage regime; it cannot arise from an ordinary smooth full-neighborhood manifold setting.
