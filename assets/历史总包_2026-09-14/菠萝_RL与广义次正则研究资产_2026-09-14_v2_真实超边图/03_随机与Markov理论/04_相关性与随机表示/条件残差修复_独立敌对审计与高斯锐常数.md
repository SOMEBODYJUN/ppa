## 2. Frozen marginal, changed metric, and actual invariant problem

### 2.1 Legal construction

For a fixed conserved marginal \(\nu\), the space

\[
\mathscr M_\nu=\{\nu(du)\mu_u(dx)\}
\]

and the distance

\[
\mathsf W_\nu^2(\mu,\eta)=\int W_2^2(\mu_u,\eta_u)\,d\nu
\]

are legitimate. Measurable optimal couplings/disintegration are established conditional-OT tools. For binary fibers all relevant measurability can also be checked directly without invoking a broad theorem. The kernel preserves \(\nu\), so analyzing its actual law orbit on this invariant class does not change the algorithm or its invariant law within that class.

**GO:** the conditional residual is legal and its zero set is the actual invariant law in \(\mathscr M_\nu\).

### 2.2 What the construction does not prove

The conditional metric is stronger than ordinary joint \(W_2\), not a locally equivalent replacement in general. All statements about optimal constants, necessary-and-sufficient relative geometric convergence, and exact rates are in \(\mathsf W_\nu\), unless explicitly stated otherwise.

The result is not a proof of necessity for the original synchronous discrepancy. It changes both the certificate and the transport restriction. Conditional convergence implies ordinary joint-Wasserstein convergence when the latter is defined, but sharp ordinary-Wasserstein rates, reverse implications and ordinary-Wasserstein local necessities do not follow.

There is nevertheless a genuine ordinary-Wasserstein **sufficient** interface, not only a change of subject. If \(\mathcal I=\operatorname{inv}P\), then

\[
d_{W_2}(\mu,\mathcal I)
\le W_2(\mu,\pi_\nu)
\le\mathsf W_\nu(\mu,\pi_\nu)
\le K\mathcal R(\mu).
\]

Thus the replacement residual does provide a valid ordinary-Wasserstein invariant-set EB and convergence certificate whenever its conditional estimates hold. What does not transfer is optimality, necessity, or an ordinary-Wasserstein relative rate normalized by the smaller ordinary initial error. This distinction should be emphasized so the legitimate positive repair is not understated.

For an ambient Euclidean conserved variable one should explicitly assume \(\nu\) has a finite second moment whenever making an ordinary joint \(W_2\) statement. The conditional metric on finite fibers is finite without that assumption, since it charges only fiber motion. This is a minor domain repair, not a defect in the conditional theorem.

### 2.3 No unmatched RL energy

The report correctly separates the local-specification residual \(\mathcal R\) from the synchronous coupling defect. The displayed two-bit counterexample to

\[
E_+^2+\mathcal R^2\le E^2
\]

is correct: the three squared quantities are \(1/8,1/4,1/4\), respectively. Hence the coordinate EB and the contraction theorem cannot be combined by simply inserting \(\mathcal R\) into the RL energy identity.

For the actual random projections, the state-level geometry is \(\gamma=1,L=1\) on each fiber. Nothing here establishes a genuine \(\gamma<1\) advantage or permits applying the finite-dimensional T5 theorem verbatim to this measure space.

## 3. Sharp binary-refresh theorem and its quantifiers

### 3.1 Tensorization proof: PASS

The sequential coupling proof is sound. Its first marginal is the given law \(\alpha\), its second marginal is the product target, and conditional Jensen gives

\[
W_2^2(\alpha,\beta)
\le\sum_i\mathbb E_\alpha
\left|\alpha(X_i=1\mid X_{-i})-b_i\right|.
\]

Conditioning on null events does not affect the integral. Positivity of the target Bernoulli parameters is sufficient; a common bound away from zero and one is not needed.

### 3.2 Optimal constants: PASS

With

\[
a_*={\rm ess\,inf}_{u}\min_i p_i(u),
\]

the sharp constants are indeed

\[
K_*=a_*^{-1/2},\qquad c_*=(1-a_*)^{1/2},
\]

and \(c_{*,k}=(1-a_*)^{k/2}\). The convention \(a_*=0\Rightarrow K_*=+\infty\) is natural.

The sharpness proof remains valid when the essential infimum is not attained. Finitely many coordinates ensure that, for each \(\epsilon>0\), at least one coordinate has a positive-measure set with \(p_i<a_*+\epsilon\). Perturbing just that factor leaves all other conditional discrepancies zero and gives

\[
E^2=\int_A h\,d\nu,\quad
\mathcal R^2=\int_Ap_i h\,d\nu,\quad
E_k^2=\int_A(1-p_i)^kh\,d\nu.
\]

Choosing the farther endpoint guarantees an available perturbation, even for highly nonuniform \(b_i(u)\). Scaling its mixing amount makes the witnesses arbitrarily local while preserving every ratio.

### 3.3 Equivalences: PASS, with quantifiers retained

The following equivalence is correctly proved:

\[
a_*>0
\iff \text{uniform linear EB}
\iff \text{uniform one-step contraction}
\iff \text{uniform fixed-block contraction}
\iff \text{uniform relative geometric rate}.
\]

“Uniform” means one set of constants for **all initial laws** in the fixed class \(\mathscr M_\nu\), or all initial laws in a specified full conditional-metric ball. It does not mean one constant chosen separately for each trajectory. The last implication uses a single block length with \(Cc^N<1\), so allowing an initial-law-dependent \(C\) would change the assertion.

These are not equivalent to arbitrary pointwise convergence, to an absolute convergence rate unnormalized by the initial error, or to an ordinary joint-Wasserstein local statement. The report generally preserves these distinctions; they should remain in any abstract or theorem extraction.

Exact invariant identification with merely pointwise \(p_i(u)>0\) is also correct. Fiberwise contraction identifies the target; preservation of \(u\) allows disintegration of the invariance identity.

## 4. Nonuniform gauges, length and countable continuous RFI

### 4.1 Knapsack formula: PASS

For one binary coordinate every measurable error density \(0\le h\le M\) is realizable. Thus

\[
\phi(t)^2=\sup\left\{\int h:\ 0\le h\le M,\ \int ah\le t^2\right\}
\]

is the smallest nondecreasing uniform gauge. Its scalar dual

\[
\inf_{\lambda\ge0}\left[\lambda t^2+\int M(1-\lambda a)_+\,d\nu\right]
\]

is correct, including atoms: partial filling is possible on a threshold atom because \(h\) is a real-valued density. Saturated budgets are handled by \(\lambda=0\); the zero-budget endpoint follows by a limit. Positivity of \(a\) almost everywhere gives \(\phi(t)\to0\).

### 4.2 Actual rates and length: PASS

The identities

\[
E_k^2=\int(1-a)^kh\,d\nu,\qquad
\mathsf W_\nu^2(\mu P^{k+1},\mu P^k)=\int a(1-a)^kh\,d\nu
\]

are exact because binary \(W_2^2\) is the absolute difference of Bernoulli probabilities and the error sign does not change under refresh. Dominated convergence proves convergence of every law and the exact worst-law curve. Summing the square roots gives actual, not merely estimated, conditional law-space length.

For \(a(u)=u^r\), uniform \(\nu\), \(b=1/2\), both the gauge coefficient and the beta-integral asymptotics are correct. The worst-law length is finite exactly for \(r<1\), while convergence holds for every \(r>0\). The example \(a(u)=e^{-1/u}\) excludes every positive Hölder gauge and still has a valid general gauge.

This is about law-space length; it is not sample-path finite length. A refresh chain can keep changing its sampled state forever even while its law has already reached equilibrium.

### 4.3 Countable continuous-map implementation: PASS

For \(U=\{0\}\cup\{2^{-n}\}\), each map changing only a single isolated fiber is continuous, including at the accumulation fiber: for a fixed map its exceptional fiber is eventually absent from any sequence tending to zero. The probabilities are summable and leave positive identity probability. The conserved law charges no zero-refresh fiber, so the conditional convergence theorem applies.

The expected almost-firm bound is valid. If the original bits differ, the output-plus-defect bit energy equals the original bit energy. If they agree on different fibers, the expected excess is \(a_n+a_m\). The geometric separation estimate gives the stated uniform bound \(17/256\). The independent full-map enumeration, not only the reduced ratio formula, also passes.

This realizes countably many continuous i.i.d. maps on a compact Euclidean subset. It is not a finite-family construction and does not prove an analogous claim for any source theorem that additionally requires finitely many maps.

### 4.4 Exact weak-Poincaré reduction: novelty warning

For the fair-bit case put \(g(u,x)=\sqrt{2h(u)}(2x-1)\), on \(\pi=\nu\otimes\operatorname{Bern}(1/2)\). Then \(g\) is centered on each conserved fiber, \(\|g\|_\infty\le1\), and

\[
\|g\|_{L^2(\pi)}^2=2E^2,
\qquad
\langle g,(I-P)g\rangle=2\mathcal R^2.
\]

Conversely every bounded fiber-centered binary function is of this form up to its sign. On that subspace \(P\) is the multiplication operator \(g\mapsto(1-a)g\). In particular,

\[
E_{2n}^2=\tfrac12\|P^n g\|_{L^2(\pi)}^2.
\]

The optimal weak-Poincaré remainder for \(\|g\|_\infty\le1\) is exactly

\[
\sup_g\{\|g\|_2^2-\lambda\langle g,(I-P)g\rangle\}
=\int(1-\lambda a)_+\,d\nu.
\]

Thus the gauge dual is an optimal weak-Poincaré profile on the complement of the conserved sigma-algebra. This is an exact algebraic relationship, not merely an analogy to “mixtures of rates.” Because the full kernel is nonergodic, one must use this invariant-complement restriction rather than directly cite an ergodic full-space theorem. Nevertheless, the profile optimization and spectral rate mechanism are mature, so their re-expression as a generalized conditional EB is not by itself a new foundational theory.

## 5. Coupled Gaussian improvement: optimal EB coefficient

The reported Gaussian theorem is correct but needlessly weak. Its conditional residual satisfies the following stronger statement.

### Proposition A — sharp conditional Gaussian error bound

Use the report's definitions \(Q\succ0\), \(D=\operatorname{diag}(p_i/Q_{ii})\),

\[
\zeta=\lambda_{\min}(Q^{1/2}DQ^{1/2})>0,
\]

\(\beta=N(m,Q^{-1})\), \(p_i>0\), \(\sum_i p_i=1\). For every finite-second-moment law \(\mu\),

\[
\boxed{W_{2,Q}(\mu,\beta)^2\le\zeta^{-1}\mathcal R_Q(\mu)^2.}
\tag{A1}
\]

The coefficient \(\zeta^{-1/2}\) in the unsquared inequality is optimal globally for this fixed \(Q,p\).

#### Proof

Couple a chain which refreshes from \(\mu\)'s full conditional distributions with the target Gaussian Gibbs chain. Start with any coupling \((X,Y)\) having marginals \(\mu,\beta\). At a coordinate \(i\):

1. Given \(X_{-i}\), optimally couple \(X'_i\sim\mu_i(\cdot\mid X_{-i})\) with \(Z_i\sim\beta_i(\cdot\mid X_{-i})\).
2. Put
   \[
   Y'_i=Z_i+m_i(Y_{-i})-m_i(X_{-i}),
   \]
   where \(m_i(x_{-i})=m_i-\sum_{j\ne i}Q_{ij}(x_j-m_j)/Q_{ii}\) is the Gaussian conditional mean. Leave other coordinates unchanged.

The second update has the correct Gaussian conditional at \(Y_{-i}\), since its conditional variance is independent of the conditioning value. The two full marginals remain \(\mu\) and \(\beta\). Measurable couplings may be constructed by one-dimensional quantiles; null conditioning events do not affect expectations.

Set \(h=X-Y\), \(\varepsilon_i=X'_i-Z_i\), and

\[
A_i=I-e_ie_i^TQ/Q_{ii}.
\]

Then

\[
h'=A_ih+e_i\varepsilon_i,
\qquad A_i^TQe_i=0.
\]

Consequently the cross term vanishes pointwise:

\[
\|h'\|_Q^2=\|A_ih\|_Q^2+Q_{ii}\varepsilon_i^2.
\]

If \(C_n\) is the expected coupling cost after \(n\) such coupled updates, the unchanged first marginal gives

\[
C_{n+1}
=C_n-\mathbb E[h^TQDQh]+\mathcal R_Q(\mu)^2
\le(1-\zeta)C_n+\mathcal R_Q(\mu)^2.
\]

Every \(C_n\) is the cost of a coupling of the same marginals, so \(W_{2,Q}^2(\mu,\beta)\le C_n\). Iterating and letting \(n\to\infty\) proves (A1). No stationary-coupling existence, continuity of \(\mu\)'s conditionals, or compactness is needed.

For sharpness take \(\mu=N(m+v,Q^{-1})\). Then

\[
W_{2,Q}^2(\mu,\beta)=v^TQv,
\qquad
\mathcal R_Q(\mu)^2=v^TQDQv.
\]

Choose \(Q^{1/2}v\) to be a unit eigenvector for \(\zeta\). Equality follows. Scaling \(v\) also makes equality witnesses arbitrarily close to \(\beta\); hence the local constant on a full \(W_{2,Q}\)-ball cannot improve. \(\square\)

### Why this matters, and the remaining caution

For small \(\zeta\), the original constant is about \(2/\zeta\), whereas the exact coefficient is \(1/\sqrt\zeta\). This is a substantial quantitative repair, not a cosmetic reparameterization. It also supplies a genuinely coupled natural model with a sharp native residual certificate.

However, the underlying mechanism is Gaussian conditional tensorization via a coupling and a Pythagorean identity. Priority remains open in this audit. It must be compared with Marton's corrected conditional-transport work, Gaussian Gibbs spectral-gap theory and newer weighted tensorization results before claiming it is new. The source report should replace its theorem with this strengthened version only after the orchestrator accepts the proof.

The proposed contraction factor \(\sqrt{1-\zeta}\) remains a valid bound. This audit does not assert its sharpness in every local topology from the EB sharpness argument; these are different statements.

## 6. Dependent Dobrushin theorem: valid, but directly existing

The report's stationary coupled-chain proof works, even if \(\mu\) has zero atoms. Its own Gibbs conditional kernel leaves \(\mu\) invariant, and a finite-state Cesàro coupling yields

\[
d\le r+Cd,
\qquad
E_w^2\le w^T(I-C)^{-1}r.
\]

The positive sampling factors cancel in the stationary coordinate identities. The weighted bound follows immediately. For contraction, \(B=I-D_p+D_pC\) is nonnegative and has spectral radius below one; the proposed explicit left weight is valid.

Minor statement repair: explicitly retain \(\sum_i p_i\le1\), with the remaining mass assigned to identity, or \(\sum_i p_i=1\). “Sampling probabilities” suggests this but a standalone theorem should say it.

The full EB is a direct special case of the classical Dobrushin comparison theorem, not merely a new theorem inspired by that mechanism. With weighted Hamming cost, Kantorovich duality identifies its optimal transport value with the appropriate Lipschitz test-function supremum; the classical local-oscillation bound gives exactly the displayed matrix expression. This import is useful, but it cannot carry a first-of-its-kind claim.

## 9. Editorial-risk assessment and minimum upgrade

### Contribution lens

The positive statements are correct and useful as explicit certificates. Their current breadth should not be mistaken for novelty: different sections repeatedly instantiate established mechanisms. No compelling exclusive nonlinear-RL application is obtained. The exact negative result about the source discrepancy may supply the article's conceptual center; the positive repair must then be positioned as a principled replacement, not as an invented OT/Gibbs theory.

### Methods lens

There is no fatal theorem error in the audited positive package. The most important mathematical improvement is Proposition A. The main domain risks are changing the metric, changing the residual, omitting finite moments for joint Wasserstein, and using a local-specification residual as if it were a matched RL defect. The source already warns against the last error and should keep that warning.

### Communication lens

The eventual paper needs three separate labels throughout: ordinary joint Wasserstein; conserved-marginal conditional Wasserstein; coordinate-conditionals holding all untouched coordinates fixed. Calling all three “the Wasserstein residual” would conceal the main mathematical issue.
