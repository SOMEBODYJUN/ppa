# Independent audit: stochastic mass dilution, moment matching, and exact scalar lifting

Date: 2026-09-09. Auditor task: `stochastic_mass_dilution_audit`.

## Executive decision

**The displayed mathematical core in Sections 4–5 of `STOCHASTIC_RL_THEORY_ATTACK.md` survives independent audit, subject to the scope clarifications below. The proposed probability-space flagship does not yet pass the novelty/application gate.**

The precise distinction is:

- The dilution obstruction is correct and extends beyond a Dirac target to any invariant family supported on a common closed set, provided the selected residual vanishes on the reference invariant law.
- The correct moment lift is exact, not a loose sufficient estimate. The required moment order can be characterized sharply.
- The matched four-variable coupling theorem is correct. Its principal unproved input is the **existence of a native compatible coupling**, not its finite-length proof.
- A complete scalar lifting classification is available through the least concave majorant. At the critical RL–EB exponents, composing before integration can give a contraction whereas separately taking worst-case RMS moduli loses a factor tending to infinity.
- These results are useful theorem modules. Their analytical mechanism is close to classical superposition-operator/moment theory and to the pointwise-to-mean regularity machinery in Pischke–Powell (2026). They are **not, by themselves, a demonstrated SIOPT/Mathematical Programming-level flagship contribution**.

`PROJECT_PROOF` below means a complete argument reproduced here, not a published theorem or a priority claim. No theorem in another agent's file has been silently edited.

### 1.1 Explicit reconstruction of the audited proof

Work in a fixed Euclidean, or Hilbert, ambient vector space. Let `(M,D)` be a complete law space with the indicated Wasserstein or fixed-marginal conditional Wasserstein distance. Require `I` to be closed and nonempty and require every coupling in `Q(μ)` to have its second marginal in `I` and to satisfy any conditional-metric fiber constraint.

For a joint realization as in the submitted theorem, put

\[
h=X-Y,\quad a=X^+-Y,\quad b=X-X^+,\quad d=\|h\|,\quad s=\|b\|.
\]

Since `a+b=h` and `a-b=2X⁺−X−Y`,

\[
s\le \frac{d+\omega(d)}2=B(d),\qquad
\|a\|^2+s^2\le\frac{d^2+\omega(d)^2}{2}.
\]

The actual marginals of `(X,X⁺)` give `D(μ,μ⁺)≤‖s‖₂`, even if that pair is not the physical Markov pair. The invariant marginal of `Y` gives

\[
D(\mu^+,I)^2+\|s\|_2^2
\le\tfrac12\{E(\mu)^2+\|\omega(d)\|_2^2\}.
\]

The *different* output reference `Z` gives

\[
E(\mu^+)\le\|X^+-Z\|_2
\le\|\psi(s/\lambda)\|_2
\le\|\psi(B(d)/\lambda)\|_2.
\]

If the last pointwise composition is at most `κd`, then `E(μ⁺)≤κE(μ)`. No interchange of a nonlinear gauge and expectation is used.

For the continuation proof, assume iterates through index `k` have been constructed. The contraction gives `E(μ_k)≤κ^kE(μ_0)≤r`, while the partial length is bounded by the corresponding partial sum in `H(E(μ_0))`. The strict margin in (5.5) therefore keeps `μ_k` in the coverage domain. A successor exists; the same estimates apply. Induction constructs all iterates. The finite tail sums make the law sequence Cauchy, and completeness supplies its limit. Since `D(μ_k,I)≤E(μ_k)→0` and `I` is closed, that limit belongs to `I`.

No lower semicontinuity of `E` is needed for this last step. Attainment and measurable realization of `Q`, however, remain assumptions, not conclusions.

### 1.2 Two important non-implications

1. A physical graph-level RL estimate does not automatically survive an arbitrary recoupling of the law update. The recoupled pair must be verified anew or generated by a native coupling theorem.
2. The existence of this law coupling does not imply an almost-sure convergent realization of the **original physical chain**. A stationary particle can keep moving while its law is constant.

The theorem is consequently a valid conditional interface, not yet a black-box stochastic RL verifier.

## 2. Strengthened dilution theorem for nonisolated targets and actual Markov outputs

### Lemma 2.1 — distance to the full family of laws supported on a set

`PROJECT_PROOF.` Let `S⊂Rⁿ` be nonempty and closed, `1≤p<∞`, and write

\[
\mathcal P_p(S)=\{\nu\in\mathcal P_p(\mathbb R^n):\nu(S)=1\}.
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{d_{W_p}(\mu,\mathcal P_p(S))
=\left(\int d(x,S)^p\,\mu(dx)\right)^{1/p}.}
\tag{2.1}
\]

Proof. Every transport from `μ` to a law on `S` has cost at least the displayed integral. A Borel nearest-point selection `p_S(x)` exists in finite dimensions. Its pushforward is in `P_p(S)`: fixing `s₀∈S`, one has `‖p_S(x)‖≤2‖x‖+‖s₀‖`. The deterministic coupling `(x,p_S(x))` attains the lower bound. ∎

### Theorem 2.2 — local full-law moment-order obstruction

`PROJECT_PROOF.` Let `I⊂P_p(S)` be a family of laws supported on the same closed set, let `π∈I`, and let `P` be a Markov kernel with `πP=π`. Let `r≥1` and let `c≥0` be measurable, with

\[
c=0\quad\pi\text{-a.e.},\qquad 0<c(a)<\infty
\]

at some state `a` such that

\[
e(a):=\left(\int d(y,S)^pP(a,dy)\right)^{1/p}>0.
\]

Assume the required moments of `π`, `δ_a`, and `P(a,·)` are finite. Define

\[
\mathcal R_r(\mu)=\left(\int c(x)^r\,\mu(dx)\right)^{1/r}.
\]

If a finite `K` and exponent `q>0` satisfy

\[
d_{W_p}(\mu P,I)\le K\mathcal R_r(\mu)^q
\tag{2.2}
\]

on a full `W_p` neighborhood of `π`, then necessarily

\[
\boxed{q\le r/p.}
\tag{2.3}
\]

Proof. For `μ_ε=(1−ε)π+εδ_a`, coupling the unchanged portion diagonally gives

\[
W_p(\mu_\epsilon,\pi)^p
\le\epsilon\int\|a-x\|^p\pi(dx)\to0.
\]

Linearity of the Markov action gives `μ_εP=(1−ε)π+εP(a,·)`. Lemma 2.1 and `I⊂P_p(S)` imply

\[
d_{W_p}(\mu_\epsilon P,I)\ge\epsilon^{1/p}e(a),
\qquad \mathcal R_r(\mu_\epsilon)=\epsilon^{1/r}c(a).
\]

If `q>r/p`, (2.2) forces `e(a)≤Kc(a)^q ε^{q/r−1/p}→0`, a contradiction. ∎

For the ordinary RMS case `p=r=2`, every `q>1` is ruled out. The reference `π` need not be a Dirac law, and `S` need not be isolated, convex, or finite. An upper bound `R_r(μ_ε)≤Cε^{1/r}` is enough for the same conclusion.

**Scope boundary.** This is not a theorem about every ergodic Markov chain: if all invariant laws have full state support, `S` may be the whole space and the separating normal-distance witness vanishes. If the physical residual is nonzero at stationarity, the premise `c=0` is false. Neither case may be omitted in a claimed application.

## 3. Exact moment matching: a necessary-and-sufficient scalar theorem

### Theorem 3.1 — universal `Lʳ` residual classification

`PROJECT_PROOF.` On a measurable state set `A`, let `e,c:A→[0,∞)` be finite measurable functions. Suppose there is `s∈A` with `e(s)=c(s)=0` and there is at least one `a∈A` with `e(a)>0` and `0<c(a)<∞`. Fix `p,r≥1`, `q>0`, and `K<∞`.

The following are equivalent:

1. For every finitely supported probability law `μ` on `A`,
   \[
   \|e\|_{L^p(\mu)}\le K\|c\|_{L^r(\mu)}^q.
   \tag{3.1}
   \]
2. Both
   \[
   \boxed{pq\le r\quad\text{and}\quad e(x)\le Kc(x)^q\quad(x\in A)}
   \tag{3.2}
   \]
   hold.

Under (3.2), (3.1) also holds for arbitrary probability laws for which its right side is finite.

Proof. Dirac laws give the pointwise bound. The two-point laws `(1−ε)δ_s+εδ_a` give `q≤r/p`. Conversely, the pointwise bound and monotonicity of probability-space moments give

\[
\|e\|_p\le K\|c^q\|_p
=K\|c\|_{pq}^{q}
\le K\|c\|_r^{q}.
\]

This argument remains valid for `pq<1`: the last comparison is an inequality of positive moments, and does not require an `L^{pq}` normed-space structure. ∎

In particular, the critical high-order error bound `q>1` requires at least residual moment order `r=pq`. For the RL setting with `p=2`, the exact matched order is `2q`.

For a Markov kernel and target `P_p(S)`, take

\[
e(x)=\left(\int d(y,S)^pP(x,dy)\right)^{1/p}.
\]

Then Lemma 2.1 identifies the left side of (3.1) with the actual output law distance. For a *smaller* invariant family `I`, this identification is generally false. The missing correlation/reference coupling has not disappeared.

### Critical local-to-global consequence

At `pq=r`, a bound of the form (3.1) on an arbitrarily small full `W_p` neighborhood of `δ_s` already forces the pointwise inequality in (3.2) at **every finite excursion amplitude in that neighborhood's permitted state domain**. The mixture scaling cancels exactly. Thus a probability-local theorem is not automatically a state-local theorem.

## 4. Exact scalar commutation classification

Fix `1≤p<∞`, `R>0`, and a continuous nondecreasing gauge `f:[0,R]→[0,∞)` with `f(0)=0`. Define its universal moment lift by

\[
\mathfrak M_p[f](t)
:=\sup\{\|f(D)\|_p:0\le D\le R,\ \|D\|_p\le t\},
\quad 0\le t\le R.
\tag{4.1}
\]

The supremum ranges over all probability laws of `D`, so in particular over all two-point laws.

### Theorem 4.1 — exact concavification formula

`PROJECT_PROOF.` Let

\[
g(z)=f(z^{1/p})^p,\qquad 0\le z\le R^p,
\]

and let `cav g` denote the least concave majorant of `g` on this interval. Then

\[
\boxed{\mathfrak M_p[f](t)^p=(\operatorname{cav}g)(t^p).}
\tag{4.2}
\]

For each `t`, an optimizing law exists and can be chosen with at most two atoms.

Proof. Put `Z=D^p`. For a prescribed mean `m`, let `H(m)` be the maximum of `E[g(Z)]` over probability laws on `[0,R^p]` with mean `m`. Compactness of the probability-law constraint and continuity of `g` give attainment. Mixtures of feasible laws show `H` is concave, and Dirac laws show `H≥g`. Every concave majorant `h≥g` satisfies `E[g(Z)]≤E[h(Z)]≤h(m)` by Jensen, hence `H≤h`; therefore `H=cav g`.

It is nondecreasing: for `m₁<m₂`, transform any admissible `Z` of mean `m₁` into `Z'=Z+θ(R^p−Z)`, where `θ=(m₂−m₁)/(R^p−m₁)`. Then `Z'≥Z`, `E Z'=m₂`, and `E g(Z')≥E g(Z)`. Thus a mean inequality `E Z≤t^p` has the same value as the exact mean `t^p`.

Finally, the maximum of this continuous affine objective over the compact moment set has an extreme maximizer. Any feasible law with at least three support points is not extreme: take three disjoint positive-mass support neighborhoods and choose a nonzero piecewise constant signed density whose total mass and first moment are zero. A sufficiently small positive and negative perturbation preserves nonnegativity and both constraints. Hence extreme laws have at most two atoms. This proves the formula and the two-point assertion. ∎

The classification is scalar and exact; it does not certify that arbitrary optimizing distributions are realizable by the original operator's graph couplings. Actual realizability is a separate, potentially stronger constraint.

### Corollary 4.2 — local RMS contraction requires the whole support envelope

`PROJECT_PROOF.` For any `ε₀>0` and `κ≥0`,

\[
\|f(D)\|_p\le\kappa\|D\|_p
\quad\text{for every }0\le D\le R,\ \|D\|_p\le\epsilon_0
\tag{4.3}
\]

holds if and only if

\[
\boxed{f(u)\le\kappa u\quad\text{for every }u\in[0,R].}
\tag{4.4}
\]

Proof. Sufficiency follows by integration. For necessity, fix `u>0` and choose `D=u` with probability `ε` and `D=0` otherwise, with `ε^{1/p}u≤ε₀`. In (4.3), the factors `ε^{1/p}` cancel. ∎

Similarly, a superlinear bound `‖f(D)‖_p≤K‖D‖_p^q`, `q>1`, on such a full local moment ball is possible only when `f≡0` on the entire permitted support interval.

This pinpoints the main locality warning for Theorem 5.1: `E(μ)≤r` alone cannot justify a gauge known only for `d≤r` almost surely. One must impose support control, a shape class that excludes the spikes, or a globally valid gauge.

### Corollary 4.3 — exact power formulas and the loss from separate aggregation

`PROJECT_PROOF.` For `f(u)=Au^α`, `A>0`, `α>0`,

\[
\boxed{
\mathfrak M_p[f](t)=
\begin{cases}
At^α,&0<α\le1,\\
AR^{α-1}t,&α\ge1.
\end{cases}}
\tag{4.5}
\]

For `α≤1`, `g(z)=A^p z^α` is concave and the optimizer is `D=t`. For `α≥1`, its least concave majorant is the chord from zero to `R^p`; the optimizer is `D=R` with probability `(t/R)^p` and zero otherwise.

Now let

\[
B(d)=Cd^γ,\quad \psi(s)=Ks^q,
\qquad 0<γ<1<q,
\]

with `d∈[0,R]` and `s∈[0,CR^γ]`. Then

\[
\boxed{
\mathfrak M_p[\psi\circ B](t)=KC^q
\begin{cases}
t^{γq},&γq\le1,\\
R^{γq-1}t,&γq\ge1,
\end{cases}}
\tag{4.6}
\]

whereas separately taking the sharp moment envelope of each certificate gives

\[
\boxed{
\mathfrak M_p[\psi]\bigl(\mathfrak M_p[B](t)\bigr)
=KC^qR^{γ(q-1)}t^γ.}
\tag{4.7}
\]

At the critical exponent `γq=1`, (4.6) equals `KC^q t`, while the ratio of (4.7) to (4.6) is

\[
\boxed{(R/t)^{1-γ}\longrightarrow\infty.}
\tag{4.8}
\]

Thus the loss is not attributable to a bad estimate of either separate scalar envelope: both separate envelopes are already exact. They use different extremizing distributions and discard the dependence `s=B(d)`.

For a general composition, (4.2) gives a necessary-and-sufficient test for any proposed universal scalar law bound. In particular,

\[
\mathfrak M_p[\psi\circ B](t)
\le\mathfrak M_p[\psi]\bigl(\mathfrak M_p[B](t)\bigr),
\]

provided the domains match. Equality or strict loss can be checked from the two concave envelopes; there is no universal permission to interchange the operations.

### Consequence for genuine nonlinear RL

When the **same pointwise matched transfer** satisfies `d₊≤ψ(B(d)/λ)≤κd`, integration gives an `L²` contraction with no moment-shape assumption. That positive conclusion survives dilution because both sides scale in the same way. What dilution forbids is the intermediate assertion `‖d₊‖₂≤K‖s‖₂^q`, `q>1`, on a full mixture-rich law neighborhood.

For supercritical state orders `γq>1`, a bounded pointwise support radius gives a linear law factor proportional to `R^{γq−1}`. Uniform law-space superlinear order is lost under arbitrary dilution even though the state-level estimate is superlinear.

## 5. Exact relationship to the closest existing machinery

### 5.1 Pischke–Powell (2026): this is a substantive overlap, not a distant citation

In [Pischke–Powell, arXiv:2605.06129v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.06129v1), Definition 3.4 formalizes regularity in mean. Lemma 3.8 lifts a convex forward modulus by Jensen; Remark 3.9 uses its convex envelope. Definition 3.6, footnote 3, explicitly flags the difficulty of local state assumptions in stochastic settings. Definition 4.2 permits measurable solution anchors; Theorem 4.8 derives quantitative state convergence; Theorem 5.1 applies it to stochastic PPA with a normal convex integrand.

Our inverse-gauge concavification is the complementary scalar formulation. Its sharp spike necessity and RL-critical loss calculation are explicit refinements, not evidence that pointwise-to-mean regularity or the locality issue was previously unknown. Their displayed theorems concern convergence of actual random states to a zero set; they do not directly provide the nonphysical transport/reference matching needed for arbitrary non-Dirac invariant laws. This is a scope difference, not a proved strict extension.

### 5.2 Classical multivalued superposition theory

[Appell–De Pascale–Zabrejko, *Multivalued superposition operators*, 1991](https://www.numdam.org/article/RSMUP_1991__86__213_0.pdf) already studies measurable selections, boundedness and continuity between ideal spaces, including `Lᵖ→Lᑫ` realizations. Its Theorems 7 and 9 address the relation between graph closure/convexification and induced function-space operators. It is a direct warning against branding “pointwise multifunction information lifted to random variables” as a new general mechanism.

The moment-order theorem here is elementary probability-space `Lᵖ` comparison, and the scalar envelope is a one-moment extremal problem. The 1991 results do not, as checked, state our Wasserstein invariant-output obstruction or our exact RL-critical loss factor. That absence from one paper is not a global priority proof. The appropriate claim is a new **application/organization** of known machinery unless a more exact novelty audit supports more.

### 5.3 HLS and conditional transport

The source [HLS paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2206.05213v3) analyzes invariant-law convergence using expected almost-firm geometry and a transport discrepancy. The submitted report's `γ=1` common-noise identity is the balanced slice of that expected geometry. Its genuinely nonlinear matched coupling is not literally the same hypothesis, but the proposed positive example is too triangular to establish a major algorithmic extension.

[Conditional Wasserstein distances](https://jmlr.org/papers/v26/24-0586.html) are established transport objects, so choosing a fixed-marginal fiber and integrating conditional costs is not itself new. The new work, if achieved, would be a native verifiable construction of the required coupling for a substantive random multivalued proximal/splitting class.

## 6. What is proved, what should be repaired, and what should be killed

### Mathematical repairs before inclusion in a paper

1. State the common vector ambient space in the matched-coupling theorem; the reflection identities are not defined on an arbitrary metric state space.
2. State the pointwise support domain separately from the law/RMS neighborhood. Retain this distinction in every application and corollary.
3. Define precisely the reference-coupling family and its attainment/measurability. Do not infer compatibility from independently valid certificates.
4. Use an actual invariant-law target when claiming invariant-law convergence. The simpler target `P_p(S)` solves support convergence and may omit correlations essential to stationarity.
5. Keep physical sample-path convergence separate from recoupled law convergence.

### Publication-value judgment

| Package | Independent judgment |

| Dilution proposition alone | Useful short obstruction; too elementary and too close to standard moment scaling for the requested flagship bar. |
| Dilution + exact scalar commutation classification | A coherent technical module, with complete proofs and a clear message. Still mostly classical convexification/`Lᵖ` machinery. |
| Matched coupling theorem + shear–refresh model | Correct conditional theorem and hypothesis separation; native matching difficulty is assumed and the example is structurally easy. NOT_READY as flagship. |
| The above plus a natural non-product multivalued proximal/splitting class whose invariant-law convergence uses the new matching essentially | Plausible high-value route, but it is **not yet an achieved result**. |

There is currently no mathematical basis for promising a SIOPT or Mathematical Programming acceptance. More importantly, there is not yet a basis for calling this particular stochastic package the project's best flagship. The standard is a theorem that settles a documented obstruction or substantially enlarges a natural algorithm class, with a comparison that survives re-expression through existing stochastic regularity and coupling machinery.
