# Multi-step, pathwise, and finite-capture RL–generalized-subregularity theory

**Status:** mathematical development and bounded primary-source audit, 2026-09-09.  
**Scope:** finite-dimensional Euclidean dynamics, possibly set-valued transitions, nonisolated target sets, cyclic/periodic algorithms, and block reconstructions of PPA type.

## 1. Executive conclusion

There is a viable enlargement of the present one-step RL–generalized-subregularity theory.

The right object is not merely the iterate \(T^m\), and not the independent composition of worst one-step scalar bounds. It is an **admissible path atlas**: the certificate remembers which active branch or phase may follow which other branch, supplies prefix-distance and within-block displacement bounds, and requires contraction only at the end of a legal block. A one-step bound may be locally expansive, while every legal two- or \(m\)-step word is contractive or reaches the target exactly.

The whole-orbit theorem below is proved directly. A block-lifted RL plus a general output gauge \(\psi_m\) is one producer of its endpoint bound; a joint active-set calculation or finite-capture identity is another. This distinction is essential: replacing a legal path by the Cartesian product of its separate worst bounds loses the correlation that makes finite capture possible.

A previously derived genuinely set-valued cyclic M1 model verifies the new theory. Its one-step reflected map has optimal Hölder exponent \(1/3\), its actual one-step output error-bound exponent is at most \(1\), and hence the one-step scalar RL–EB product is \(1/3<1\). Nevertheless, every initial point in an explicit ball reaches the same nonisolated solution set in at most two steps. The map is not locally almost nonexpansive, averaged, or paracontractive at the endpoint. Thus the multi-step result is not a repackaging of those standard theories on the same neighborhood.

The abstract idea “study a composition or \(T^m\)” is not new. Existing work already combines quasi-\(\alpha\)-firmness of finite compositions with gauge metric subregularity, and Fejér-modulus theory already treats cyclic projections and finite termination. The defensible potential novelty is narrower:

> a nonlinear RL/path-atlas calculus for non-Lipschitz, possibly multivalued transitions, with branch-adjacency constraints and an explicit intermediate-path localization budget.

Global priority for that exact formulation is **PENDING_VERIFICATION**.

## 2. Three levels of multi-step information

Let \(X\) be a finite-dimensional Euclidean space, \(S\subset X\) nonempty and closed, and \(T:X\rightrightarrows X\). An admissible path of length \(m\) is

\[
\boldsymbol x=(x_0,x_1,\ldots,x_m),
\qquad x_{j+1}\in T_{\sigma_j}(x_j),
\]

where the labels \(\sigma_j\) record the phase, active face, or selected graph branch. The word \(\boldsymbol\sigma=(\sigma_0,\ldots,\sigma_{m-1})\) must belong to a prescribed language \(\mathcal W_m\). This language prevents impossible cross-branch combinations.

### 2.1 Block-lifted RL

Fix \(\Lambda>0\). Define the block-lifted operator

\[
\mathcal F_m(y)
:=
\left\{\frac{x-y}{\Lambda}:y\in T^m(x)\right\}.
\tag{2.1}
\]

Then, as relations,

\[
J_{\Lambda\mathcal F_m}=T^m.
\tag{2.2}
\]

Moreover, AP-RL for \(\mathcal F_m\) is exactly Hölder control of the reflected block map:

\[
\|2(y-y')-(x-x')\|
\le L_m\|x-x'\|^{\gamma_m},
\tag{2.3}
\]

for all relevant \(y\in T^m x\), \(y'\in T^m x'\). As in the one-step theory, (2.3) forces \(T^m\) to be single-valued on the declared input patch; it does not supply input coverage.

The zero set changes:

\[
\operatorname{zer}\mathcal F_m=\operatorname{Fix}T^m.
\tag{2.4}
\]

Therefore a block theorem about the original fixed points requires the **no-spurious-cycle condition**

\[
\operatorname{Fix}T^m\cap V
=
\operatorname{Fix}T\cap V
=S\cap V,
\tag{2.5}
\]

unless the intended target is an \(m\)-cycle rather than a fixed point.

### 2.2 Solution-comparison block RL plus general \(\psi_m\)

For every admissible block \(x=x_0\mapsto y=x_m\), suppose some \(p\in P_S(x)\) satisfies

\[
\|2y-x-p\|\le\omega_m(d(x,S)),
\tag{2.6}
\]

and the same block output satisfies

\[
d(y,S)
\le
\psi_m\!\left(\frac{\|x-y\|}{\Lambda}\right),
\tag{2.7}
\]

where \(\omega_m,\psi_m\) are nondecreasing and vanish at zero.

Ordinary generalized subregularity of the lifted operator,

\[
d(y,S)\le\psi_m\bigl(d(0,\mathcal F_m(y))\bigr),
\]

implies (2.7) on every selected block because
\(d(0,\mathcal F_m(y))\le\|x-y\|/\Lambda\). The transition form (2.7) may also be verified directly; it is then weaker than a full-neighborhood ordinary subregularity assertion.

Put

\[
B_m(t)=\frac{t+\omega_m(t)}2,
\qquad
\rho_m(t)=
\min\left\{
B_m(t),
\psi_m\!\left(\frac{B_m(t)}{\Lambda}\right)
\right\}.
\tag{2.8}
\]

The identities

\[
2(y-p)=(2y-x-p)+(x-p),
\qquad
2(x-y)=(x-p)-(2y-x-p)
\]

give

\[
d(y,S)\le B_m(d(x,S)),
\quad
\|x-y\|\le B_m(d(x,S)),
\quad
d(y,S)\le\rho_m(d(x,S)).
\tag{2.9}
\]

This is the exact \(m\)-block analogue of the current one-step scalar interface. It still does not control the intermediate states \(x_1,\ldots,x_{m-1}\).

### 2.3 Path atlas

A **pathwise RL–EB atlas of horizon \(m\)** consists of legal words \(\mathcal W_m\), nondecreasing prefix gauges \(D_{w,j}\), and displacement gauges \(G_{w,j}\) such that every path with word \(w\) and \(t=d(x_0,S)\) obeys

\[
d(x_j,S)\le D_{w,j}(t),
\qquad
\|x_{j+1}-x_j\|\le G_{w,j}(t),
\tag{2.10}
\]

for \(j=0,\ldots,m-1\), with \(D_{w,0}(t)=t\). Define

\[
\Theta(t):=\sup_{w\in\mathcal W_m}D_{w,m}(t),
\qquad
G(t):=
\sup_{w\in\mathcal W_m}
\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}G_{w,j}(t).
\tag{2.11}
\]

The prefix bounds may be produced edge by edge from mode-dependent RL and output EB. The terminal \(D_{w,m}\), however, may use a joint active-set identity, a block RL–\(\psi_m\) estimate, or a finite-capture calculation. This is the step that preserves path correlation.

If mode \(\sigma\) has the one-step solution comparison

\[
\|2x^+-x-p\|\le\omega_\sigma(d(x,S)),
\]

then a valid one-step displacement producer is

\[
G_{w,j}(t)
\le
\frac{
D_{w,j}(t)+
\omega_{\sigma_j}(D_{w,j}(t))
}{2}.
\tag{2.12}
\]

Thus RL continues to supply localization and path-length information even when terminal contraction comes from a genuinely multi-step correlation.

## 3. Main whole-orbit theorem

### Theorem 3.1 (pathwise \(m\)-block convergence and localization)

Let \(V\subset X\) be open and let \(S\subset X\) be nonempty and closed. Suppose:

1. **local path coverage:** every admissible state in \(V\) with distance below a radius \(\eta\) has a next transition, and every transition is represented in the path atlas;
2. **prefix closure:** for \(0\le t\le r\), all prefix gauges in (2.10) are below \(\eta\);
3. **block contraction:** for some \(\kappa\in[0,1)\),
   \[
   \Theta(t)\le\kappa t
   \qquad(0\le t\le r);
   \tag{3.1}
   \]
4. **summable path budget:**
   \[
   H(t):=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}G(\kappa^nt)<\infty
   \qquad(0\le t\le r).
   \tag{3.2}
   \]

If \(x_0\in V\), \(d_0=d(x_0,S)\le r\), and

\[
H(d_0)<\operatorname{dist}(x_0,X\setminus V),
\tag{3.3}
\]

then every admissible orbit governed by the atlas can be continued indefinitely, stays in \(V\), has finite length, and converges to a point \(x_\infty\in S\). More precisely,

\[
d(x_{nm},S)\le\kappa^n d_0,
\tag{3.4}
\]

\[
\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\|x_{k+1}-x_k\|
\le H(d_0).
\tag{3.5}
\]

#### Proof

Assume blocks through index \(nm\) have been constructed. By (2.10) and (3.1), induction gives (3.4). Prefix closure keeps every within-block state in the certificate tube. The length of block \(n\) is at most

\[
G(d(x_{nm},S))\le G(\kappa^n d_0).
\]

Hence every finite prefix remains at distance less than the corresponding partial sum in (3.2) from \(x_0\). Condition (3.3) keeps it in \(V\), and local coverage supplies the next transition. This proves indefinite continuation.

Summing the block bounds proves (3.5), so the full sequence, not only the block subsequence, is Cauchy. Finite dimensionality gives a limit. By (3.4) and closedness of \(S\), the limit lies in \(S\). \(\square\)

### Corollary 3.2 (block-lifted RL plus generalized subregularity)

Assume (2.6)–(2.7), let \(\Theta=\rho_m\) from (2.8), and separately verify an intermediate path budget \(G\). If \(\rho_m(t)\le\kappa t\) on a positive radius and (3.2)–(3.3) hold, Theorem 3.1 applies.

This is the direct \(m\)-step extension of the current RL–general-\(\psi\) theorem. No power domination of \(\psi_m\) is required.

### Corollary 3.3 (finite capture)

Suppose \(S\) is absorbing and every legal path of length \(m\) starting in the certified neighborhood satisfies

\[
x_m\in S.
\tag{3.6}
\]

Then \(\Theta\equiv0\). Every admissible orbit reaches \(S\) in at most \(m\) steps. Only the first-block localization budget is needed.

Finite capture by itself proves termination; RL is not logically necessary for that elementary fact. Its role is to produce uniform displacement bounds, control cross-branch consistency where required, and connect the class to the larger PPA geometry. This boundary should be stated explicitly in any novelty claim.

### Corollary 3.4 (phase-cycle convergence)

For a deterministic periodic family \(T_0,\ldots,T_{m-1}\), let

\[
C=T_{m-1}\circ\cdots\circ T_0.
\]

If Theorem 3.1 yields \(x_{nm}\to\bar x_0\in\operatorname{Fix}C\) and every prefix

\[
P_j=T_{j-1}\circ\cdots\circ T_0
\]

is continuous at \(\bar x_0\), then

\[
x_{nm+j}\to\bar x_j:=P_j\bar x_0.
\tag{3.7}
\]

Thus an inconsistent cyclic method may converge phasewise to an \(m\)-cycle. Full point convergence occurs only when \(\bar x_0=\cdots=\bar x_{m-1}\). This is why one must not silently replace \(\operatorname{Fix}T\) by \(\operatorname{Fix}T^m\).

## 4. Power bookkeeping on legal words

Suppose an edge-level producer gives

\[
d_{j+1}\le c_{\sigma_j}d_j^{\alpha_{\sigma_j}}.
\tag{4.1}
\]

For a legal word \(w=(\sigma_0,\ldots,\sigma_{m-1})\), composition yields

\[
d_m\le C_wd_0^{A_w},
\tag{4.2}
\]

where

\[
A_w=\prod_{j=0}^{m-1}\alpha_{\sigma_j},
\qquad
C_w=
\prod_{j=0}^{m-1}
c_{\sigma_j}^{\prod_{\ell=j+1}^{m-1}\alpha_{\sigma_\ell}}.
\tag{4.3}
\]

Consequently:

- if every legal word has \(A_w>1\), block contraction is automatic on a sufficiently small radius;
- if the smallest \(A_w\) equals \(1\), the corresponding \(C_w\) must be strictly below \(1\);
- if an admissible word has \(A_w<1\), the separated edge certificate cannot yield uniform block contraction, unless a sharper joint path identity overrides it;
- a finite-capture edge or terminal path has zero endpoint gauge and dominates the power bookkeeping.

In an EB-dominated genuine RL regime, \(\alpha_\sigma=\gamma_\sigma q_\sigma\). Formula (4.3) is the multi-step replacement for the one-step balance \(\gamma q\ge1\). More importantly, the finite-capture example below shows why even (4.3) may be pessimistic: legal active regimes contain correlations absent from separately composed scalar bounds.

## 5. Verified multivalued, nonisolated finite-capture class

Consider

\[
c=\frac23,
\qquad
f(r)=\operatorname{sign}(r)
\left[\frac32(|r|-c)_+\right]^{1/3},
\tag{5.1}
\]

\[
T(z_1,z_2)
=
\bigl(z_1-f(z_1-3z_2),0\bigr),
\qquad
Z=[-c,c]\times\{0\}.
\tag{5.2}
\]

This is the actual outer map obtained in the previously verified cyclic M1 construction with a \(\operatorname{Sign}\) graph. The generating generalized equation is genuinely set-valued, while the selected implicit inverse and \(T\) are single-valued. The solution set \(Z\) is a nondegenerate line segment, not an isolated point.

At the right endpoint \(z_*=(c,0)\), set

\[
R=\frac1{96\sqrt{10}}.
\tag{5.3}
\]

The active-set calculation gives

\[
T^2(B_R(z_*))\subset Z,
\qquad
T|_Z=I.
\tag{5.4}
\]

Hence every orbit starting in this ball reaches an individual point of \(Z\) in at most two steps. A uniform coarse length bound established in the same calculation is \(73/96\).

On the same full neighborhood, the actual one-step reflection has optimal Hölder exponent

\[
\gamma_{\max}=\frac13,
\tag{5.5}
\]

whereas the best actual-output error-bound exponent is

\[
q_{\mathrm{EB},\max}=1.
\tag{5.6}
\]

Thus

\[
\gamma_{\max}q_{\mathrm{EB},\max}=\frac13<1,
\tag{5.7}
\]

and every one-step independent scalar RL–EB certificate of the present power type fails. The path atlas succeeds because its legal two-step terminal gauge is exactly zero.

### Separation from almost-averaged and paracontractive theories

For \(t>0\), let \(z_t=(c+t,0)\). Then

\[
Tz_t-z_*
=
\left(t-\left(\frac32t\right)^{1/3},0\right).
\]

Therefore

\[
\frac{\|Tz_t-z_*\|}{\|z_t-z_*\|}
\longrightarrow\infty.
\tag{5.8}
\]

So \(T\) is not calm or pointwise almost nonexpansive at \(z_*\) with any finite violation, hence is not locally averaged there. It is also not paracontractive relative to all fixed points: the distance to the fixed point \(z_*\) increases along this sequence. Nevertheless, (5.4) gives two-step capture.

For all sufficiently small \(t>0\), the displayed \(Tz_t\) already lies in \(Z\), hence \(T^2z_t=Tz_t\). Thus the same divergent quotient excludes a finite almost-nonexpansive or almost-\(\alpha\)-firm certificate for the block map \(T^2\), not merely for the individual step.

Because \(T^2z\in Z\) on the ball,

\[
d(z,Z)\le\|z-T^2z\|,
\tag{5.9}
\]

so \(I-T^2\) is metrically subregular there with constant \(1\), and
\(\operatorname{Fix}T^2\cap B_R(z_*)=Z\cap B_R(z_*)\). Metric subregularity alone is not the missing ingredient: existing composition theorems also require firm/almost-firm geometry, which fails by (5.8). The nonlinear pathwise certificate replaces that geometry.

**Evidence status for Section 5:** formulas, capture radius, exponent obstruction, and two-step proof are VERIFIED_USER_MATERIAL; (5.8)–(5.9) are AI_INFERENCE by direct calculation from those verified formulas.

### Two indispensable obstructions

1. **Spurious cycles.** For \(T(x)=1-x\), \(T^2=I\), so every point is a block fixed point although \(\operatorname{Fix}T=\{1/2\}\). A theorem about \(T^m\) must impose (2.5) or explicitly target phase cycles.
2. **Block endpoints do not localize intermediate steps.** Even when an endpoint map is contractive, a local proof must bound every internal displacement. The summable \(G\)-budget in Theorem 3.1 is not cosmetic.

## 7. Public problems that this upgrade can attack

### P1. Weak almost-averaged/set-valued selection theory

Luke–Thao–Tam explicitly leave open a theory based on ordinary calmness rather than their all-values strong calmness, which would yield existence of convergent selections for iterated set-valued mappings. [Primary source](https://arxiv.org/html/1605.05725v2)

**Fit:** high. A path atlas can quantify either every legal selection or an existentially chosen selection without demanding all-pairs Lipschitz control. A genuine advance requires a selection/coverage theorem from native graph data; Theorem 3.1 alone is only the trajectory backend.

**2026 warning:** Durea–Tron–Théra now give an adjacent directional-orbital route that constructs a convergent selection under minimal-time regularity. The broad claim “first convergent-selection theory below strong almost-averagedness” is not defensible. The sharper remaining target is a prescribed PPA/splitting selection with RL energy, ordinary set-distance general-\(\psi\) data, and algorithm-specific coverage.

### P2. Compositions without common fixed points

Bërdëllima–Lauster–Luke ask whether pointwise \(\alpha\)-firm regularity is preserved under compositions or convex compositions when the maps do not share fixed points, with direct relevance to inconsistent cyclic projections. [Exact open-problem passage](https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/10.1007/s11784-021-00919-4.pdf)

**Fit:** medium-high as an alternative route. The pathwise theory does not prove preservation of \(\alpha\)-firmness; it bypasses that requirement by analyzing legal cycles and phasewise limits. To claim resolution, one must construct native projector/reflector path certificates for a nontrivial inconsistent class.

### P3. Verifiable gauge metric subregularity

The same paper explicitly asks for characterizations of gauge metric subregularity that are easier to verify, especially structural analogues of polyhedrality and semialgebraicity. [Exact open-problem passage](https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/10.1007/s11784-021-00919-4.pdf)

**Fit:** high for the project as a whole, but not solved by the trajectory theorem. The promising contribution is a finite active-atlas verifier that outputs legal-word endpoint gauges and constants rather than one global residual modulus.

### P4. Cyclic projections: regularity and geometry are entangled

Luke–Thao–Tam state that elemental regularity and subtransversality become entangled, and that it is unclear whether they can be separated in necessary convergence conditions for cyclic projections. They also explain that different nonconvex cycles can have different lengths and orientations, so \(P_0-I\) can be uninformative. [Primary discussion](https://arxiv.org/html/1605.05725v2)

**Fit:** very high conceptually. A path atlas retains exactly the cycle/branch information destroyed by independent scalar aggregation. A publishable attack should target a finite union of prox-regular or semialgebraic sets with multiple admissible cycles and compute the full word-dependent certificate.

### P5. RAAR phase retrieval metric subregularity

Luke–Thao–Tam ask whether the RAAR fixed-point operator is “usually,” in an appropriate sense, metrically subregular for phase retrieval. [Exact statement](https://arxiv.org/html/1605.05725v2)

**Fit:** medium. Multi-step/pathwise regularity could certify local convergence when a single global residual bound is too coarse, but it does not answer generic metric subregularity. A data-model-specific active-stratum proof is still required.
