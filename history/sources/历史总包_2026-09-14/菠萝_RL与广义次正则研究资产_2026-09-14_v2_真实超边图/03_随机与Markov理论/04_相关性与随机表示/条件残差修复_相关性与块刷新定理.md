# Correlation-aware conditional residuals for random projections and block refresh

Date: 2026-09-09. Task: `markov_conditional_repair_attack`.

## Executive verdict

**PROJECT_PROOF: a positive repair is available, with sharp constants and exact quantifiers for a natural random-coordinate-projection class.** The repair borrows the established conditional/fibered Wasserstein metric. It is not a new optimal-transport distance and is not a theorem about the old synchronous discrepancy unchanged.

The completed results are:

1. A conditional local-specification residual detects the entire joint invariant law, including correlations missed by marginal or unrestricted synchronous residuals.
2. For independent coordinate refresh, the native minimum refresh probability gives the **optimal linear error-bound constant** and **optimal one-step contraction factor**. Uniform linear subregularity, uniform one-step contraction, uniform finite-block contraction and uniform relative geometric convergence are equivalent, with their quantifiers frozen below.
3. When refresh probabilities approach zero, an exact one-dimensional variational formula computes the **smallest generalized error-bound gauge** from the native refresh probabilities and the conserved marginal. It also gives exact convergence and finite-length formulas. Thus general gauges are genuinely useful here; no power envelope is imposed.
4. A dependent Gibbs/conditional-reset extension supplies an explicit verifier from a Dobrushin influence matrix. This is deliberately identified as an import of mature coupling/comparison machinery.
5. A genuinely coupled Gaussian Gibbs class consists of random orthogonal projections in a precision-matrix metric. A computable matrix gives invariant identification, a conditional error bound and geometric convergence.

**Boundary:** these results repair the certificate in a stronger, conserved-variable conditional metric. They do not establish necessity of the source discrepancy in ordinary joint Wasserstein space. The elementary product and Dobrushin mechanisms are not claimed as new. The potentially publishable package is the source-discrepancy obstruction plus the exact native conditional repair and nonuniform-gauge classification; global priority and sufficient flagship novelty are not established.

## 1. Sources, scope and choice of existing tool

The relevant existing tool is conditional/fibered Wasserstein, not a new adapted path-space distance. The missing variable in the parallel-projection example is a **conserved state variable**, not temporal nonanticipativity.

For a fixed probability law \(\nu\) on a standard Borel parameter space \(U\), let
\[
\mathscr M_\nu=\{\mu(du,dx)=\nu(du)\mu_u(dx)\}.
\]
On laws with the same first marginal, use the established distance
\[
\mathsf W_\nu(\mu,\eta)^2
=\int W_2(\mu_u,\eta_u)^2\,\nu(du).
\tag{1.1}
\]
Equivalently, minimize transport cost only over couplings that keep \(u=u'\). Measurable optimal couplings and this disintegration identity are available in Chemseddine–Hagemann–Steidl–Wald, Proposition 1 and Corollary 2, [JMLR 26(141), 2025](https://jmlr.org/papers/v26/24-0586.html), [full text, Section 3](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.18705v3). Those authors explicitly relate the construction to earlier Wasserstein tangent-space and fibered-transport literature. We claim no novelty for (1.1).

The source comparison is HLS, [*Rates of Convergence for Chains of Expansive Markov Operators*, Theorem 3.7(iii), equation (34)](https://arxiv.org/html/2206.05213v3). Its extra assumption is \(\Psi(\mu)\ge qW_2(\mu,\mu P)\). The purpose here is to **produce a valid residual and constants from update data**, not simply to re-assume that domination. The source already separates zero identification from regularity.

All proofs below are finite-dimensional in each state fiber. Sections 2–5 use finite binary fibers, avoiding measurable-selection complications. The Gaussian extension uses finite-dimensional Euclidean fibers and finite second moments. Fixed \(\nu\) permits a nonisolated family of invariant laws when \(\nu\) varies; it is not a claim of a globally unique invariant law.

## 2. Native random coordinate projections and a computable residual

Let \(X=\{0,1\}^m\subset\mathbb R^m\), with squared Euclidean cost
\(\|x-y\|^2=\sum_i1_{x_i\ne y_i}\). Given \(u\), let
\[
\beta_u=\bigotimes_{i=1}^m\operatorname{Bern}(b_i(u)),
\qquad 0<b_i(u)<1.
\]
Let \(p_i(u)>0\), \(\sum_i p_i(u)\le1\). At each step choose coordinate \(i\) with probability \(p_i(u)\), replace it by an independent \(\operatorname{Bern}(b_i(u))\) draw, and otherwise do nothing. The conservative coordinate \(u\) is unchanged. For fixed \(u\), each selected deterministic state map is an ordinary orthogonal projection onto \(\{x_i=j\}\); it is firmly nonexpansive. Constant \(p_i,b_i\) gives an ordinary finite-i.i.d. random projection family on the whole Euclidean product with a passive conserved variable.

Write \(Q_i\) for coordinate refresh and \(P\) for the resulting Markov kernel. The invariant law in \(\mathscr M_\nu\) is
\[
\pi_\nu(du,dx)=\nu(du)\beta_u(dx).
\tag{2.1}
\]
Define \(E(\mu)=\mathsf W_\nu(\mu,\pi_\nu)\). For each coordinate, define
\[
d_i(u;\mu)^2
=\sum_{x_{-i}}\mu_u(x_{-i})
\left|\mu_u(X_i=1\mid x_{-i})-b_i(u)\right|.
\tag{2.2}
\]
The summand is exactly the squared Wasserstein distance between the two Bernoulli laws. Conditional probabilities on zero-mass conditioning events can be defined arbitrarily.

Our **residual**, not a new distance, is
\[
\boxed{\mathcal R(\mu)^2
=\int\sum_i p_i(u)d_i(u;\mu)^2\,\nu(du).}
\tag{2.3}
\]
It compares each current conditional coordinate law with the native refresh law while preserving **all** untouched variables \((u,x_{-i})\). It requires no search over unknown invariant laws. On a finite fiber it is a finite sum of conditional probability differences.

### Lemma 2.1: conditional tensorization

For every probability law \(\alpha\) on \(X\) and every product Bernoulli law \(\beta\),
\[
W_2(\alpha,\beta)^2\le\sum_i
\mathbb E_\alpha
\left|\alpha(X_i=1\mid X_{-i})-b_i\right|.
\tag{2.4}
\]

Proof. Construct a coupling sequentially. Given the past pairs \((X_{<i},Y_{<i})\), optimally couple \(\alpha_i(\cdot\mid X_{<i})\) and \(\operatorname{Bern}(b_i)\). The first marginal has law \(\alpha\); the second has the product law \(\beta\), because its conditional law at step \(i\) is the fixed Bernoulli law independent of the preceding pairs. The expected cost is
\(\sum_i\mathbb E|\alpha(X_i=1\mid X_{<i})-b_i|\).
The tower property and convexity of absolute value bound each term by its counterpart conditioned on all other coordinates. This proves (2.4). This is ordinary product coupling/tensorization, not a claimed new principle. \(\square\)

### Theorem 2.2: sharp native error bound and convergence equivalence

Fix \(\nu\), assume \(p_i(u)>0\) for \(\nu\)-almost every \(u\), all \(i\), and put
\[
a_*:=\operatorname*{ess\,inf}_{u\sim\nu}\min_i p_i(u).
\]
Then:

**(a) Exact identification.** For every \(\mu\in\mathscr M_\nu\),
\[
\mathcal R(\mu)=0\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\mu=\pi_\nu\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\mu P=\mu.
\tag{2.5}
\]

**(b) If \(a_*>0\), the optimal global error-bound constant is**
\[
\boxed{E(\mu)\le a_*^{-1/2}\mathcal R(\mu).}
\tag{2.6}
\]
The optimal global one-step distance contraction factor is
\[
\boxed{E(\mu P)\le\sqrt{1-a_*}\,E(\mu).}
\tag{2.7}
\]
For each integer \(k\ge1\), the optimal factor is \((1-a_*)^{k/2}\).

**(c) The following are equivalent, with one constant for all \(\mu\in\mathscr M_\nu\):**

1. \(a_*>0\).
2. There exists a finite \(K\) such that \(E\le K\mathcal R\).
3. There exists \(c<1\) such that \(E(\mu P)\le cE(\mu)\).
4. There exist a fixed integer \(N\ge1\) and \(c_N<1\) such that \(E(\mu P^N)\le c_NE(\mu)\).
5. There exist \(C<\infty\), \(c<1\) such that \(E(\mu P^k)\le Cc^kE(\mu)\) for every \(\mu\) and \(k\).

The same equivalence holds for these estimates on any full \(\mathsf W_\nu\)-ball about \(\pi_\nu\): the witnesses below can be scaled to have arbitrarily small error.

Proof. Applying (2.4) fiberwise gives
\(E^2\le\int\sum_i d_i^2\le\mathcal R^2/a_*\).
For convergence, optimally couple \(\mu_u\) with \(\beta_u\), then use the same selected coordinate and the same fresh bit on both sides. A difference in coordinate \(i\) survives with probability \(1-p_i(u)\), so the output coupling cost is at most \((1-a_*)\) times its input cost. Iteration proves the displayed upper factors.

For sharpness and all converses, choose an index \(i\) and a positive-measure set \(A\) where \(p_i<a_*+\epsilon\). Such an index exists because \(m\) is finite. On \(A\), perturb only the \(i\)-th Bernoulli factor of \(\beta_u\), toward whichever endpoint is at least \(1/2\) away, by a common mixing amount \(s\in(0,1]\); elsewhere retain \(\beta_u\). Write the resulting coordinate probability difference as \(h(u)>0\). All other conditional discrepancies vanish, and exactly
\[
E^2=\int_Ah\,d\nu,\quad
\mathcal R^2=\int_Ap_i h\,d\nu,\quad
E(\mu P^k)^2=\int_A(1-p_i)^kh\,d\nu.
\tag{2.8}
\]
The kernel preserves the product form with only this one coordinate perturbed. Let \(\epsilon\downarrow0\) to prove optimality. If \(a_*=0\), these ratios exclude every finite linear EB constant and every fixed-block strict relative contraction. A purported estimate in item 5 would give a strict finite-block factor by choosing \(k\) with \(Cc^k<1\), a contradiction. Letting \(s\downarrow0\) makes all witnesses arbitrarily close in the conditional metric without changing any ratio.

Identification from \(\mathcal R=0\) follows from (2.4) without uniform positivity: each strictly positive weighted discrepancy is zero almost everywhere. To identify every invariant law, for each fixed \(u\) the coupling calculation gives contraction with factor \(\sqrt{1-\min_i p_i(u)}<1\), so its only invariant law is \(\beta_u\). Invariance disintegrates because \(u\) is preserved. This proves (2.5). \(\square\)

### Consequences and exact limits

With \(a_*>0\), the actual laws converge to \(\pi_\nu\), and their total conditional Wasserstein length is bounded by
\[
\sum_{k\ge0}\mathsf W_\nu(\mu P^{k+1},\mu P^k)
\le\frac{1+\sqrt{1-a_*}}{1-\sqrt{1-a_*}}E(\mu).
\tag{2.9}
\]
This follows by the triangle inequality through \(\pi_\nu\). Invariant laws are nonunique globally, since their conserved marginal is arbitrary.

The sharp relationship is \(c_*^2=1-K_*^{-2}\). It is not the generic HLS triangle bound. Both constants are produced directly by the sampling probabilities, and (2.8) proves necessity.

For general continuous coordinate laws, the same synchronous coupling yields (2.7), and conditional tensorization with general \(W_2^2\) gives a sufficient version of (2.6). The exact sharpness proof (2.8) uses binary transport and is not asserted without further work for every continuous target.

## 3. General gauges: exact answer for nonuniform binary refresh

Consider one binary coordinate, with measurable \(0<a(u)\le1\), target \(\operatorname{Bern}(b(u))\), and update
\[
r(u)\longmapsto r_+(u)=(1-a(u))r(u)+a(u)b(u).
\]
This includes rare updates depending on the frozen environment. Put
\[
h(u)=|r(u)-b(u)|,\qquad M(u)=\max\{b(u),1-b(u)\}.
\]
Every measurable \(h\) with \(0\le h\le M\) is realized by a measurable choice of \(r\): move from \(b\) toward the farther endpoint by \(h\). Then exactly
\[
\boxed{
E^2=\int h\,d\nu,\quad
\mathcal R^2=\int ah\,d\nu,\quad
E_k^2=\int(1-a)^kh\,d\nu.}
\tag{3.1}
\]
Moreover the **actual conditional law displacement** satisfies
\[
\mathsf W_\nu(\mu P,\mu)^2=\mathcal R(\mu)^2,
\quad E_k^2-E_{k+1}^2=\mathcal R(\mu P^k)^2.
\tag{3.2}
\]
These are identities, not upper bounds.

### Theorem 3.1: the smallest gauge from native data

Define
\[
\boxed{\phi(t)^2=
\sup\left\{\int h\,d\nu:0\le h\le M,
\int ah\,d\nu\le t^2\right\}.}
\tag{3.3}
\]
Then \(E(\mu)\le\phi(\mathcal R(\mu))\) for all \(\mu\), and \(\phi\) is the smallest nondecreasing uniform gauge with that property. It is explicitly computable by filling the fibers with the smallest refresh probability first. Equivalently,
\[
\boxed{\phi(t)^2=
\inf_{\lambda\ge0}\left\{\lambda t^2+
\int M(u)(1-\lambda a(u))_+\,\nu(du)\right\}.}
\tag{3.4}
\]
It satisfies \(\phi(0)=0\), \(\phi(t)\to0\) as \(t\downarrow0\), because \(a>0\) almost everywhere. If \(\operatorname{ess\,inf}a=0\), there is no uniform linear gauge but there is still this exact general gauge.

Proof. Formula (3.1) and the realizability of every \(h\) prove (3.3) and minimality. For (3.4), introduce the finite measure \(d\zeta=M\,d\nu\) and write \(h=Mf\), \(0\le f\le1\). This is the continuous fractional-knapsack problem: maximize \(\int f\,d\zeta\) subject to \(\int af\,d\zeta\le t^2\). Its optimizers take \(f=1\) below a threshold in \(a\), \(f=0\) above it, and possibly a fractional value on the threshold level. Such a threshold is obtained from the right-continuous cumulative distribution of \(a\) under \(\zeta\). The upper bound in (3.4) follows for every \(\lambda\) from
\(f\le\lambda af+(1-\lambda a)_+\).
The threshold choice, with \(\lambda\) equal to its reciprocal, attains equality; saturation of the whole domain is obtained with \(\lambda=0\). Approximation covers zero or infinite endpoint thresholds. Finally, for every \(\epsilon>0\),
\[
\phi(t)^2\le t^2/\epsilon+
\int_{\{a\le\epsilon\}}M\,d\nu.
\tag{3.5}
\]
First send \(t\) to zero and then \(\epsilon\) to zero. \(\square\)

### Theorem 3.2: exact uniform rates and finite length

For every initial law, \(\mu P^k\to\pi_\nu\) in the conditional metric. The smallest uniform absolute error bound at time \(k\) is
\[
\boxed{\sup_{\mu\in\mathscr M_\nu}E(\mu P^k)^2
=\int M(u)(1-a(u))^k\,\nu(du).}
\tag{3.6}
\]
For a particular initial error density \(h\), the actual total length is exactly
\[
\boxed{\sum_{k\ge0}
\left[\int a(u)(1-a(u))^kh(u)\,\nu(du)\right]^{1/2}.}
\tag{3.7}
\]
Thus pointwise distributional convergence does not imply finite law-space length. These formulas also give an exact relationship between a proposed gauge and a desired rate: the gauge is valid iff it dominates (3.3), while a uniform absolute rate is valid iff it dominates (3.6). Uniform relative geometric rates are instead governed by the essential lower bound on \(a\), as in Theorem 2.2.

Proof. Apply dominated convergence to (3.1). The supremum in (3.6) is achieved by the measurable farthest-endpoint law, for which \(h=M\). Equation (3.7) is (3.2) at each time. \(\square\)

For a generalized inverse \(\phi^{\leftarrow}(e)=\inf\{t:\phi(t)\ge e\}\), the exact energy identity also gives the valid matched-gauge recurrence
\[
E_{k+1}\le\sqrt{E_k^2-[\phi^{\leftarrow}(E_k)]^2}.
\tag{3.10}
\]
It follows from \(E_k\le\phi(\mathcal R_k)\) and (3.2). This does not impose a power gauge. The exact integral rate (3.6) can retain more information than this scalar recurrence.

### Explicit polynomial example

Let \(U=(0,1)\), \(\nu\) uniform, \(b=1/2\), and \(a(u)=u^r\), \(r>0\). For \(0\le t^2\le1/[2(r+1)]\), (3.3) gives
\[
\phi(t)=2^{-r/[2(r+1)]}(r+1)^{1/[2(r+1)]}
t^{1/(r+1)}.
\tag{3.8}
\]
Indeed the optimal error density is \(h=(1/2)1_{[0,s]}\), so
\(E^2=s/2\) and \(\mathcal R^2=s^{r+1}/[2(r+1)]\).
For the worst initial law \(h=1/2\),
\[
E_k^2=\frac1{2r}B(1/r,k+1)
\sim\frac{\Gamma(1+1/r)}2k^{-1/r},
\]
\[
\mathcal R_k^2=\frac1{2r}B(1+1/r,k+1)
\asymp k^{-1-1/r}.
\tag{3.9}
\]
Consequently all laws converge with the sharp uniform absolute order \(k^{-1/(2r)}\). The worst-law total length is finite iff \(r<1\). For \(r\ge1\), a convergent actual law orbit has infinite total conditional Wasserstein length. This does not contradict the finite-length T5 theorem, whose fixed-Q hypotheses are absent.

For \(a(u)=e^{-1/u}\), (3.3) is still an explicit non-power gauge. No positive Hölder gauge is possible: choosing \(h=(1/2)1_{[0,s]}\) gives \(E^2=s/2\) and \(\mathcal R^2\le(s/2)e^{-1/s}\). Thus \(E/\mathcal R^q\to\infty\) for every \(q>0\). This is exact invariant identification, genuine convergence and a useful general gauge without any power envelope.

**Scope warning:** this interval example has a state-dependent refresh probability. The kernel is Feller for continuous \(a,b\), but the usual threshold implementation need not consist of continuous random maps in \(u\). It is not silently claimed to meet the original finite continuous-RFI assumptions.

### A compact continuous-RFI implementation of arbitrarily slow fibers

Let \(U=\{0\}\cup\{2^{-n}:n\ge1\}\), \(X=\{0,1\}\). For each \(n\), choose the map that refreshes the bit only on the clopen fiber \(u=2^{-n}\), with total probability
\(a_n=2^{-n^2-4n-4}\), split equally between the two bits; use identity with the remaining probability. Each individual map is continuous on compact \(U\times X\). Use conserved marginal \(\nu(2^{-n})=2^{-n}\), \(\nu(0)=0\).

All conclusions (3.1)–(3.7) hold with sums. There is no positive Hölder EB: an error supported at fiber \(n\) has \(E_n^2=2^{-n-1}\), \(\mathcal R_n^2=a_nE_n^2\). Nonetheless the optimal generalized gauge is given by (3.3), and the exact worst-law convergence curve is \((1/2)\sum_n2^{-n}(1-a_n)^k\to0\).

These maps also satisfy a uniform expected almost-firm estimate in the ambient Euclidean metric. For pairs in the same fiber the firm-energy identity holds. For different fibers with different bit values it again holds. If their bits agree, the excess output-plus-displacement energy is at most \(a_n+a_m\). For \(n<m\),
\[
|2^{-n}-2^{-m}|^2\ge2^{-2n-2},\quad
a_n+a_m\le(17/16)2^{-4n-4},
\]
so the relative excess is at most \(17/256<1\); the comparison with the limiting fiber is easier. Thus \((\alpha,\epsilon)=(1/2,17/256)\) is valid. The identity probability is positive since \(\sum_na_n<1\). This is countably many continuous actual maps, not a finite-map claim.

## 4. Genuinely dependent blocks: import Dobrushin comparison

The product class is not the limit of the method. Here is a complete finite-state dependent version, with the underlying mechanism credited to classical Gibbs coupling/Dobrushin comparison. See Wang–Wu, [Bernoulli 20(4), 2014, equations (2.2)–(2.3), Theorem 2.3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1410.4329), for the mature influence-matrix and coupling theory.

Fix one fiber \(u\) and a strictly positive target \(\beta\) on \(\{0,1\}^m\), specified by its full conditionals \(\beta_i(\cdot\mid x_{-i})\). Let \(C=(c_{ij})\ge0\), \(c_{ii}=0\), satisfy
\[
\operatorname{TV}(\beta_i(\cdot\mid x_{-i}),
\beta_i(\cdot\mid y_{-i}))
\le\sum_jc_{ij}1_{x_j\ne y_j}.
\tag{4.1}
\]
Assume \(\operatorname{spr}(C)<1\), and sampling probabilities \(p_i>0\). Define
\[
r_i(\mu)=\mathbb E_\mu\operatorname{TV}
(\mu_i(\cdot\mid X_{-i}),\beta_i(\cdot\mid X_{-i})).
\]
Choose coordinate weights \(w_i>0\); let \(E_w^2\) be optimal transport with cost \(\sum_iw_i1_{x_i\ne y_i}\), and set \(\mathcal R_w^2=\sum_ip_iw_ir_i\).

### Theorem 4.1: an explicit correlated-specification EB

For every \(\mu\),
\[
\boxed{E_w(\mu)^2\le w^T(I-C)^{-1}r(\mu)
\le K_w^2\mathcal R_w(\mu)^2,}
\]
\[
K_w^2=\max_i\frac{[w^T(I-C)^{-1}]_i}{p_iw_i}.
\tag{4.2}
\]
In particular \(\mathcal R_w=0\) iff \(\mu=\beta\). This residual uses local specifications of the actual kernel and the current law, not an unknown invariant-transport optimization.

Proof. Couple two Gibbs chains, one having the current law's own full conditionals and one having \(\beta\)'s conditionals. At an update of coordinate \(i\), maximally couple the two Bernoulli distributions. Both \(\mu\) and \(\beta\) are stationary for their respective Gibbs kernels. Start a joint law with these marginals and take a subsequential Cesàro limit; finiteness gives a stationary joint coupling with these marginals. If \(d_i\) is its mismatch probability, conditional triangle inequalities and (4.1) imply
\[
d_i\le r_i(\mu)+\sum_jc_{ij}d_j.
\]
The positive sampling factor cancels from the stationarity equation. Since \((I-C)^{-1}\ge0\), \(d\le(I-C)^{-1}r\). The coupling cost bounds the optimal cost from above, giving (4.2). \(\square\)

### Native convergence certificate

For two actual \(\beta\)-Gibbs chains, the same coupling gives the mismatch update bound
\[
d^+\le Bd,\qquad B=I-D_p+D_pC.
\tag{4.3}
\]
There exist \(w>0\) and \(\theta<1\) with \(w^TB\le\theta w^T\). To see this, \((I-C)\) is a nonsingular M-matrix; hence so is \(D_p(I-C)=I-B\), implying \(\operatorname{spr}(B)<1\). Explicitly choose \(w^T=\boldsymbol1^T(I-B)^{-1}\) and \(\theta=\max_j(w_j-1)/w_j<1\). Thus
\[
E_w(\mu P)\le\sqrt\theta E_w(\mu).
\tag{4.4}
\]
For frozen \(u\), all constants can be computed pointwise and integrated if common weights and uniform bounds hold. Nonuniform cases require a tail/gauge analysis; uniformity is not automatic.

This genuinely retains interactions between coordinates. It is a useful import into the project, **not a new Dobrushin theorem and not yet a strict extension of Gibbs theory**. Necessity of \(\operatorname{spr}(C)<1\) is not claimed: influence bounds are generally only sufficient.

## 5. A natural coupled random-projection/Gaussian class

Fix a symmetric positive definite matrix \(Q\in\mathbb R^{m\times m}\), vector \(b\), and coordinate probabilities \(p_i>0\), \(\sum_ip_i=1\). Let \(\beta=N(Q^{-1}b,Q^{-1})\). Draw \(i\), then \(\xi_i\sim N(0,Q_{ii})\), and update
\[
T_{i,\xi}(x)=x+\frac{b_i+\xi_i-(Qx)_i}{Q_{ii}}e_i.
\tag{5.1}
\]
This is the \(Q\)-orthogonal projection onto the random affine hyperplane \(\{x:(Qx)_i=b_i+\xi_i\}\), hence a proximal map of a normal cone in that metric. It is also exactly the Gaussian Gibbs conditional update. Off-diagonal \(Q\) makes the blocks genuinely coupled.

Put
\[
D=\operatorname{diag}(p_i/Q_{ii}),\quad
\zeta=\lambda_{\min}(Q^{1/2}DQ^{1/2})>0.
\tag{5.2}
\]
Let \(W_{2,Q}\) use cost \(\|x-y\|_Q^2\). For the local conditional transport residual define
\[
\mathcal R_Q(\mu)^2=
\sum_ip_iQ_{ii}\mathbb E_{X_{-i}\sim\mu}
W_2\bigl(\mu_i(\cdot\mid X_{-i}),
\beta_i(\cdot\mid X_{-i})\bigr)^2.
\tag{5.3}
\]

### Theorem 5.1: explicit projection-matrix verifier

For every finite-second-moment law \(\mu\),
\[
W_{2,Q}(\mu P,\beta)\le\sqrt{1-\zeta}\,W_{2,Q}(\mu,\beta),
\tag{5.4}
\]
\[
\boxed{W_{2,Q}(\mu,\beta)
\le\frac{\mathcal R_Q(\mu)}{1-\sqrt{1-\zeta}}.}
\tag{5.5}
\]
Thus \(\mathcal R_Q=0\) iff \(\mu=\beta\), with computable coefficients from the native precision matrix and sampling probabilities.

Proof. Each Gibbs update preserves \(\beta\). Under identical noise, a difference \(h\) is sent to \(A_ih\), where \(A_i=I-e_ie_i^TQ/Q_{ii}\). The projection identity gives
\[
\mathbb E\|A_ih\|_Q^2
=\|h\|_Q^2-h^TQDQh
\le(1-\zeta)\|h\|_Q^2.
\]
Apply this to an optimal coupling for (5.4). To couple \(\mu\) with \(\mu P\), select \(i\), hold \(X_{-i}\) fixed and optimally transport its conditional coordinate law to the known Gaussian conditional. The mean squared \(Q\)-cost is exactly (5.3), so \(W_{2,Q}(\mu,\mu P)\le\mathcal R_Q\). The triangle inequality and (5.4) give (5.5). \(\square\)

One may add a frozen variable \(u\) and use pointwise \(Q(u),b(u),p(u)\). Integrating the correspondingly weighted fiber distance is legitimate under measurable coefficients, finite integrated moments, and a uniform positive \(\zeta\). If using the unweighted Euclidean fiber metric, common spectral upper/lower bounds on \(Q(u)\) supply explicit norm-equivalence factors.

The final triangle step in (5.5) is **already the known HLS-style mechanism**. What is supplied here is its native residual domination and explicit matrix, not a new abstract principle. Sharpness of (5.5) is not claimed.

## 6. Relation to the old synchronous discrepancy and to RL

### 6.1 The parallel-projection failure is repaired exactly

For \(T_j(u,v)=(u,j)\), \(j\) fair, write \(r(u)=\mu(V=1\mid U=u)\). The old discrepancy is
\(\Psi(\mu)=W_2(\mu_V,\operatorname{Bern}(1/2))\), as proved in the upstream attack. Our conditional residual instead is
\[
\mathcal R(\mu)^2=\int|r(u)-1/2|\,\mu_U(du)
=\mathsf W_{\mu_U}(\mu,\mu P)^2.
\tag{6.1}
\]
It vanishes exactly when the bit is conditionally fair given the untouched variable, which is exactly invariance. For
\(\mu_t=\tfrac12\delta_{(1/2-t,0)}+\tfrac12\delta_{(1/2+t,1)}\),
\[
\Psi(\mu_t)=0,\qquad \mathcal R(\mu_t)=1/\sqrt2>0.
\]
Actual convergence occurs in one step. No finite constant can compare \(\mathcal R\) from above by the old \(\Psi\). Thus this is a strict improvement in **identification**, not a proof that the old residual was necessary.

### 6.2 Retaining only coordinate marginals still fails

Let \(\mu=\tfrac12\delta_{(0,0)}+\tfrac12\delta_{(1,1)}\), target the independent fair-bit product. Both coordinate marginal distances vanish. Nevertheless
\(d_1^2=d_2^2=1/2\), so the conditional residual is positive. For equal random-scan probabilities, either coordinate update destroys the dependence and the law reaches the target in one step. Full conditionals, not only coordinate marginals, are essential.

### 6.3 This changes topology: a mandatory limitation

Ordinary joint Wasserstein permits horizontal transport between conserved fibers. In the preceding \(\mu_t\) example,
\(W_2(\mu_t,\delta_{1/2}\otimes\beta)=t\to0\), while \(\mathcal R(\mu_t)=1/\sqrt2\).
Conditional distance therefore cannot be treated as a locally equivalent replacement for ordinary joint \(W_2\) across varying conserved marginals. Even within a fixed nonatomic marginal, rapidly oscillating conditional laws can be close in ordinary \(W_2\) and far conditionally. Our equivalence theorem freezes \(\nu\) and explicitly uses \(\mathsf W_\nu\).

For every initial law, convergence in \(\mathsf W_\nu\) implies ordinary joint \(W_2\) convergence because the latter has more admissible couplings. The reverse and equality of local rates are not asserted.

### 6.4 Safe stochastic RL interface

For coordinate projections, use a coupling that preserves \(u\), and share the sampled update. The reflected difference \(2(T_i x-T_i y)-(x-y)\) has the same norm as \(x-y\). Thus the original state-level RL geometry is exactly the linear monotone slice, \(\gamma=1,L=1\), on each fiber. The quadratic identity yields expected output-plus-synchronous-defect energy equal to input energy. With \(p_i\ge a_*\), that **same coupling's** defect is at least \(a_*\) times its input squared norm, proving (2.7).

This is a valid stochastic coupling application of the Hilbert RL identity. It is not genuine nonlinear \(\gamma<1\), and it does not require defining the invalid signed-measure expression \(2\mu P-\mu\) as a probability reflector. The conditional space is generally infinite-dimensional; one cannot cite finite-dimensional T5 verbatim as a theorem there.

The conditional specification residual \(\mathcal R\) is not always the same as that synchronous defect. In particular, it is generally **false** for multicoordinate scan that
\(E(\mu P)^2+\mathcal R(\mu)^2\le E(\mu)^2\).
For a fair-bit target in two coordinates, equal scan, and masses \(\mu=(1/2,0,1/4,1/4)\) on \(00,01,10,11\), exact transport gives
\[
E^2=1/4,\qquad E_+^2=1/8,\qquad\mathcal R^2=1/4.
\]
Their sum is \(3/8>1/4\). Indeed \(\mu P=(5/16,3/16,5/16,3/16)\): optimal transport changes only the second bit at total mass \(1/8\). The initial law has excess mass \(1/4\) at \(00\) and deficient mass \(1/4\) at \(01\); both conditional-coordinate discrepancies are \(1/4\). This is why Theorem 2.2 proves convergence using its actual synchronous coupling and proves the independently computable EB separately. Only in the one-bit refresh model do the exact matched identities (3.2) hold.

### Decisive additional overlap: weak Poincaré profiles

The general-gauge calculation has a mature functional-inequality neighbor that must not be omitted. Andrieu–Lee–Power–Wang, [*Comparison of Markov chains via weak Poincaré inequalities*, Section 2, Definitions 4–6, Theorem 8 and Remark 13](https://arxiv.org/html/2112.05605v2), already develop general profiles, their convex conjugates, and an information-preserving equivalence between the profile and a one-step scalar decay inequality. Their subsequent [*Weak Poincaré inequalities for Markov chains: theory and applications*, Annals of Applied Probability 36(1), 46–107, 2026](https://research-information.bris.ac.uk/en/publications/weak-poincar%C3%A9-inequalities-for-markov-chains-theory-and-applicati/) treats necessity and subgeometric rates; the latter publication metadata and abstract were verified, not its entire theorem system.

There is an exact elementary analytic reduction in our one-bit model. Set \(f=\sqrt h\) and let \(A\) be multiplication by \(a\) on \(L^2(\nu)\). Then
\[
E^2=\|f\|_2^2,\quad\mathcal R^2=\langle Af,f\rangle,
\quad E_k^2=\|(I-A)^{k/2}f\|_2^2,
\quad |f|^2\le M.
\]
Formula (3.4) is precisely the optimal bounded-profile estimate for this multiplication operator. This observation does **not** change the actual algorithm; it identifies the existing spectral/weak-Poincaré nature of the scalar calculation. Therefore the abstract gauge-to-rate mechanism, even with non-power gauges, cannot be advertised as a new convergence paradigm. The conditional residual's exact relation to the source discrepancy and its native verification remain the specific object of this project.
