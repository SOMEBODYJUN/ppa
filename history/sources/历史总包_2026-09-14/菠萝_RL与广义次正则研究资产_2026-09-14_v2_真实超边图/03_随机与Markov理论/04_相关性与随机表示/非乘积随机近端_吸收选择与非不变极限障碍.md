# Native non-product random proximal maps: an absorption and selection barrier

Date: 2026-09-09. Bounded task: `native_nonproduct_random_prox_attack`.

## 0. Decision

**KILL as the requested positive stochastic-RL flagship application. GO as a rigorous boundary module and an adversarial benchmark.**

The class attacked here is genuine random selection among the *global minimizers* of a nonconvex proximal subproblem, with a nonseparable group-sparsity quadratic example. It is not a deterministic map with an independently appended refresh coordinate. Its full proximal graph and its actual randomized selection kernel are computed from the objective.

Four precise obstructions result.

1. A matched RL certificate using the **physical** transition and a zero-distance stationary reference forces the stationary chain to be absorbing, not merely stationary. This excludes every invariant law with continuing movement. It is a limitation of that certificate, not a theorem that such chains cannot converge in law.
2. For random global proximal minimizers of **one fixed objective**, every invariant law is in fact supported on absorbing states. Strict descent proves this without an integrability assumption on the objective. Random tie selection cannot create sustained stationary circulation in this class.
3. A concrete nonseparable group-\(\ell_0\) quadratic has a closed, genuinely two-valued proximal graph and a Borel randomized kernel with a unique invariant law. Nevertheless, arbitrarily close to that invariant law in \(W_2\), there are law orbits of finite Wasserstein length converging to **noninvariant** limits. Every vanishing-gauge error bound for the actual law-step residual fails on every full \(W_2\) neighborhood of the invariant law.
4. Absolutely continuous input noise makes the multivalued tie choices irrelevant almost surely in this finite-dimensional global-proximal class. For the explicit model it also regularizes the kernel by ordinary convolution. That repair does not establish an essential multivalued/nonlinear RL advantage.

The mathematical proofs below are complete project derivations (`AI_INFERENCE` / `PROJECT_PROOF`). An independent sibling audit checked the concrete proximal formula, invariant identification, limiting orbit and unavoidable non-Feller boundary. Global priority is **not** claimed. These are not yet a SIOPT or Mathematical Programming flagship package: the general mechanisms are elementary stationarity/descent, convex-potential differentiability and the classical need to pass to the limit through a Markov kernel.

## 1. Scope, native objects and literature gate

The inputs reviewed were the current RL manuscript and the stochastic coupling report, especially the distinction between a physical transition coupling and an arbitrary law transport coupling:

- `upload/01-RL_PPA_T5_core_manuscript_2026-09-07.md`;
- `output/STOCHASTIC_RL_THEORY_ATTACK.md`;
- `output/ATTACK_RANDOM_PROXIMAL_CORRELATION_AWARE.md`.

We write

\[
\mathsf P_\lambda f(x)
=\arg\min_y\left\{f(y)+\frac{\|y-x\|^2}{2\lambda}\right\}
\]

for global proximal minimizers. For nonconvex \(f\), this is generally only a subset of \((I+\lambda\partial f)^{-1}(x)\). The full limiting-subdifferential resolvent can contain nonminimizing stationary points of the proximal subproblem. Those two objects must not be interchanged.

A *kernel-faithful* joint realization means

\[
\mathcal L(X^+\mid X)=K(X,\cdot),
\]

not just \(X\sim\mu\), \(X^+\sim\mu K\), and not just \(X^+\in\mathsf P_\lambda f(X)\). A support-faithful coupling preserves the last relation but can still change the conditional selection probabilities. A law-only transport coupling need preserve neither.

The latest strong baseline was included: Pischke–Powell's *Convergence guarantees for stochastic algorithms solving non-unique problems in metric spaces* already supplies stochastic regularity in mean, measurable moving references, and pointwise-to-mean lifting under convex-modulus conditions. Its stochastic PPA application uses convex integrands and diminishing steps. These are genuine prior mechanisms, not proposed innovations here. [Definitions 3.3–3.6, Lemma 3.8 and Section 5.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.06129v1)

HLS explicitly uses continuity/Feller to conclude that a distributional limit is invariant. The counterexample below removes precisely that protection; it does **not** refute their theorem. [HLS, proof ending at the Feller/invariance step](https://arxiv.org/html/2206.05213v3)

## 2. A universal physical-coupling obstruction

### Theorem 2.1 — zero-distance physical RL forces absorption

Let \(K\) be a Borel Markov kernel on \(\mathbb R^n\), and let \(\pi K=\pi\). Suppose a matched-coupling certificate at \(\mu=\pi\) provides random variables \((X,Y,X^+)\) with

\[
X\sim\pi,\qquad \mathcal L(X^+\mid X)=K(X,\cdot),
\qquad \|X-Y\|_{L^2}=0,
\]

and, almost surely,

\[
\|2X^+-X-Y\|\le\omega(\|X-Y\|),\qquad \omega(0)=0.
\tag{2.1}
\]

Then

\[
X^+=X\ \text{a.s.},\qquad K(x,\{x\})=1\quad\pi\text{-a.e.}
\tag{2.2}
\]

**Proof.** Zero \(L^2\) distance gives \(X=Y\) almost surely. Substituting in (2.1) gives \(2\|X^+-X\|=0\). Disintegration of the physical transition proves the second assertion. \(\square\)

This applies whenever the reference certificate vanishes exactly at the invariant family and includes its diagonal references, as the proposed matched law theorem does. It requires neither a power modulus nor an error bound.

### Approximate-reference version

One cannot evade this obstruction merely by declining to attain a diagonal reference. Suppose the physical \((X,X^+)\) law is fixed, references \(Y_j\) satisfy \(\|X-Y_j\|_2\to0\), and

\[
\|2X^+-X-Y_j\|_2\le L\|X-Y_j\|_2^\gamma,
\qquad L<\infty,\quad\gamma>0.
\]

Then

\[
2\|X^+-X\|_2
\le\|X-Y_j\|_2+L\|X-Y_j\|_2^\gamma\to0.
\]

For pointwise \(L d^\gamma\), \(0<\gamma\le1\), Jensen supplies the displayed \(L^2\) estimate. The same conclusion holds for other moduli when their \(L^2\) closure tends to zero.

### Multi-step and support-only boundaries

If the same argument uses the *physical* \(m\)-step output, it forces \(K^m(x,\cdot)=\delta_x\) for \(\pi\)-almost every \(x\). A deterministic period dividing \(m\) can satisfy this; a genuinely mixing nondegenerate stationary chain cannot. Multi-step recoupling alone therefore does not eliminate the physical-coupling issue.

If only support-faithfulness is required, the weaker conclusion is

\[
x\in\mathsf P_\lambda f(x)\quad\pi\text{-a.e.}
\]

or the analogous fixed-graph relation. It does not force the prescribed kernel to select the diagonal. This distinction is essential: a transport coupling may represent the correct output marginal using different conditional branch frequencies. Such a theorem can be correct, but its native verification must explicitly construct and justify that recoupling.

### What does and does not survive

The result does not kill an RL theory involving a *moving* stationary reference \(Y^+\) and the relative difference

\[
(X^+-Y^+)-(X-Y).
\]

That avoids (2.1)'s stationary diagonal obstruction. However, its relative defect is not the actual law step or physical step; correlation-aware recoupling must still be verified. HLS already works with moving pairs in its expected-firmness geometry. The proposed new contribution cannot consist only of replacing a fixed reference by a moving one.

## 3. Same-objective randomized global prox: all stationary motion disappears

### Theorem 3.1 — exact invariant-family identification from proximal descent

Let \(f:\mathbb R^n\to(-\infty,+\infty]\) be proper and Borel, and suppose the required global proximal minimizers exist. Let \(K\) be a Borel kernel satisfying

\[
K(x,\mathsf P_\lambda f(x))=1
\quad (x\in\operatorname{dom}f),\qquad\lambda>0.
\tag{3.1}
\]

Define its absorbing set

\[
\mathcal A_K=\{x:K(x,\{x\})=1\}.
\]

Among laws supported on \(\operatorname{dom}f\),

\[
\boxed{\operatorname{inv}K
=\{\pi:\pi(\mathcal A_K)=1\}.}
\tag{3.2}
\]

In particular every stationary physical chain is pointwise constant almost surely. No assumption \(\int|f|\,d\pi<\infty\) is needed.

**Proof.** For every allowed transition, comparison with \(y=x\) gives

\[
f(X^+)+\frac{\|X^+-X\|^2}{2\lambda}\le f(X).
\tag{3.3}
\]

If \(X\sim\pi\) and \(\pi K=\pi\), then \(f(X)\) and \(f(X^+)\) have the same distribution and are finite almost surely. Their bounded strictly increasing transforms \(\arctan f(X)\) and \(\arctan f(X^+)\) have the same mean, while the latter is at most the former. Therefore they are equal almost surely; strict monotonicity implies \(f(X^+)=f(X)\). Equation (3.3) forces \(X^+=X\). Conversely, every law supported on absorbing states is invariant by direct integration. \(\square\)

The same argument works with variable positive steps chosen as part of a stationary transition, provided the actual inequality has a strictly positive coefficient on every nonzero move. It does not require a common uniform lower bound for the coefficient in this stationary argument.

### Full-support tie selection

If \(\mathsf P_\lambda f(x)\) is finite and the policy assigns positive probability to each distinct minimizer, then

\[
\mathcal A_K=\{x:\mathsf P_\lambda f(x)=\{x\}\}.
\tag{3.4}
\]

A mere fixed point \(x\in\mathsf P_\lambda f(x)\) need not be absorbing: another tied minimizer has lower objective and can be selected. This is exactly the distinction exploited in Section 5.

### Exceptions are real changes of problem

Theorem 3.1 does not apply to inconsistent randomly changing objectives, noisy/inexact proximal minimization without common strict descent, splitting maps without a common descent function, or nonminimizing stationary selections from \((I+\lambda\partial f)^{-1}\). These are legitimate remaining territories, but they require new native arguments. They cannot inherit the single-objective proof by changing terminology.

## 4. Common minimizers already give linear solution comparison

### Proposition 4.1 — no essential nonlinear anchored scale in consistent global prox

Suppose \(p\) is a global minimizer of \(f_i\), and \(x^+\in\mathsf P_\lambda f_i(x)\). Then

\[
\|x-x^+\|\le\|x-p\|,
\qquad \|x^+-p\|\le2\|x-p\|,
\]

and consequently

\[
\|2x^+-x-p\|\le3\|x-p\|.
\tag{4.1}
\]

**Proof.** Comparing the proximal objective with \(p\), then using \(f_i(x^+)\ge f_i(p)\), proves the first bound. The remaining two are triangle inequalities. \(\square\)

If several functions share a nonempty minimizer set \(S\), these bounds hold uniformly for every sampled function and every proximal minimizer with \(p\in P_S(x)\). The corresponding statement for nearest projections is the indicator-function case. It does **not** assert full all-pairs Lipschitz regularity, Fejér monotonicity, or convergence. It says only that an essentially sublinear solution-comparison exponent is not forced in this consistent global-minimizer class. An all-pairs Hölder advantage could remain, but it would need a different indispensable role.

## 5. Fully native non-product group-sparse proximal counterexample

### 5.1 Objective, complete graph and actual policy

Consider the ordinary group-selection objective

\[
f(x)=\frac12x^THx-b^Tx+4\mathbf1_{\{x\ne0\}},\qquad
H=\begin{pmatrix}2&1\\1&2\end{pmatrix},\quad
b=\binom33,
\quad\lambda=1.
\tag{5.1}
\]

This is proper, lower semicontinuous, coercive, semialgebraic and nonconvex. The quadratic coordinates interact, and the penalty charges the joint event that the whole group is nonzero. The update is not a Cartesian product of two scalar proximal kernels. There is no appended refresh variable or spectator coordinate. Nevertheless, one witness ray below has an exact scalar reduction; nonseparability by itself is not an originality claim.

Write

\[
Q=H+I=\begin{pmatrix}3&1\\1&3\end{pmatrix},
\quad Q^{-1}=\frac18\begin{pmatrix}3&-1\\-1&3\end{pmatrix},
\]
\[
t(x)=Q^{-1}(b+x),\qquad
g(x)=\frac12(b+x)^TQ^{-1}(b+x).
\]

Completing the square in the nonzero branch gives the **complete** global proximal mapping

\[
\boxed{
\mathsf P_1 f(x)=
\begin{cases}
\{0\},&g(x)<4,\\
\{0,t(x)\},&g(x)=4,\\
\{t(x)\},&g(x)>4.
\end{cases}}
\tag{5.2}
\]

At \(g=4\), \(t(x)\ne0\). If \(b+x=0\), the nonzero branch has infimum above the zero candidate, and the first case remains valid. Thus no omitted minimizer occurs at the excluded origin of that branch.

Define the actual kernel by uniform tie breaking:

\[
K(x,\cdot)=
\begin{cases}
\delta_0,&g(x)<4,\\
\tfrac12\delta_0+\tfrac12\delta_{t(x)},&g(x)=4,\\
\delta_{t(x)},&g(x)>4.
\end{cases}
\tag{5.3}
\]

It is Borel since \(g,t\) are continuous. An iid fair bit selects the two branches on the tie ellipsoid and is ignored elsewhere. Hence this is a fully specified Markov chain, not an unspecified set-valued recursion. Both outputs at a tie are actual global proximal minimizers.

The mixed term of the threshold is explicit:

\[
g(x)=\frac{3x_1^2-2x_1x_2+3x_2^2+12x_1+12x_2+36}{16}.
\tag{5.4}
\]

The graph in (5.2) is closed: along either open region its branch is continuous, and all one-sided branch limits are included at the boundary.

### Theorem 5.1 — unique invariant law but noninvariant finite-length limits

For (5.1)–(5.3), let \(v=(1,1)^T\). Then:

1. The unique invariant law is \(\delta_0\).
2. For every \(a_0>1\), starting at \(x_0=a_0v\) produces
   \[
   x_k=a_kv,\qquad a_k=1+(a_0-1)4^{-k},
   \tag{5.5}
   \]
   so the path has finite total length and converges to \(v\), but \(\delta_v\) is not invariant.
3. Such failure occurs in every full \(W_2\) neighborhood of \(\delta_0\), through arbitrarily small mixture masses.
4. No Borel choice of tie probabilities can make a kernel supported on (5.2) weakly continuous at \(v\). In particular the uniform policy is non-Feller.

**Proof of 1.** One has \(g(0)=9/4<4\), hence zero is absorbing. Every nonzero absorbing point must satisfy \(t(x)=x\), that is \(Hx=b\). Its only candidate is \(v=H^{-1}b\). But

\[
g(v)=4,\quad t(v)=v,\quad
K(v,\cdot)=\tfrac12\delta_0+\tfrac12\delta_v,
\]

so \(v\) is not absorbing. Theorem 3.1 identifies the entire invariant family as \(\{\delta_0\}\), with no moment assumption on a putative invariant law.

**Proof of 2.** On \(x=av\),

\[
t(av)=\frac{a+3}{4}v,
\qquad g(av)=\frac{(a+3)^2}{4}.
\]

For \(a>1\), \(g>4\) and \((a+3)/4>1\). The sampled bit is irrelevant on these actual inputs; the unique proximal branch is used forever. The affine recursion solves to (5.5). Its total length is

\[
\sum_{k\ge0}\|x_{k+1}-x_k\|
=(a_0-1)\|v\|=(a_0-1)\sqrt2.
\tag{5.6}
\]

The kernel at the limit is the nondegenerate mixture displayed above. Thus the limit Dirac law is not invariant. At the limit state itself, repeated tie sampling has a geometrically distributed waiting time before reaching zero. This confirms that the declared kernel really includes random branch evolution, even though the witness ray has forced branches before the limit.

**Proof of 3.** For any \(0<\epsilon<1\), set

\[
\mu_0=(1-\epsilon)\delta_0+\epsilon\delta_{a_0v}.
\]

Then

\[
\mu_k=(1-\epsilon)\delta_0+\epsilon\delta_{a_kv}
\xrightarrow[W_2]{}
\mu_\infty=(1-\epsilon)\delta_0+\epsilon\delta_v,
\tag{5.7}
\]

where \(\mu_\infty K=(1-\epsilon/2)\delta_0+(\epsilon/2)\delta_v\ne\mu_\infty\). Moreover

\[
W_2(\mu_k,\delta_0)=\sqrt{2\epsilon}\,a_k,
\]
\[
W_2(\mu_k,\mu_{k+1})
=\sqrt{2\epsilon}\,(a_k-a_{k+1})
=\frac34\sqrt{2\epsilon}\,(a_0-1)4^{-k}.
\tag{5.8}
\]

These exact transport costs follow from ordered one-dimensional transport on the common ray; matching the zero masses and matching the positive atoms is optimal. Choosing \(\sqrt{2\epsilon}a_0<r\) puts the whole orbit in any specified \(W_2\) ball of radius \(r\) around its unique invariant law. The finite total law length equals \(\sqrt{2\epsilon}(a_0-1)\).

**Proof of 4.** Let \(a\uparrow1\) from below near one. Then \(g(av)<4\), so every supported policy has kernel \(\delta_0\). Approaching from \(a>1\), every policy has kernel \(\delta_{t(av)}\to\delta_v\). These distinct weak limits cannot agree with one kernel value at \(v\). A bounded continuous test separating zero and \(v\) witnesses failure of Feller continuity. \(\square\)

### Corollary 5.2 — every vanishing-gauge local law-step EB fails

Let

\[
\mathcal R(\mu)=W_2(\mu,\mu K),\qquad\mathcal I=\{\delta_0\}.
\]

Its zeros identify stationarity exactly, but there is no radius \(r>0\) and no nondecreasing \(\psi\) with \(\psi(t)\to0\) at zero such that

\[
d_{W_2}(\mu,\mathcal I)\le\psi(\mathcal R(\mu))
\quad\text{for all }W_2(\mu,\delta_0)<r.
\tag{5.9}
\]

**Proof.** Fix the small \(\epsilon\) chosen in Theorem 5.1, and let \(k\to\infty\) in (5.7)–(5.8). The residual tends to zero while distance to \(\mathcal I\) tends to \(\sqrt{2\epsilon}>0\). \(\square\)

For the fair-bit representation of (5.3), the HLS-style synchronous residual relative to the sole invariant \(\delta_0\) has the same value on these witness laws. Indeed each map fixes zero, and at \(a_kv\) both labels give \(t(a_kv)\). Globally that synchronous residual vanishes only for laws supported on absorbing states, again exactly \(\delta_0\). The example is nevertheless outside the source continuity/expected almost-firmness hypotheses. It is a native selection obstruction, not a counterexample to their result or a replacement for the previously audited continuous Markov examples.

### No Hölder selection or probabilistic continuity across the jump

The two one-sided limits above exclude every bound

\[
W_2(K(x,\cdot),K(y,\cdot))\le L\|x-y\|^\gamma,
\qquad \gamma>0,
\]

on a full neighborhood of \(v\), regardless of the tie policy. The deterministic full proximal graph also violates AP-RL at the tie itself: identical Minty input and distinct outputs contradict any modulus vanishing at zero. Making \(\gamma\) smaller cannot accommodate an actual jump.

### Why this is not a counterexample to T5

For the limiting subdifferential,

\[
\partial f(x)=Hx-b\quad(x\ne0),\qquad
\partial f(0)=\mathbb R^2.
\]

The latter follows directly from the positive jump in \(f\) away from zero. Hence

\[
\operatorname{zer}\partial f=\{0,v\}.
\]

The witness path converges to a valid zero \(v\). The deterministic zero problem and the chosen-kernel invariant-law problem have different target sets. Locally around the nonzero graph branch, the resolvent is affine and Lipschitz; the randomized transition from \(v\) to zero leaves that local branch. The full limiting-subdifferential resolvent is larger still than (5.2), since the zero output is stationary for every input. None of these objects has been silently substituted for another.

## 6. Continuous noise removes tie-choice relevance

### Proposition 6.1 — almost-everywhere single-valued global prox

Let \(f\) be proper lsc and let

\[
h(y)=\lambda f(y)+\tfrac12\|y\|^2,
\qquad h^*(x)=\sup_y\{\langle x,y\rangle-h(y)\}.
\]

Suppose \(h^*\) is finite on an open set \(U\), and \(\mathsf P_\lambda f(x)\) is nonempty there. Then \(\mathsf P_\lambda f(x)\) is single-valued for Lebesgue-almost every \(x\in U\).

**Proof.** A proximal minimizer \(y\) attains the supremum defining \(h^*(x)\). For any \(z\),

\[
h^*(z)\ge\langle z,y\rangle-h(y)
=h^*(x)+\langle z-x,y\rangle.
\]

Thus every proximal minimizer lies in the convex subdifferential \(\partial h^*(x)\). The finite convex function \(h^*\) is locally Lipschitz on \(U\), and differentiable almost everywhere. At its differentiability points all proximal minimizers equal \(\nabla h^*(x)\). \(\square\)

The convex-potential characterization of even nonconvex proximity operators is established machinery; the elementary derivation above deliberately makes no novelty claim. Gribonval–Nikolova's Theorem 1 supplies that characterization, and their Example 4 explicitly records discontinuous, two-valued hard thresholding. [Primary full text](https://arxiv.org/html/1807.04014v4)

If a noisy proximal input has an absolutely continuous conditional distribution in \(U\), any two measurable tie policies induce the same transition law. Thus continuous input noise does not turn tie-selection multiplicity into a new essential advantage; it avoids that set almost surely. This does not say the proximal map must become globally Lipschitz, nor that every noise model has an absolutely continuous conditional law.

For (5.1), the tie set is the ellipsoid \(g=4\), explicitly a null set. If additive noise has density \(p\in L^1\), define

\[
K_p(x,A)=\int k(u,A)p(u-x)\,du,
\]

where \(k\) is any measurable tie policy supported on (5.2). For a bounded test \(|\varphi|\le1\),

\[
|K_p\varphi(x)-K_p\varphi(y)|
\le\|p(\cdot-x)-p(\cdot-y)\|_{L^1}.
\tag{6.1}
\]

Translation continuity in \(L^1\) gives strong Feller continuity; tie policies have disappeared from the law. This is ordinary convolution regularization. It does not supply the invariant law, a sharp rate, or a new RL–EB theorem.

## 7. Native finite-support calculus: why the same barrier is not special to one group

For the standard coordinate-sparse quadratic

\[
f(x)=\tfrac12x^THx-b^Tx+\kappa\|x\|_0,
\qquad H\succ0,\quad\kappa>0,
\]

put \(Q=H+\lambda^{-1}I\), \(c=b+\lambda^{-1}x\). For every support \(J\subset\{1,\ldots,n\}\), set

\[
(T_Jx)_J=Q_{JJ}^{-1}c_J,\quad (T_Jx)_{J^c}=0,
\qquad V_J(x)=\kappa|J|-\tfrac12c_J^TQ_{JJ}^{-1}c_J.
\tag{7.1}
\]

The full proximal mapping consists precisely of \(T_Jx\) for globally minimizing scores \(V_J(x)\). A score-minimizing support cannot have a zero candidate coordinate: removing that coordinate leaves the same quadratic minimizer but saves \(\kappa\), contradicting score minimality. Thus the formula includes every branch without duplicate false active labels. Uniform selection over minimizing supports is Borel.

All stationary laws for that policy are therefore supported on its absorbing fixed points by Theorem 3.1. Those points can be checked from finitely many native candidates

\[
x^J_J=H_{JJ}^{-1}b_J,\quad x^J_{J^c}=0,
\]

with nonzero active coordinates and **strict** winning-score inequalities \(V_J(x^J)<V_I(x^J)\) for every \(I\ne J\). Merely finding \(x^J\in\mathsf P_\lambda f(x^J)\) is not enough under full-support random ties.

This calculus is finite active-set minimization, not new generalized differentiation. Hard-thresholding algorithms and their support-identification mechanisms are mature; for example the explicit multivalued threshold is already displayed in [Bao et al., *Extrapolated Proximal Iterative Hard Thresholding Methods*](https://ww3.math.ucla.edu/camreport/cam14-85.pdf). The purpose here is to show that the obstruction arises from available native data, not an artificial Markov matrix unrelated to an optimization problem.

### The closest existing stochastic and non-Feller frameworks

- **Pischke–Powell (2026).** Regularity in mean, moving approximate references and convex-modulus averaging are already available. Their stated PPA application assumes convex integrands, unlike (5.1), and diminishing steps rather than this fixed-step selected kernel. The general almost-supermartingale framework cannot imply the false convergence-to-invariance claim in Theorem 5.1. This is a distinction of target and assumptions, not a domination claim. [Full text](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.06129v1)
- **HLS.** Expected almost-firmness and a compatible source discrepancy analyze distributions of random iterations, with a continuity/Feller step used to identify limits. Our threshold kernel fails the continuity requirement; the example shows why replacing it with graph closedness is invalid. The source already knows that law convergence is distinct from physical sample-path convergence. [Full text](https://arxiv.org/html/2206.05213v3)
- **Bianchi–Hachem–Salim.** Their constant-step stochastic-approximation work treats a double limit of long time followed by small step and differential-inclusion limits. In its nonconvex proximal-gradient application the nonsmooth proximal term is convex. It is not a theorem for arbitrary global nonconvex proximal tie policies at a fixed step. [Section 10.1](https://arxiv.org/html/1612.03831v3)
- **Quasi-Feller/Harris theory.** Non-Feller chains are not an unstudied field: Attali (2004) already provides quasi-Feller stationary-distribution criteria and stronger recurrence conclusions. The present example does not exclude such tools on other models; it lacks convergence to its unique invariant measure from the witness states. [Original publication record](https://www.numdam.org/item/PS_2004__8__76_0/)
- **Recent non-Feller work.** The 2026 preprint *Existence and uniqueness of invariant measures for non-Feller Markov semigroups* expressly separates tightness from the need for limiting measures not to charge discontinuity sets. Our witness limit charges exactly the problematic seam. This citation records a relevant existing line, not an independent endorsement of all proofs in that preprint. [Sections 2.4–2.6](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.13354v1)

No claim is made that the sparse counterexample or the absorption formulation has global priority. The ingredients are simple enough that a literature-first novelty gate is mandatory before presenting them as an original standalone contribution.

## 9. What this actually adds to the RL research program

The positive flagship target was not met. The useful output is a sharper selection criterion for future candidates:

1. **Decide whether stationarity is absorbing or moving.** If it is moving, a kernel-faithful fixed-reference RL proof is structurally impossible. A matched moving-reference/recoupling construction must be part of the native theorem.
2. **Do not use genuine resolvent jumps as a Hölder advantage.** AP-RL with a vanishing modulus forces single-valuedness on its Minty image. A multivalued operator can still have a single-valued non-Lipschitz resolvent; that is a different and viable geometry.
3. **Specify the branch probabilities, not only the graph.** Closed graphs and PPA finite length do not determine the invariant law of a selected Markov chain. At discontinuous active seams a limit can be a valid operator zero but not stationary for the selection policy.
4. **Account for averaging before claiming a nonlinear EB.** The separately audited small-mass obstruction still applies once the residual is a quadratic integral. The current example has an even stronger no-gauge failure caused by seam discontinuity; these are distinct mechanisms.

The remaining credible positive territory is inconsistent/noisy/randomly switched generalized equations with persistent stationary motion, where (i) the selected transition is sufficiently regular on the invariant support, (ii) a native recoupling retains the relevant operator structure, and (iii) conditional nonlinear certificates are composed before averaging. This is a description of what remains unproved, not a theorem or a recommendation to keep attacking the same killed class.
