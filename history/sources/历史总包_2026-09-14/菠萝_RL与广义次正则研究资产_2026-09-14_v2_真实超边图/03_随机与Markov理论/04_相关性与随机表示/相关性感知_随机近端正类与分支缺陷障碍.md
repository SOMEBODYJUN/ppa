# Correlation-aware residuals beyond Gibbs: genuine random proximal maps

Date: 2026-09-09. Bounded task: `random_proximal_extension_attack`.

## 0. Outcome

**GO as a correct application/obstruction; KILL as a new flagship positive theorem.**

The conditional repair extends rigorously to a non-Gibbs class of inconsistent random proximal algorithms, including noncommuting, rank-deficient quadratic proximal maps and inconsistent affine projections of multivalued normal-cone operators. Native matrices give invariant identification, a linear error bound and quantitative convergence to a nonisolated family of invariant laws. A smooth scalar proximal example gives exact sharp constants and extends the original correlation false-zero phenomenon from reset projections to a nontrivial autoregressive invariant law.

However, the positive proof is precisely conditional lifting of mean-square contractive random iterations plus the Banach a posteriori error estimate. HLS already contains this mechanism, and its original discrepancy-domination hypothesis can be verified on the active space by an explicit second native matrix. Neither tensorization nor Dobrushin is needed; their absence does **not** make the result new.

The useful additional structural conclusion is a no-go theorem: **a nonnegative sum of branchwise stationarity defects cannot identify an invariant law of an inconsistent random proximal algorithm.** Such a sum tests common branch invariance, whereas the actual invariant law is maintained by cancellation between branches. Gibbs succeeds because each grouped conditional-update kernel preserves the same target. Generic random proximal maps do not have that property.

No SIAM-level or higher-journal acceptance claim is made. The intended positive extension has been completed, but it does not pass the requested novelty threshold.

## 2. Natural model: inconsistent semidefinite quadratic proximal maps

Let \(H_i=H_i^T\succeq0\), \(b_i\in\operatorname{range}H_i\), \(p_i>0\), \(\sum_{i=1}^m p_i=1\), and \(\lambda>0\). Define

\[
f_i(z)=\tfrac12z^TH_i z-b_i^Tz,
\qquad
T_i(z)=\operatorname{prox}_{\lambda f_i}(z)
=A_i z+c_i,
\]
\[
A_i=(I+\lambda H_i)^{-1},\qquad c_i=\lambda A_i b_i.
\tag{2.1}
\]

Every \(f_i\) is a proper smooth convex function with a nonempty minimizer set. We do **not** require a common minimizer; the component minimizer sets may be inconsistent. Nor do we require any individual \(H_i\) to be positive definite, or the matrices to commute.

Set

\[
U=\bigcap_i\ker H_i,\qquad V=U^\perp.
\tag{2.2}
\]

The matrices preserve this orthogonal decomposition, act as identity on \(U\), and \(c_i\in V\). Thus \(z=(u,x)\) evolves as

\[
T_i(u,x)=(u,A_i|_V x+c_i).
\tag{2.3}
\]

The conserved variable here is derived from the native Hessians, not artificially selected after knowing the invariant law. A rank-deficient least-squares model supplies this structure directly. We assume \(V\ne\{0\}\); otherwise every map is identity and the stochastic invariant problem is trivial.

Define two native matrices on \(V\):

\[
B=\sum_i p_i A_i^TA_i\big|_V,\qquad
G=\sum_i p_i(I-A_i)^T(I-A_i)\big|_V,
\]
\[
c=\sqrt{\lambda_{\max}(B)},\qquad
\eta=\lambda_{\min}(G).
\tag{2.4}
\]

Then \(c<1\) and \(\eta>0\). Indeed \(0\prec A_i\preceq I\); equality \(\|A_i v\|=\|v\|\) holds precisely when \(H_i v=0\). If a unit \(v\in V\) attained \(v^TBv=1\), it would lie in every \(\ker H_i\), contradicting \(v\in V\). The same argument for \(G\) proves positivity. Compactness of the finite-dimensional unit sphere converts strictness in every direction into a positive uniform gap.

**Important limitation:** for a fixed finite family of these quadratics, removing the common nullspace already produces a linear gap. This class cannot demonstrate an essential non-power generalized gauge or genuine nonlinear RL. Slow nonuniform fiber parameters or infinite-dimensional spectral accumulation would change the assumptions and require a new theorem.

### The correlation-aware residual

Let \(K\) be the active-space kernel induced by (2.1), and let \(P\) preserve \(u\) while applying \(K\). Define

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal R_\nu(\mu)^2
=\int W_2\left(\mu_u,\sum_i p_i(A_i\cdot+c_i)_\#\mu_u\right)^2\,d\nu
=\mathsf W_\nu(\mu,\mu P)^2.}
\tag{2.5}
\]

It depends only on the current conditional law and the native proximal maps/probabilities, not an unknown invariant distribution or an infimum over invariant laws. For finite conditional supports, one first forms the finite one-step image mixture and solves an ordinary finite transport LP. For a scalar fiber it can be calculated from one-dimensional quantiles.

This is nevertheless the **actual conditional law displacement**. Its identification property is definitional once the Markov kernel is fixed. Naming it a proximal residual does not create a new stationarity theorem, and evaluating conditional laws from samples is a separate statistical/computational problem not solved here.

## 3. Complete positive theorem, with native constants

### Theorem A — non-Gibbs random-proximal conditional EB

Under Section 2, there is a unique \(\pi\in\mathscr P_2(V)\) invariant under \(K\). The entire finite-second-moment invariant family of \(P\) is

\[
\mathcal I_2=\{\nu\otimes\pi:\nu\in\mathscr P_2(U)\}.
\tag{3.1}
\]

For a fixed \(\nu\), put \(\pi_\nu=\nu\otimes\pi\) and \(E_\nu(\mu)=\mathsf W_\nu(\mu,\pi_\nu)\). Then, for every \(\mu\in\mathscr M_\nu\),

\[
\mathcal R_\nu(\mu)=0
\iff\mu P=\mu
\iff\mu=\pi_\nu,
\tag{3.2}
\]

\[
\boxed{(1-c)E_\nu(\mu)\le\mathcal R_\nu(\mu)\le(1+c)E_\nu(\mu),}
\tag{3.3}
\]

\[
\boxed{E_\nu(\mu P^k)\le c^kE_\nu(\mu),\qquad
\mathcal R_\nu(\mu P^k)\le c^k\mathcal R_\nu(\mu).}
\tag{3.4}
\]

Consequently the actual law orbit has finite conditional length and an a posteriori certificate:

\[
\sum_{k=0}^\infty\mathsf W_\nu(\mu P^{k+1},\mu P^k)
\le\frac{\mathcal R_\nu(\mu)}{1-c},
\quad
E_\nu(\mu P^k)\le\frac{c^k}{1-c}\mathcal R_\nu(\mu).
\tag{3.5}
\]

Thus \(\phi(t)=t/(1-c)\) is an explicitly verified linear metric-subregularity gauge. No optimality of the general matrix constant is claimed.

#### Proof

For any pair \(x,y\in V\), identical sampled indices give

\[
\mathbb E\|T_\xi x-T_\xi y\|^2=(x-y)^TB(x-y)\le c^2\|x-y\|^2.
\tag{3.6}
\]

Starting from an optimal coupling of \(\alpha,\beta\in\mathscr P_2(V)\), this gives \(W_2(\alpha K,\beta K)\le cW_2(\alpha,\beta)\). Finitely many affine shifts have finite second moments, so \(K\) maps \(\mathscr P_2(V)\) into itself. Completeness and Banach's theorem give the unique \(\pi\) and contraction to it.

Because \(u\) is preserved, invariance disintegrates into \(\mu_uK=\mu_u\) for \(\nu\)-almost every \(u\). The conditional measures have finite second moments almost everywhere. Uniqueness on \(V\) proves (3.1)–(3.2). Integrating the squared fiberwise contraction gives

\[
\mathsf W_\nu(\mu P,\eta P)\le c\mathsf W_\nu(\mu,\eta).
\tag{3.7}
\]

Triangle and reverse-triangle inequalities through \(\pi_\nu\) prove (3.3). Iterating (3.7), first with \(\eta=\pi_\nu\) and then with \(\eta=\mu P\), proves (3.4); geometric summation proves (3.5). These are actual law-space statements, not assertions of sample-path convergence or finite sample-path length. \(\square\)

### Ordinary joint Wasserstein interface

When \(\nu\in\mathscr P_2(U)\),

\[
d_{W_2}(\mu,\mathcal I_2)
\le W_2(\mu,\pi_\nu)
\le E_\nu(\mu)
\le\frac{\mathcal R_\nu(\mu)}{1-c}.
\tag{3.8}
\]

Hence this is a valid sufficient EB and convergence certificate for the original joint invariant-law problem. It is **not** a relative ordinary-Wasserstein rate normalized by the smaller ordinary distance to \(\mathcal I_2\), nor a necessity result in the ordinary topology.

### A noncommuting inconsistent example

Take \(V=\mathbb R^2\), \(p_i=1/3\), \(\lambda=1\), and

\[
n_1=e_1,\quad n_2=e_2,\quad n_3=(e_1+e_2)/\sqrt2,
\qquad f_i(x)=\tfrac12(n_i^Tx-d_i)^2,
\]
\[
(d_1,d_2,d_3)=(0,0,1).
\tag{3.9}
\]

The three minimizer lines have no common point. Every branch has Lipschitz constant one, and \(A_1A_3\ne A_3A_1\). Nevertheless,

\[
A_i=I-\tfrac12n_i n_i^T,\quad
\operatorname{spec}(B)=\{1/2,3/4\},\quad
\operatorname{spec}(G)=\{1/12,1/6\}.
\tag{3.10}
\]

Thus \(c=\sqrt3/2\), \(\eta=1/12\), and every conclusion of Theorem A holds. The stationary mean is \(m=n_3/2\); applying branch 1 changes that mean, proving immediately that the invariant law is not branch-1 invariant. Adding a shared null coordinate gives a nonisolated invariant family without changing these constants.

### Multivalued normal-cone endpoint

The same proof applies to \(T_i=P_{C_i}=J_{N_{C_i}}\) for nonempty affine subspaces \(C_i\), with

\[
T_i(z)=P_{L_i}z+c_i,\qquad L_i=C_i-C_i,\quad c_i\in L_i^\perp.
\]

Here \(N_{C_i}\) is a genuinely set-valued maximal monotone operator, while the resolvent remains single-valued. Take \(U=\cap_iL_i\), \(V=U^\perp\), and calculate (2.4) with \(A_i=P_{L_i}\). Equality conditions again give \(c<1\), \(\eta>0\), and now \(B=I-G\) on \(V\). The affine constraints may be inconsistent. This gives a real multivalued-operator example, **not** an exclusive advantage for nonlinear RL or multivalued resolvent selections.

## 4. Smooth scalar random prox: exact sharp constants and missed correlations

Choose \(0<a<1\), \(\kappa=(1-a)/(\lambda a)\), and let

\[
f_\varepsilon(u,x)=\tfrac\kappa2(x-\varepsilon)^2,
\qquad
T_\varepsilon(u,x)=(u,ax+(1-a)\varepsilon),
\quad\mathbb P(\varepsilon=\pm1)=1/2.
\tag{4.1}
\]

These are actual Euclidean proximal maps of smooth convex functions; their minimizer sets \(\{x=1\}\) and \(\{x=-1\}\) are inconsistent. Unlike coordinate reset, the update retains the previous active state whenever \(a>0\).

The active invariant law is

\[
\pi_a=\mathcal L\left((1-a)\sum_{j=0}^\infty a^j\varepsilon_j\right),
\quad
\mathbb E_{\pi_a}X=0,\quad
\operatorname{Var}_{\pi_a}X=\frac{1-a}{1+a}.
\tag{4.2}
\]

Absolute convergence of the series proves existence; separating its first term proves stationarity; the contraction proves uniqueness. At \(a=1/2\), \(\pi_a\) is uniform on \([-1,1]\): the two affine images of that uniform distribution are uniform on the two half intervals with equal mixture weights. No Gaussian or product-coordinate assumption is involved.

### Exact native residual evaluation

If \(F_u\) is the CDF of \(\mu_u\), its one-step CDF is

\[
F_u^+(x)=\tfrac12F_u\!\left(\frac{x-(1-a)}a\right)
+\tfrac12F_u\!\left(\frac{x+(1-a)}a\right).
\]

With generalized quantiles,

\[
\mathcal R_\nu(\mu)^2
=\int\!\int_0^1|F_u^{-1}(s)-(F_u^+)^{-1}(s)|^2\,ds\,d\nu(u).
\tag{4.3}
\]

This requires no formula for \(\pi_a\).

### Sharpness

Here \(c=a\). For any \(h\in L^2(\nu)\), consider the conditional translation

\[
\mu_u=(\mathrm{Id}+h(u))_\#\pi_a.
\]

Its update is exactly the conditional translation by \(a h(u)\). Since Wasserstein distance between a finite-second-moment law and its translate equals the translation length,

\[
E_\nu(\mu)^2=\int h^2d\nu,\quad
\mathcal R_\nu(\mu)^2=(1-a)^2\int h^2d\nu,\quad
E_\nu(\mu P^k)^2=a^{2k}\int h^2d\nu.
\tag{4.4}
\]

Therefore the optimal global linear EB coefficient is \(1/(1-a)\), and the optimal one-step and \(k\)-step contraction factors are \(a\) and \(a^k\). Scaling \(h\) proves the same sharpness in every full conditional-Wasserstein neighborhood of \(\pi_\nu\). If a compact state set is desired, take an invariant active interval \([-M,M]\) with \(M>1\) and sufficiently small translations.

### Exact old-discrepancy formula

Let \(\Psi\) be the original source discrepancy, with ordinary joint \(W_2\)-optimal couplings and the outer infimum over all invariant laws. Then

\[
\boxed{\Psi(\mu)=(1-a)W_2(\mu_X,\pi_a).}
\tag{4.5}
\]

Indeed the synchronous displacement difference is

\[
(z-T_\varepsilon z)-(z'-T_\varepsilon z')
=(0,(1-a)(x-x')).
\]

Every invariant target has active marginal \(\pi_a\), giving the lower bound in (4.5). For the upper bound choose the invariant target \(\delta_{u_0}\otimes\pi_a\). The conserved-coordinate cost is then independent of the coupling. An active-space optimal coupling, lifted with the source conditional law of \(u\), is also optimal for the full joint cost and attains the lower bound.

Thus the original discrepancy still observes only the active marginal, despite genuinely proximal memory and a non-two-point stationary distribution.

Take \(X\sim\pi_a\) and

\[
\mu_t=\mathcal L(tX,X),\qquad t>0.
\tag{4.6}
\]

Then \(\Psi(\mu_t)=0\), but \(\mu_t\) is correlated and is not invariant. Moreover \(\mu_t\to\delta_0\otimes\pi_a\) in ordinary joint \(W_2\). Its own conserved marginal is \(\nu_t\), and the corrected quantities are strictly positive:

\[
E_{\nu_t}(\mu_t)^2=\frac{2(1-a)}{1+a},\qquad
\mathcal R_{\nu_t}(\mu_t)^2=\frac{2(1-a)^2}{1+a}.
\tag{4.7}
\]

The conditional initial state is a Dirac mass; (4.7) follows from the variance in (4.2). In particular this false-zero phenomenon persists arbitrarily close to an invariant law in ordinary joint transport, and the conditional residual is not locally bounded by the original \(\Psi\).

At \(a=1/2\), a direct conditional quantile computation also gives

\[
(E_{\nu_t}^2,\mathcal R_{\nu_t}^2,E_{\nu_t,+}^2)
=(2/3,1/3,1/6).
\tag{4.8}
\]

The topology change is explicit: (4.6) approaches an invariant in ordinary joint transport while its conditional error remains constant. This is an extension of the previously established obstruction, not a new conceptual mechanism or a proof of the source discrepancy's necessity.

## 5. Minimal structural obstruction: stationarity is not branchwise stationarity

### Proposition B — branchwise residuals fail for inconsistent prox

Let \(T_i\) be finitely many firmly nonexpansive maps on a finite-dimensional Hilbert space, each with a nonempty fixed-point set, and let \(p_i>0\). For

\[
\mathcal L(\mu)^2=\sum_i p_i W_2(\mu,(T_i)_\#\mu)^2,
\tag{5.1}
\]

one has

\[
\mathcal L(\mu)=0
\iff\mu\left(\bigcap_i\operatorname{Fix}T_i\right)=1.
\tag{5.2}
\]

In particular, when the component fixed-point problem is inconsistent, \(\mathcal L\) is positive at **every** finite-second-moment law, including every true invariant law of the mixture kernel.

Proof. Zero in (5.1) means invariance under every individual map. Fix \(z_i\in\operatorname{Fix}T_i\). Firm nonexpansiveness yields

\[
\|T_ix-z_i\|^2+\|x-T_ix\|^2\le\|x-z_i\|^2.
\]

Integrating against a law invariant under \(T_i\), the first and rightmost integrals cancel. Thus \(x=T_ix\) almost surely. Intersecting finitely many probability-one sets proves one direction of (5.2); its converse is immediate. The same argument works for averaged maps with a positive fixed-point descent coefficient. \(\square\)

More generally, if \(d_i(\mu)\ge0\) has zero set exactly \(\{\mu:\mu P_i=\mu\}\), then the zero set of \(\sum_i p_i d_i(\mu)^2\) is the **intersection** of those branch-invariant sets. It need not equal the invariant set of \(\sum_i p_iP_i\). This set-theoretic issue precedes every error-bound or rate argument.

### Why the Gibbs repair does not transplant directly

In Gibbs, the grouped coordinate-update kernel \(Q_i\) includes its conditional random draw and individually preserves the same target \(\pi\). A sum of conditional discrepancies can therefore vanish at \(\pi\). The preceding obstruction concerns these **grouped kernels**, not the finest deterministic random-map labels: even Gibbs's individual deterministic reset maps generally do not preserve \(\pi\).

For the scalar prox (4.1), each deterministic branch has the sole invariant active law \(\delta_{\varepsilon}\), while the mixed invariant is \(\pi_a\). For \(a>0\), comparing the active conditional law to the Bernoulli innovation law is also wrong: the latter is not \(\pi_a\). Grouping all branches into one kernel restores zero identification, but leads exactly to (2.5), the full transition-displacement residual.

The actual independent-noise sample-path step at stationarity illustrates the required cancellation:

\[
\mathbb E_{X\sim\pi_a,\varepsilon}|X-T_\varepsilon X|^2
=\frac{2(1-a)^2}{1+a}>0,
\qquad
W_2(\pi_a,\pi_a K)=0.
\tag{5.3}
\]

Thus a valid stationary residual must permit re-coupling of law mass, or use centered aggregate stationarity identities. It cannot be obtained merely by declaring the positive physical step or a sum of local branch defects to be the invariant-law error.

## 6. Exact comparison with HLS, tensorization and Dobrushin

### 6.1 HLS domination is already verified after the conserved variable is frozen

On the active space, with the unique invariant law \(\pi\), the original synchronous discrepancy is

\[
\Psi_V(\alpha)^2
=\inf_{\gamma\in\operatorname{Opt}_{W_2}(\alpha,\pi)}
\int (x-y)^TG(x-y)\,d\gamma.
\tag{6.1}
\]

Consequently, directly from the native matrix \(G\),

\[
\Psi_V(\alpha)\ge\sqrt\eta W_2(\alpha,\pi)
\ge\frac{\sqrt\eta}{1+c}W_2(\alpha,\alpha K).
\tag{6.2}
\]

The last inequality uses the upper side of (3.3). This is exactly the source's required domination, with explicit constant \(q_{\rm HLS}=\sqrt\eta/(1+c)\). The first inequality already supplies the sharper direct source-residual EB \(W_2(\alpha,\pi)\le\Psi_V(\alpha)/\sqrt\eta\).

One may apply (6.2) fiberwise and integrate; one may not identify the resulting conditional discrepancy with the original global discrepancy. Formula (4.5) disproves such an identification. In the scalar example, \(\Psi_V=(1-a)E\), so the fixed-fiber domination is especially elementary.

This answers the novelty question precisely: the native calculation repairs the *scope of the coupling*, and (2.5) avoids evaluating an unknown invariant target, but the positive convergence mechanism is already inside the HLS contractive framework. It is not a theorem strictly beyond that framework.

### 6.2 Tensorization and Dobrushin: inapplicability is not novelty

The active invariant \(\pi\) of (3.9) is not given by native coordinate conditionals, and its sampled quadratic branches do not individually preserve it. Thus the product tensorization proof and Gaussian Gibbs Pythagorean argument from the earlier repair do not directly apply. No claim that \(\pi\) lacks every possible product representation is needed.

Dobrushin's Gibbs influence matrix concerns a compatible system of conditional-update kernels. Its established definition and coupling setting are explicit in Wang–Wu, [equations (2.2)–(2.3) and Section 3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1410.4329). Our branches do not form those Gibbs updates. However, the synchronous estimate (3.6) is a standard Wasserstein contraction coefficient and does not require Gibbs structure. Calling it a different matrix from Dobrushin's does not establish a new general principle.

## 7. RL interface and the remaining genuine research barrier

For (2.1), every reflected proximal map \(2T_i-I\) is affine with linear part \(2A_i-I\), whose spectrum lies in \([-1,1]\). For affine projections it is an isometry on the relevant linear directions. Thus the native RL geometry is the classical \(\gamma=1,L=1\) monotone slice. Fitting a subunit Holder upper bound on a bounded domain would not establish genuine non-Lipschitz RL.

The core manuscript's same-selected-graph-point and same-target requirements are essential. In the inconsistent model there is no common state solution \(s\) satisfying \(0\in\partial f_i(s)\) for every sampled branch. Its invariant distribution is not a point solution to which one may apply the deterministic RL step bound. Moreover \(2\mu P-\mu\) need not be a probability measure, so a probability-level reflected resolvent cannot be introduced through that expression.

Even after choosing a legitimate coupling, the law displacement (2.5) is not generally the synchronous two-chain defect consumed by a Hilbert energy identity; the upstream conditional-repair report gives an explicit failed energy inequality for such a substitution. Equation (5.3) establishes the separate distinction between a physical one-chain step and a law displacement. It does **not** say that the original two-chain synchronous defect is positive at stationarity: coupling a stationary law with itself identically makes that defect zero. A proposed extension must align its residual and its energy inequality on the same admissible coupling, rather than substituting one for the other.

The minimal worthwhile next barrier is therefore specific:

> For a natural inconsistent, genuinely non-Lipschitz/multivalued-proximal selection family that is not already mean-square contractive on a fixed quotient, derive from native data an aggregate stationarity residual that (i) allows branch cancellation, (ii) retains conserved correlations, and (iii) is quantitatively controlled by the **same** admissible coupling used in an RL energy or step estimate.

Neither assuming another invariant-law EB nor assuming a new full Wasserstein contraction resolves that barrier. One must also construct the actual randomized selection kernel and prove measurability, coverage, localization and moment control. These requirements identify what is missing; no theorem solving them is claimed here.
