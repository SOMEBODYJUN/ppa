## 4. Four-cycle: direct sharp proof without enumeration

Use physical coordinates

\[
G=(0,1,3,4),\qquad T:0\to1\to3\to4\to0,
\qquad P_p=(1-p)I+pT,\quad 0<p<1.
\]

Stationarity implies `T_# beta=beta`, hence the unique invariant law is

\[
\beta=(1/4,1/4,1/4,1/4).
\]

The nonidentity displacement vector is

\[
d=(-1,-2,-1,4),\qquad R_{ij}=p(d_i-d_j)^2.
\]

Let `eta` be the unique increasing quantile transport from `mu` to `beta`. Its uniqueness follows from strict Monge uncrossing for squared distance on distinct ordered real states. Define

\[
H=C-\frac{13}{p}R=C-13\big((d_i-d_j)^2\big)_{ij}
=\begin{pmatrix}
0&-12&9&-309\\
-12&0&-9&-459\\
9&-9&0&-324\\
-309&-459&-324&0
\end{pmatrix}.
\]

Its only positive entries are the physical edges `0 -> 3` and `3 -> 0`.

**Case 1: `eta(0,3)>0`.** The source-0 quantile interval then extends past target cumulative mass `1/2`. Consequently the complete target-1 interval `[1/4,1/2]` is supplied by source 0: `eta(0,1)=1/4`. Also `eta(0,3)<=1/4`. The reverse positive edge cannot occur because the plan is increasing. Therefore

\[
H\cdot\eta\le 9\eta(0,3)-12\eta(0,1)
\le 9/4-12/4<0.
\]

**Case 2: `eta(3,0)>0`.** The source-3 interval begins before target cumulative mass `1/4`. Thus every unit in the target-1 interval `[1/4,1/2]` comes from source 3 or source 4:

\[
\eta(3,1)+\eta(4,1)=1/4.
\]

Here `eta(3,0)<=1/4`, and the other positive edge is impossible. Hence

\[
\begin{aligned}
H\cdot\eta
&\le9\eta(3,0)-9\eta(3,1)-459\eta(4,1)\\
&\le 9\big[\eta(3,0)-\eta(3,1)-\eta(4,1)\big]\le0.
\end{aligned}
\]

**Case 3: neither positive edge occurs.** Every used entry of `H` is nonpositive.

All cases give

\[
\boxed{E(\mu)\le\frac{13}{p}\phi(\mu)\quad(\mu\in\Delta_4).}
\]

At

\[
\mu_*=(0,0,3/4,1/4),
\]

the increasing plan sends mass `1/4` from 3 to each of 0, 1, 3 and keeps mass `1/4` at 4. Thus

\[
E(\mu_*)=\frac94+\frac44=\frac{13}{4},
\qquad \phi(\mu_*)=\frac p4,
\]

so `13/p` is sharp. At `p=1/2` it is exactly `26`.

### Exact zeros and the essential OT constraint

The sharp bound already proves `phi(mu)=0 => mu=beta`. An independent proof is useful: zero residual permits only diagonal edges and the two collision edges `0 -> 3`, `3 -> 0`. State 1 has a unique displacement signature, forcing diagonal mass `eta(1,1)=1/4`. Either collision edge crosses this diagonal edge and can be uncrossed to reduce squared transport cost by `4 epsilon`, contradicting optimality. Hence only the diagonal remains.

Pointwise co-Lipschitz control fails because `d(0)=d(3)` while `0 != 3`.

Dropping input-OT optimality does change the zero set. For

\[
\mu^+=(1/2,1/4,0,1/4),
\]

the plan with mass `1/4` on `(0,0),(0,3),(1,1),(4,4)` has zero residual and cost `9/4`. The genuine increasing optimal plan has

\[
E(\mu^+)=5/4,\qquad \phi(\mu^+)=p/2>0.
\]

Thus the successful certificate really uses optimal-transport structure, rather than injectivity of pointwise residual signatures or unrestricted residual matching.

## 5. The 35-vertex computation: why it is exhaustive

In cumulative coordinates `0<=s_1<=s_2<=s_3<=1`, input quantile breakpoints are compared with target breakpoints `1/4,1/2,3/4`. Fixing their relative order makes every monotone-plan entry affine. There are `20=binom(6,3)` full-dimensional interleaving cells.

Every subdivision vertex has each `s_i` in `{0,1/4,1/2,3/4,1}`. Indeed, if a maximal block of equal cumulative coordinates lies strictly between two consecutive target breakpoints, this entire block can be perturbed in both directions while staying in the same closed cell. Such a point is not a vertex. Conversely, fixing every cumulative coordinate at a target breakpoint or endpoint gives a subdivision vertex.

Consequently the complete vertex list is

\[
\mu=z/4,\qquad z\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^{4},\quad \sum_i z_i=4,
\]

with `binom(7,3)=35` elements. Affineness of both costs on each cell makes vertex checking sufficient for the linear inequality `E<=26 phi`. This is an exact polyhedral reduction, not a numerical sampling argument. The direct proof in Section 4 eliminates reliance on the enumeration for the principal theorem.

The original `Fraction` script correctly enumerates those vertices, includes zero masses, constructs the unique increasing plan, identifies the sole zero-residual vertex, and obtains the stated sharp witness. It makes no unsupported floating-point claims.

## 6. Local constant and source-compatible parameters

### 6.1 Global sharpness must not be transferred to a local ray

The claim that `mu_s=(1-s)beta+s mu_*` preserves the sharp global ratio near `beta` is **FALSE**. This ray crosses transport cells. Its exact costs are

\[
E(\mu_s)=
\begin{cases}
9s/4,&0\le s\le1/2,\\
(17s-4)/4,&1/2\le s\le1,
\end{cases}
\]

\[
\phi(\mu_s)=
\begin{cases}
3ps/4,&0\le s\le1/2,\\
p(2-s)/4,&1/2\le s\le1.
\end{cases}
\]

Near `beta` its squared ratio is `3/p`, not `13/p`.

The correct sharp local squared error-bound constant is `4/p`. In a sufficiently small neighborhood of `beta`, a quantile interval can cross only one adjacent target boundary. Therefore only adjacent off-diagonal transport occurs, whose `C/R` ratios are

\[
1/p,\qquad4/p,\qquad1/(25p).
\]

It follows that `E<=4 phi/p` locally. Equality arbitrarily close to `beta` is witnessed by

\[
\mu_u=\beta+u(\delta_3-\delta_1)
=(1/4,1/4-u,1/4+u,1/4),\quad0<u\le1/4,
\]

for which the only off-diagonal transport is `3 -> 1` with mass `u`, so

\[
E(\mu_u)=4u,\qquad\phi(\mu_u)=pu.
\]

### 6.2 Exact expected almost-firm violation for every parameter

Put `tau=(1-alpha)/alpha>0`. The least violation required by the source expected almost-firm inequality equals the maximum over distinct state pairs of

\[
p\frac{|Tx-Ty|^2-|x-y|^2+\tau|(x-Tx)-(y-Ty)|^2}{|x-y|^2}.
\]

The six expressions after dividing by `p` are:

| Pair | Required expression |
|---|---:|
| `(0,1)` | `3+tau` |
| `(0,3)` | `0` |
| `(0,4)` | `(-15+25 tau)/16` |
| `(1,3)` | `(-3+tau)/4` |
| `(1,4)` | `4 tau` |
| `(3,4)` | `15+25 tau` |

The last is strictly the largest for every `tau>0`. Therefore

\[
\boxed{\epsilon_{\min}=p(15+25\tau).}
\]

For balanced `alpha=1/2`, this is `40p`. Consequently `p=1/100` gives `epsilon=2/5<1`, satisfying the source's structural expected almost-firm hypothesis. The original `p=1/2` example does **not** satisfy the source's `epsilon<1` requirement: even its limiting almost-nonexpansive violation is at least `15/2`. The small-`p` version corrects that scope issue without changing any OT argument.

### 6.3 No source-compatible general gauge, not merely no compatible linear constant

Any local error-bound gauge `rho` must, by the exact local witness, satisfy

\[
\rho(r)\ge\frac{2}{\sqrt p}\,r
\quad\text{for every sufficiently small }r>0.
\]

The source strict-rate relation

\[
\theta(d)^2=(1+\epsilon)d^2-\tau[\rho^{-1}(d)]^2<d^2
\]

requires, after taking `d=rho(r)`,

\[
\rho(r)<\sqrt{\tau/\epsilon}\,r
\le\sqrt{\frac{\tau}{p(15+25\tau)}}\,r
<\frac{1}{5\sqrt p}\,r.
\]

These bounds contradict each other. Thus no general source-compatible gauge can exist for any fixed `alpha` and valid native expected almost-firm violation. This proof does not use the false global-to-local ray argument.

The exclusion concerns the original discrepancy, original `W_2` metric and strict one-step scalar mechanism. It is not an exclusion of all alternative metrics, multi-step estimates or law-level Lyapunov certificates.

## 7. Actual geometric convergence and local one-step expansion

Let

\[
q_p=\sqrt{(1-p)^2+p^2}\in(0,1).
\]

The normal circulant transition matrix has eigenvalues

\[
1,\quad1-p+ip,\quad1-2p,\quad1-p-ip.
\]

Because `q_p^2-(1-2p)^2=2p(1-p)>0`, the Euclidean operator norm on the zero-sum subspace after `k` steps is at most `q_p^k`.

On the four-state space,

\[
\operatorname{TV}(\mu,\beta)\le E(\mu)
\le16\operatorname{TV}(\mu,\beta),
\qquad
\operatorname{TV}(v)\le\|v\|_2\le\sqrt2\operatorname{TV}(v)
\]

for zero-sum vectors `v`. Hence the following relative estimate holds for all initial laws and all `k>=0`:

\[
E(\mu P_p^k)\le16\sqrt2\,q_p^k E(\mu),
\]

\[
\boxed{W_2(\mu P_p^k,\beta)
\le4\,2^{1/4}q_p^{k/2}W_2(\mu,\beta).}
\]

The law orbit has finite `W_2` length by the triangle inequality and geometric summability. At `p=1/2`, an independent check gives the Dobrushin coefficient of `P_p^2` exactly `1/2`, providing another mixing proof.

There is nevertheless **no one-step distance contraction on any full neighborhood of `beta`**. Set

\[
\nu_u=\beta+u(\delta_3-\delta_4),\qquad0<u\le1/4.
\]

Then `E(nu_u)=u`. The successive cumulative deviations of `nu_u P_p` from `beta` are `-pu,-pu,(1-2p)u`; transport remains adjacent. Consequently

\[
E(\nu_uP_p)=
\begin{cases}
(1+3p)u,&0<p\le1/2,\\
(7p-1)u,&1/2\le p<1.
\end{cases}
\]

Both factors exceed one. Thus strict scalar one-step contraction fails for a direct dynamical reason, not just because a displayed numerical upper bound was crude. The chain is relatively R-linearly mixing; it is not uniformly Q-contracting in the given Wasserstein distance.
