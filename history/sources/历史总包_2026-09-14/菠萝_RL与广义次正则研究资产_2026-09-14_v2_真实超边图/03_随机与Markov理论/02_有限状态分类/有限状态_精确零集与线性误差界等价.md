# Finite-state classification for the original invariant transport discrepancy

Date: 2026-09-09. Companion to `MARKOV_GLOBAL_NOVELTY_AUDIT.md`.

**Mathematical status: PROVED below; independently checked by `finite_state_eb_check`. Priority status: PENDING.** The mechanism is classical finite-dimensional polyhedral geometry and Hoffman's bound; the exact application, quantifiers and sharp certificate are derived here. This is not a claim of a new general Hoffman theorem or of journal readiness.

**Mandatory prior-art deduction:** the general implication “bounded-domain continuous piecewise-affine residual with the right zeros admits an error bound” is already covered by piecewise-affine EB theory, notably Dolgopolik's Theorem 5. ## 1. Outcome and exact quantifiers

Fix a finite set of distinct states

\[
G=\{g_1,\ldots,g_N\}\subset\mathbb R^d,
\]

fixed maps \(T_a:G\to G\), \(a=1,\ldots,m\), and fixed probabilities \(p_a\ge0\), \(\sum_a p_a=1\). Write probability laws as column vectors in \(\Delta_N\), and define the row-stochastic transition matrix and stationary polytope by

\[
P_{ij}=\sum_a p_a\mathbf 1_{\{T_a g_i=g_j\}},\qquad
\mathcal I=\{\pi\in\Delta_N:P^\top\pi=\pi\}.
\]

The set \(\mathcal I\) is nonempty, compact and convex. Define the fixed matrices

\[
C_{ij}=\|g_i-g_j\|^2,
\qquad
R_{ij}=\sum_a p_a
\|(g_i-T_a g_i)-(g_j-T_a g_j)\|^2.
\tag{1.1}
\]

In particular \(C,R\ge0\), \(C_{ii}=R_{ii}=0\), and \(C_{ij}>0\) for \(i\ne j\). For a nonnegative matrix \(\eta\), let \(r\eta\) and \(c\eta\) denote its row and column sums. Put

\[
w(\mu,\pi)=\min\{C\mathbin\cdot\eta:\eta\ge0,\ r\eta=\mu,\ c\eta=\pi\},
\quad
\mathsf O_C(\mu,\pi)=\operatorname{argmin}\{C\mathbin\cdot\eta:\eta\ge0,\ r\eta=\mu,\ c\eta=\pi\}.
\]

Thus \(w=W_2^2\). The source discrepancy, without changing either minimization, is

\[
\phi(\mu):=\Psi(\mu)^2
=\inf_{\pi\in\mathcal I}\ \inf_{\eta\in\mathsf O_C(\mu,\pi)}R\mathbin\cdot\eta.
\tag{1.2}
\]

The distance function used in the error bound is

\[
E(\mu)=d_{W_2}(\mu,\mathcal I)^2
=\min_{\pi\in\mathcal I}w(\mu,\pi).
\tag{1.3}
\]

The \(\pi\) in (1.2) ranges over **all stationary laws**, not only those attaining (1.3). The inner coupling must be optimal for its own actual pair \((\mu,\pi)\). This distinction is preserved throughout the proof.

### Theorem 1 — finite-state exact-zero/linear-EB equivalence

For every fixed data set (1.1), the following are equivalent:

1. For every \(\mu\in\Delta_N\), \(\Psi(\mu)=0\) if and only if \(\mu\in\mathcal I\).
2. There exists one finite constant \(K\ge0\), depending on the fixed data but not on \(\mu\), such that
   \[
   d_{W_2}(\mu,\mathcal I)\le K\Psi(\mu)
   \qquad\text{for every }\mu\in\Delta_N.
   \tag{1.4}
   \]

Moreover, \(\phi=\Psi^2\) is a globally Lipschitz, continuous piecewise-affine function on \(\Delta_N\), with a finite polyhedral subdivision. The minimum in (1.2) is attained. Section 4 gives a finite vertex formula for the smallest squared constant \(K^2\); Section 5 supplies a separate Hoffman proof of existence.

There is **no convergence, irreducibility, uniqueness of the invariant law, paracontraction, or almost-firmness hypothesis** in this theorem. The maps, probabilities, geometry and residual realization are fixed before the existential quantifier on \(K\). A stochastic matrix alone does not determine \(R\).

The case \(N=1\) is immediate, with \(E=\phi=0\) and smallest \(K=0\). Below the same formulas apply, but one may assume \(N\ge2\).

## 2. Exact finite-polytope representation of the nested minimization

Consider the normalized Kantorovich dual polyhedron

\[
\mathcal D_C=\{(u,v)\in\mathbb R^{2N}:u_i+v_j\le C_{ij}\ \forall i,j,\ u_1=0\}.
\tag{2.1}
\]

It is nonempty. Its only potential lineality directions satisfy \(a_i+b_j=0\) for every \(i,j\); these have all \(a_i=a\), all \(b_j=-a\), and normalization gives \(a=0\). Thus \(\mathcal D_C\) is pointed and has finitely many vertices, denoted \((u^k,v^k)\), \(k=1,\ldots,L\).

For any \((\mu,\pi)\in\Delta_N^2\), finite linear-programming duality gives an attained finite dual optimum equal to \(w(\mu,\pi)\). Normalizing a dual optimizer does not change its objective because both marginals have total mass one. The nonempty optimal face of the pointed polyhedron (2.1) contains a vertex. Consequently an optimal dual vertex exists **also when some marginal coordinates vanish**.

For each vertex define its tight edge set

\[
\mathcal E_k=\{(i,j):u_i^k+v_j^k=C_{ij}\}
\]

and the compact, possibly empty, polytope

\[
\mathcal F_k=\left\{\eta\ge0:
\sum_{ij}\eta_{ij}=1,\ (P^\top-I_N)c\eta=0,
\ \eta_{ij}=0\text{ if }(i,j)\notin\mathcal E_k\right\}.
\tag{2.2}
\]

### Lemma 2 — exact union, not a relaxation

\[
\bigcup_{k=1}^L\mathcal F_k
=\{\eta\ge0:c\eta\in\mathcal I,\ \eta\in\mathsf O_C(r\eta,c\eta)\}.
\tag{2.3}
\]

Proof. If \(\eta\) belongs to the right-hand side, choose an optimal dual vertex for its own marginals. Primal–dual equality is

\[
0=C\mathbin\cdot\eta-u^k\mathbin\cdot r\eta-v^k\mathbin\cdot c\eta
=\sum_{ij}(C_{ij}-u_i^k-v_j^k)\eta_{ij}.
\]

Every summand is nonnegative. The support is therefore contained in \(\mathcal E_k\), proving membership in \(\mathcal F_k\). Conversely, membership in \(\mathcal F_k\) makes the displayed primal–dual gap zero for the actual marginals \(r\eta,c\eta\). Weak duality then proves optimality for that pair. No nearest-stationary-law condition has been introduced. \(\square\)

Define the extended-valued branch functions

\[
f_k(\mu)=\min\{R\mathbin\cdot\eta:\eta\in\mathcal F_k,\ r\eta=\mu\},
\quad \min\varnothing:=+\infty.
\]

Lemma 2 gives the exact identity

\[
\phi(\mu)=\min_{1\le k\le L}f_k(\mu).
\tag{2.4}
\]

For every \(\mu\), at least one fiber in (2.4) is nonempty: choose any stationary \(\pi\) and an optimal transport plan. All nonempty fibers are compact, and there are finitely many. This proves attainment of (1.2) directly, without assuming continuity of \(\Psi\).

The epigraph of \(f_k\) is the linear projection of

\[
\{(\mu,t,\eta):\eta\in\mathcal F_k,\ r\eta=\mu,\ R\mathbin\cdot\eta\le t\}.
\]

It is a polyhedron; its finite domain is the polytope \(r\mathcal F_k\). Thus each branch is an extended-valued convex polyhedral function. The original \(\phi\) is a finite lower envelope of these branches, not generally one convex LP value function. This establishes finite polyhedral structure; Section 6 proves the continuity needed for the usual continuous piecewise-affine terminology.

## 3. Two facts about squared distance to the stationary polytope

First, \(E(\mu)=0\) if and only if \(\mu\in\mathcal I\), since \(W_2\) is a genuine metric and \(\mathcal I\) is compact. Second, \(E\) is convex on the probability simplex.

For the latter, let \(\eta_1,\eta_2\) attain (1.3) for \(\mu_1,\mu_2\). The column marginal of \(t\eta_1+(1-t)\eta_2\) is still stationary by convexity of \(\mathcal I\). Its row marginal is \(t\mu_1+(1-t)\mu_2\). Its transport cost proves

\[
E(t\mu_1+(1-t)\mu_2)\le tE(\mu_1)+(1-t)E(\mu_2).
\tag{3.1}
\]

Also \(\mathcal I\subseteq\phi^{-1}(0)\) always: for \(\mu\in\mathcal I\), choose \(\pi=\mu\) and the diagonal plan, which has both \(C\)-cost and \(R\)-cost zero.

## 4. Vertex proof and the sharp finite constant

Let \(\mathcal V\) be the union of the vertex sets of all nonempty \(\mathcal F_k\). This is a finite set of coupling matrices, independent of \(\mu\). Assume exact zeros. If \(v\in\mathcal V\) and \(R\mathbin\cdot v=0\), then (2.3) gives \(\phi(rv)=0\), so

\[
E(rv)=0.
\tag{4.1}
\]

Set

\[
B_*=
\max_{\substack{v\in\mathcal V\\R\mathbin\cdot v>0}}
\frac{E(rv)}{R\mathbin\cdot v},
\qquad \max\varnothing:=0.
\tag{4.2}
\]

This number is finite. For any \(\mu\), choose a minimizing plan \(\eta\in\mathcal F_k\) from (2.4) and express it as a convex combination of vertices of that polytope: \(\eta=\sum_v\lambda_vv\). Convexity (3.1), (4.1), and (4.2) yield

\[
E(\mu)=E\left(\sum_v\lambda_vrv\right)
\le\sum_v\lambda_vE(rv)
\le B_*\sum_v\lambda_v(R\mathbin\cdot v)
=B_*\phi(\mu).
\tag{4.3}
\]

Taking square roots proves (1.4) with \(K=\sqrt{B_*}\). Conversely, (1.4) implies that every zero of \(\Psi\) has zero distance to \(\mathcal I\); together with the diagonal argument this gives exact zeros.

The constant in (4.2) is not only an upper bound. If some \(B\ge0\) satisfies \(E(\mu)\le B\phi(\mu)\) for all \(\mu\), then every positive-cost vertex satisfies

\[
E(rv)\le B\phi(rv)\le B(R\mathbin\cdot v),
\]

because \(v\) is an admissible plan for the nested residual problem at \(rv\). Hence \(B\ge B_*\). Thus \(B_*\) is the **smallest squared error-bound constant** and \(\sqrt{B_*}\) is the smallest \(K\).

This proof does not use continuity of \(\Psi\), nor any dynamical property of \(P\). Its essential ingredients are finitely many compact polytopes, a nonnegative linear residual on each, and a convex nonnegative target function that vanishes on their zero-residual vertices.

## 5. Independent Hoffman proof, including branches without zero faces

We use the classical fixed-matrix Hoffman bound: for a nonempty linear-inequality/equality solution set, distance to the set is bounded by a constant times constraint violation, with the constant depending on the coefficient matrices, not on a consistent right-hand side. Equalities are represented by two inequalities; all finite-dimensional norms are allowed. This is the classical result, not a claimed new ingredient. [Hoffman, 1952, pp. 263–265](https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/jres/049/4/v49.n04.a05.pdf).

Let \(D=\operatorname{diam}G\). For each nonempty \(\mathcal F_k\), set

\[
\mathcal Z_k=\{\eta\in\mathcal F_k:R\mathbin\cdot\eta=0\}.
\]

If \(\mathcal Z_k\ne\varnothing\), apply Hoffman to the constraints defining \(\mathcal Z_k\). A point \(\eta\in\mathcal F_k\) already satisfies all constraints except possibly the new equality \(R\mathbin\cdot\eta=0\). Since this scalar is nonnegative, its equality violation is exactly \(R\mathbin\cdot\eta\) in the corresponding one-sided encoding. There is \(H_k<\infty\) such that

\[
\operatorname{dist}_1(\eta,\mathcal Z_k)\le H_kR\mathbin\cdot\eta
\qquad(\eta\in\mathcal F_k).
\tag{5.1}
\]

Choose a nearest \(\eta_0\in\mathcal Z_k\); compactness gives attainment. Exact zeros and Lemma 2 imply \(r\eta_0\in\mathcal I\). The standard maximal-coupling bound on a finite metric space and contraction of row marginalization in \(\ell^1\) give

\[
\begin{aligned}
E(r\eta)
&\le W_2^2(r\eta,r\eta_0)
\le D^2\operatorname{TV}(r\eta,r\eta_0)\\
&=\frac{D^2}{2}\|r\eta-r\eta_0\|_1
\le\frac{D^2}{2}\|\eta-\eta_0\|_1
\le\frac{D^2H_k}{2}R\mathbin\cdot\eta.
\end{aligned}
\tag{5.2}
\]

If \(\mathcal Z_k=\varnothing\), one must **not** apply Hoffman to an inconsistent system. Instead compactness and nonnegativity give

\[
m_k=\min_{\eta\in\mathcal F_k}R\mathbin\cdot\eta>0.
\]

Since \(E\le D^2\), on this branch

\[
E(r\eta)\le (D^2/m_k)R\mathbin\cdot\eta.
\tag{5.3}
\]

Take the maximum over the finitely many nonempty branches of \(D^2H_k/2\) or \(D^2/m_k\), as appropriate, and apply it to an attaining plan in (2.4). This proves the same global linear \(W_2\)-EB, with a possibly non-sharp constant. Empty branches are omitted.

## 6. Proven continuity and continuous piecewise-affine structure

The main EB proof above did not assume continuity. In this fixed finite setting continuity is nevertheless true and can be proved uniformly, rather than guessed from a nested minimization.

Write \(b=(\mu,\pi)\), \(A\eta=(r\eta,c\eta)\), \(w(b)=w(\mu,\pi)\). The finite cost satisfies \(0\le C_{ij}\le D^2\), and

\[
|w(b)-w(b')|\le\frac{D^2}{2}\|b-b'\|_1
\qquad(b,b'\in\Delta_N^2).
\tag{6.1}
\]

For completeness, start from an optimal old coupling. Couple its first marginal maximally to the new first marginal and its second marginal maximally to the new second marginal; apply the two resulting stochastic kernels to the two coordinates. The new plan has the required new marginals. The chance either coordinate changes is at most the sum of the two total-variation distances; on that event the cost changes by at most \(D^2\). This gives one direction of (6.1), and interchanging old/new gives the other. Zero-mass conditional kernels may be defined arbitrarily on the unused states.

The optimal-coupling set can be written using one fixed constraint matrix:

\[
\mathsf S(b)=\{\eta\ge0:A\eta=b,\ C\mathbin\cdot\eta=w(b)\}.
\]

Applying the fixed-matrix Hoffman inequality with target \(\mathsf S(b')\) to \(\eta\in\mathsf S(b)\) yields a constant \(H<\infty\), independent of the marginals, such that

\[
\operatorname{dist}_1(\eta,\mathsf S(b'))
\le H\big(\|b-b'\|_1+|w(b)-w(b')|\big)
\le H(1+D^2/2)\|b-b'\|_1.
\tag{6.2}
\]

Both optimal-coupling sets are nonempty and compact. Applying (6.2) in both directions bounds their \(\ell^1\) Hausdorff distance. Consequently

\[
h(\mu,\pi)=\min_{\eta\in\mathsf O_C(\mu,\pi)}R\mathbin\cdot\eta
\]

is globally Lipschitz on \(\Delta_N^2\), with, for example, constant

\[
L_h=\|R\|_\infty H(1+D^2/2).
\]

Minimizing over the **same fixed compact set** \(\pi\in\mathcal I\) therefore preserves the Lipschitz constant in the first argument:

\[
|\phi(\mu)-\phi(\mu')|\le L_h\|\mu-\mu'\|_1.
\tag{6.3}
\]

Combining this continuity with (2.4) proves the asserted finite continuous piecewise-affine representation. More explicitly, each branch is the maximum of finitely many affine functions on its polyhedral domain. Subdivide the simplex using all domain-facet hyperplanes and all equalities between those affine functions. On each relatively open cell the feasible branch list and all affine orderings are fixed, so the lower envelope equals one affine function. Continuity extends this expression to each cell closure; finitely many such closures cover \(\Delta_N\). The function need not be convex.

Also

\[
|\Psi(\mu)-\Psi(\mu')|\le\sqrt{L_h}\,\|\mu-\mu'\|_1^{1/2}.
\]

This regularity statement holds regardless of exact-zero identification. It does not by itself imply the error bound to \(\mathcal I\).

## 7. Finite exact-zero certificates and applicability boundaries

Since \(R\ge0\), every nonempty \(\mathcal Z_k\) is a face of \(\mathcal F_k\). Exact-zero identification is equivalent to

\[
r\mathcal Z_k\subseteq\mathcal I\quad\text{for every }k,
\tag{7.1}
\]

or equivalently, to stationarity of the row marginal of every zero-residual vertex:

\[
(P^\top-I_N)rv=0
\quad\text{for every }v\in\mathcal V\text{ with }R\mathbin\cdot v=0.
\tag{7.2}
\]

Necessity follows from admissibility in the original residual minimization. For sufficiency, an attaining zero-residual plan belongs to a zero face and decomposes into its zero-residual vertices; their stationary row marginals mix to a stationary law.

Thus (7.1) can be checked by finite LPs: on every nonempty \(\mathcal Z_k\), maximize both signs of every coordinate of \((P^\top-I_N)r\eta\); all maxima must be zero. Formula (4.2) then gives a finite sharp certificate. This is a finite verification procedure, **not an assertion of polynomial-time or practical large-state complexity**. With rational input matrices, its LP values and the squared sharp constant can be represented exactly as rationals.

The argument actually needs only a fixed nonnegative residual matrix \(R\) with diagonal zero and a fixed compact convex polyhedral target \(\mathcal J\subseteq\Delta_N\), replacing \(\mathcal I\) throughout. For the requested source application these conditions follow from (1.1) and stationarity. Finiteness of the random-map list is more than necessary: on finite \(G\), any fixed random self-map realization induces a finite residual matrix, and there are only finitely many distinct self-maps of \(G\).

The proof does **not** automatically apply to any of the following changes:

- Replacing \(G\) by an infinite compact set, even if every individual block is finite.
- Letting \(P\), the maps, probabilities, geometry or residual realization vary while demanding one uniform constant.
- Replacing the outer stationary polytope by a nonlinear or nonconvex family without separately rechecking the convex-distance step.
- Using a residual matrix with negative entries; the source squared residual is nonnegative, which is essential here.
- Claiming a rate-compatible almost-firm gauge solely from existence of some linear EB: the allowable constant in a specified scalar rate formula remains a separate quantitative condition.

## 8. How this narrows the public open question

| Setting, with the original residual and metric fixed | What is now established | What is not established |
|---|---|---|
| Fixed finite \(G\), no exact-zero hypothesis | \(\Psi^2\) is continuous piecewise affine; false zeros may still occur | A bound to the invariant set without identification |
| Fixed finite \(G\), exact zeros | Global linear \(W_2\)-EB, independently of mixing; sharp finite certificate | A uniform constant over changing systems; convergence from EB alone |
| Infinite compact Euclidean example in the project, exact zeros | Project audit proves global Q/relative R rates but no positive-Hölder EB | Failure of every arbitrary general gauge; compactness supplies some gauge |
| Fixed valid source rate formula | The infinite example excludes its rate-compatible gauges by the separate scalar argument | Exclusion of all possible metrics, residual redesigns or dynamical certificates |

In particular, a counterexample satisfying exact zeros and failing **every** positive-Hölder error bound cannot live on one fixed finite state space with this fixed source definition. The project construction's infinitely many shrinking blocks are therefore structural, not merely decorative. Finite truncations may each possess an EB while their best constants diverge with scale; Theorem 1 asserts no uniformity across those truncations.

The finite theorem also separates identification from convergence. For example, take \(G=\{0,1\}\), deterministic \(T(x)=1-x\), and \(\beta=(1/2,1/2)\). Then \(\mathcal I=\{\beta\}\), \(C_{01}=C_{10}=1\), \(R_{01}=R_{10}=4\), and for \(\mu=(a,1-a)\),

\[
E(\mu)=|a-1/2|,\qquad\phi(\mu)=4|a-1/2|.
\]

Exact zeros and the sharp EB constant \(K=1/2\) hold, but every nonstationary law alternates and fails to converge. Any convergence theorem still needs its dynamical hypotheses.

The public-source necessity questions must be quoted with their qualifications: the 2025 partially separable optimization paper discusses non-paracontractions and an appropriate metric, while the 2026 X-FEL article works with specific exact-zero and subregularity assumptions. Our positive finite classification and negative infinite example address the **fixed original synchronous discrepancy and ordinary \(W_2\)**. They do not settle a question allowing one to redesign the metric or residual. ## 10. Strictly stronger than pointwise displacement injectivity: two positive examples

The obvious pointwise sufficient condition

\[
R_{ij}\ge c\,C_{ij}\quad\forall i,j\quad\text{for some }c>0
\tag{10.1}
\]

implies an EB immediately, but is not necessary. The three-state example below shows this using the invariant support alone. The four-state lazy example is stronger: dropping the input-OT constraint actually creates false zeros, so the OT-tight-edge condition is essential to the successful certificate.

### 10.1 A minimal nontrivial support-based example

Take \(G=\{0,1,2\}\), \(T(0)=0,T(1)=1,T(2)=0\), with this single map selected surely. Then

\[
\mathcal I=\{(a,1-a,0):a\in[0,1]\},\qquad
(g-Tg)_{g\in G}=(0,0,2).
\]

The displacement signatures at states 0 and 1 collide, so (10.1) fails. Write \(\mu=(a,b,c)\). For **every** invariant \(\pi\) and **every** coupling from \(\mu\) to \(\pi\), residual cost is \(4c\): rows 0 and 1 incur zero, while all mass in row 2 must reach 0 or 1 and incurs cost 4 per unit. Therefore the original nested problem gives exactly

\[
\Psi(\mu)^2=4c.
\]

Every unit of mass at 2 must move at least squared distance 1 to reach an invariant law. The plan that keeps masses at 0 and 1 and moves all mass from 2 to 1 attains that lower bound, with stationary target \((a,b+c,0)\). Thus

\[
d_{W_2}(\mu,\mathcal I)^2=c,\qquad K_{\mathrm{sharp}}=1/2.
\]

This checks the outer invariant-law minimization exactly; the nearest invariant law need not equal the actual limit \(\mu T_\#=(a+c,b,0)\). The OT constraint is not needed to explain this particular success. A two-state displacement collision for random self-maps forces both states to be fixed by every positive-probability map, so all laws are invariant; thus three states are minimal for a nontrivial proper-invariant-set example of this simple kind.

### 10.2 A genuinely OT-dependent, geometrically convergent four-state example

Take

\[
G=(0,1,3,4),\qquad
T:0\mapsto1\mapsto3\mapsto4\mapsto0,
\qquad
\mathcal P_p=(1-p)\operatorname{Id}_\#+pT_\#,
\qquad0<p<1.
\tag{10.2}
\]

The random maps are exactly \(\operatorname{Id}\) and \(T\), selected with probabilities \(1-p\) and \(p\). The kernel is irreducible and aperiodic, with the unique stationary law

\[
\pi=(1/4,1/4,1/4,1/4).
\]

The nontrivial-map displacement vector and synchronous residual cost are

\[
d=(-1,-2,-1,4),\qquad R_{ij}=p(d_i-d_j)^2.
\tag{10.3}
\]

The signatures at the distinct states 0 and 3 coincide, so (10.1) and global pointwise injectivity both fail.

#### Exact zeros require input-OT optimality

Suppose a source-admissible coupling \(\eta\in\mathsf O_C(\mu,\pi)\) has zero residual cost. By (10.3), its only possible off-diagonal entries are the edges \(0\to3\) and \(3\to0\). Since state 1 has a unique displacement signature and the target assigns it mass \(1/4\), necessarily \(\eta_{1,1}=1/4\), where subscripts here denote the actual state coordinates.

If \(\eta_{0,3}>0\), take any \(0<\varepsilon\le\min(\eta_{0,3},1/4)\). Replace mass \(\varepsilon\) on the edges \((0,3),(1,1)\) by the same mass on \((0,1),(1,3)\). Marginals are unchanged and transport cost decreases by

\[
\varepsilon\bigl((3-0)^2+0-(1-0)^2-(3-1)^2\bigr)=4\varepsilon>0,
\]

contradicting input-OT optimality. The edge \(3\to0\), paired with \(1\to1\), is excluded by the identical calculation using \((3,1),(1,0)\). Hence the zero-residual plan is diagonal and \(\mu=\pi\). Conversely, \(\mu=\pi\) admits the diagonal zero plan. Attainment established in Section 2 turns this plan-level argument into

\[
\Psi^{-1}(0)=\{\pi\}.
\tag{10.4}
\]

For contrast, let

\[
\widetilde\Psi(\mu)^2
=\min_{\eta\in\Pi(\mu,\pi)}R\mathbin\cdot\eta
\]

be the relaxation obtained by dropping input-OT optimality. At \(\mu^+=(1/2,1/4,0,1/4)\), the plan putting mass \(1/4\) on each of \((0,0),(0,3),(1,1),(4,4)\) has residual cost zero, so \(\widetilde\Psi(\mu^+)=0\) although \(\mu^+\ne\pi\). Its transport cost is \(9/4\), whereas the actual unique monotone OT plan has transport cost \(5/4\) and source residual cost \(p/2\). Thus

\[
\widetilde\Psi(\mu^+)=0<\Psi(\mu^+)=\sqrt{p/2}.
\tag{10.5}
\]

This establishes strict superiority of the full finite OT-polyhedral certificate over (10.1), and also over a certificate that ignores input-OT optimality. It is not a claim that every target-aware pointwise condition is weaker than our test.

#### Sharp global constant: hand proof and exhaustive exact verification

For this example,

\[
\boxed{\ d_{W_2}(\mu,\pi)\le\sqrt{13/p}\,\Psi(\mu)
\quad\forall\mu\in\Delta_4,\qquad K_{\mathrm{sharp}}=\sqrt{13/p}.\ }
\tag{10.6}
\]

For strictly ordered one-dimensional states, squared-distance costs are strictly Monge:

\[
C_{i\ell}+C_{kj}-C_{ij}-C_{k\ell}
=2(g_k-g_i)(g_\ell-g_j)>0\quad(i<k,\ j<\ell).
\]

Thus an optimal coupling cannot cross and is the unique monotone coupling.

There is a short hand proof of the upper bound. Set \(H=C-(13/p)R\), so entrywise \(H_{ij}=C_{ij}-13(d_i-d_j)^2\). In coordinate order \((0,1,3,4)\),

\[
H=\begin{pmatrix}
0&-12&9&-309\\
-12&0&-9&-459\\
9&-9&0&-324\\
-309&-459&-324&0
\end{pmatrix}.
\]

The only positive entries are the edges \(0\to3\) and \(3\to0\). Monotonicity prevents both from occurring in one optimal plan. If \(0\to3\) occurs, all target mass at state 1 must come from state 0; its contribution is \(-12/4\), whereas the positive edge contributes at most \(9/4\). If \(3\to0\) occurs, all target mass at state 1 must come from state 3 or state 4; its contribution is at most \(-9/4\), again dominating the positive edge, whose mass is at most \(1/4\). If neither occurs, all contributions are nonpositive. Thus \(H\mathbin\cdot\eta\le0\) for every optimal plan, proving the upper bound in (10.6).

For an independent exhaustive arithmetic verification, let \(s_1,s_2,s_3\) be the three cumulative source masses. The domain is \(0\le s_1\le s_2\le s_3\le1\), and target cumulative masses are \(1/4,1/2,3/4\). Partition the domain at every hyperplane \(s_i=j/4\), \(j=0,\ldots,4\). #### Actual Markov convergence, with all-initial-law relative quantifiers

Let \(Q\) be the four-cycle permutation matrix. The transition matrix is \(P_p=(1-p)I_4+pQ\), a normal circulant matrix. Its eigenvalues are \(1,1-p+ip,1-2p,1-p-ip\). Define \(\gamma_p=\sqrt{(1-p)^2+p^2}<1\). On the zero-sum subspace,

\[
\|(P_p^\top)^kv\|_2\le\gamma_p^k\|v\|_2.
\]

For two probability vectors on this four-state space, squared Wasserstein distance lies between their total-variation distance and 16 times that distance, because the smallest nonzero squared state distance is 1 and the diameter squared is 16. Also, for zero-sum vectors in \(\mathbb R^4\),

\[
\operatorname{TV}(v)=\tfrac12\|v\|_1\le\|v\|_2
\le\sqrt2\operatorname{TV}(v).
\]

Combining these estimates yields the explicit uniform relative geometric bound

\[
W_2^2(\mu\mathcal P_p^k,\pi)
\le16\sqrt2\,\gamma_p^k W_2^2(\mu,\pi),
\qquad \forall\mu\in\Delta_4,\quad\forall k\ge0,
\]

or

\[
W_2(\mu\mathcal P_p^k,\pi)
\le4\,2^{1/4}\,\gamma_p^{k/2}W_2(\mu,\pi).
\tag{10.7}
\]

The prefactor is a convenient bound, not a claimed sharp mixing constant. At \(p=1/2\), \(\gamma_p=2^{-1/2}\), recovering the benchmark's \(2^{-k/4}\) Wasserstein rate. This example therefore has actual all-law geometric convergence, collision of the pointwise displacement signatures, and a sharp source-specific linear EB certified by the OT constraints.

#### Small-update almost-firmness and failure of the specific scalar-rate compatibility condition

For balanced \(\alpha=1/2\), the expected almost-firm inequality is

\[
\mathbb E|T_\xi x-T_\xi y|^2+
\mathbb E|(x-T_\xi x)-(y-T_\xi y)|^2
\le(1+\epsilon)|x-y|^2.
\]

Its exact least nonnegative violation for this realization is \(\epsilon=40p\): the pair \((3,4)\) has squared output difference 16 and squared displacement difference 25 under \(T\), and all other pairs have a smaller ratio. Thus \(p=1/100\) gives \(\epsilon=2/5<1\). In contrast, the \(p=1/2\) arithmetic benchmark has \(\epsilon=20\) and must not be presented as satisfying a source hypothesis that requires \(\epsilon<1\).

Existence of the linear EB still does not make the source's scalar rate relation contractive. For any \(\tau=(1-\alpha)/\alpha>0\), the same pair \((3,4)\) forces every valid **global pointwise expected almost-firm** violation to satisfy

\[
\epsilon\ge p(15+25\tau).
\tag{10.8}
\]

For a local gauge obstruction, take the adjacent-mass perturbations

\[
\mu_u=(1/4,1/4-u,1/4+u,1/4),\qquad0<u\le1/4.
\]

Their unique OT plan keeps all common mass and moves mass \(u\) from state 3 to state 1. Hence

\[
E(\mu_u)=4u,\qquad\phi(\mu_u)=pu.
\]

Any increasing local EB gauge \(\rho\) with \(d_{W_2}(\mu,\pi)\le\rho(\Psi(\mu))\) must therefore satisfy

\[
\rho(r)\ge2r/\sqrt p\qquad(0<r\le\sqrt p/2)
\]

after restricting to the relevant neighborhood if necessary. But the specified source formula

\[
\theta(t)^2=(1+\epsilon)t^2-\tau[\rho^{-1}(t)]^2<t^2
\]

requires \(\rho(r)<\sqrt{\tau/\epsilon}\,r\) locally. By (10.8),

\[
\sqrt{\tau/\epsilon}\le
\frac1{\sqrt p}\sqrt{\frac{\tau}{15+25\tau}}
<\frac1{5\sqrt p},
\]

contradicting the required local lower envelope. Thus this example has exact zeros, a global linear EB and all-law geometric convergence, but **no local EB gauge compatible with that scalar rate formula and any valid global pointwise expected almost-firm parameters**. This is not failure of all EB gauges: (10.6) gives one. It also does not exclude redesigned metrics, residuals, or independently derived measure-level dissipation inequalities.

The example already gives a finite obstruction to this specific rate-compatibility requirement. Infinitely many compact blocks remain necessary for the stronger no-positive-Hölder phenomenon.
