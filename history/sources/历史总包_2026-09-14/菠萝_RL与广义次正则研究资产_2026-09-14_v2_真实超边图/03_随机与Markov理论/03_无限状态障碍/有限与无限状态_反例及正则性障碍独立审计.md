The proposed four-state chain has a sharp, global one-step squared-Wasserstein distance contraction to its invariant set, yet its exact source-defined synchronous invariant transport discrepancy vanishes at noninvariant measures arbitrarily close to an invariant point mass. ### 1.4 Exact discrepancy retained in this audit

For a law \(\mu\) on the state set and \(\mathcal I=\operatorname{inv}\mathcal P\), the object audited is exactly

\[
\Psi(\mu)^2=
\inf_{\pi\in\mathcal I}\inf_{\eta\in C_*(\mu,\pi)}
\int \mathbb E\left| (x-T_\xi x)-(y-T_\xi y)\right|^2\,d\eta(x,y),
\]

where \(C_*\) denotes *Wasserstein-cost-optimal* couplings of the fixed pair \((\mu,\pi)\). This is not minimization over arbitrary couplings, not a distance between separately sampled noises, and not the displacement \(W_2(\mu,\mu\mathcal P)\).

## 2. Exact finite-state model

Put

\[
G=\{0,\tfrac12,1,2\},\qquad T(x)=|x-1|,
\qquad \mathcal P=\tfrac78 I+\tfrac18 T_\#.
\]

The same random map is applied to both members of a discrepancy coupling: choose identity with probability \(7/8\), and choose \(T\) with probability \(1/8\). The transition matrix, acting on row laws, is

\[
P=\begin{pmatrix}
7/8&0&1/8&0\\
0&1&0&0\\
1/8&0&7/8&0\\
0&0&1/8&7/8
\end{pmatrix}.
\]

Thus

\[
(a,b,c,d)P=(\tfrac78a+\tfrac18c,\ b,
\tfrac18a+\tfrac78c+\tfrac18d,\ \tfrac78d).
\]

Stationarity first forces \(d=0\), then \(a=c\), and normalization supplies the entire invariant set:

\[
\boxed{\mathcal I=\{(r,1-2r,r,0):0\le r\le1/2\}.}
\]

The common fixed-point set of the two state maps is \(\{1/2\}\). Invariant measures need not be supported there: the closed two-state class \(\{0,1\}\) supports its stationary symmetric law. The random functions are continuous restrictions of globally 1-Lipschitz real maps. All measurability, compactness, second-moment and Feller requirements hold.

### Expected almost-firmness

Let \(R(x)=x-Tx\), so \(R\) is 2-Lipschitz. For all real \(x,y\),

\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb E|T_\xi x-T_\xi y|^2
&=\tfrac78|x-y|^2+\tfrac18|Tx-Ty|^2
\le |x-y|^2,\\
\mathbb E|R_\xi(x)-R_\xi(y)|^2
&=\tfrac18|R(x)-R(y)|^2\le\tfrac12|x-y|^2.
\end{aligned}
\]

Adding proves the source expected almost-firm inequality with
\(\alpha=1/2\) and \(\epsilon=1/2<1\). The pair \((0,1)\) attains the combined factor \(3/2\), so this is not a numerically assumed bound. The condition even holds on the entire real line, although only the finite invariant domain is needed.

## 3. Exact Wasserstein distance and sharp contraction

Use \(\mu=(a,b,c,d)^T\) as a column only when evaluating the linear forms below, and set

\[
\begin{aligned}
f_A&=(1,0,-1,3)/4,& f_B&=(-1,0,1,5)/4,\\
f_C&=(-3,-2,1,7)/4,& f_D&=(-5,-4,-1,9)/4.
\end{aligned}
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{E(\mu):=d_{W_2}(\mu,\mathcal I)^2
=\max\{f_A\mu,f_B\mu,f_C\mu,f_D\mu\}.}
\tag{A1}
\]

### Proof of the distance formula

An admissible transport has arbitrary target mass at \(0,1/2,1\), except that the arrivals at \(0\) and \(1\) must be equal. Introduce one Lagrange multiplier \(\lambda\) for this balance. Finite LP duality gives

\[
E(\mu)=\sup_{\lambda\in\mathbb R}\sum_{x\in G}\mu_x
\min\{x^2+\lambda,(x-1/2)^2,(x-1)^2-\lambda\}.
\tag{A2}
\]

There are no missing normalization constraints: the row-mass constraints already force total target mass to one. The primal is feasible, and all costs are finite.

The breakpoints in this sign convention are
\(-7/4,-5/4,-3/4,-1/4,1/4,3/4\).
The six potential rows, in this order, are

\[
\tfrac14(-7,-6,-3,9),\quad f_D,\quad f_C,
\quad f_B,\quad f_A,\quad\tfrac14(1,-2,-3,1).
\]

The first is componentwise dominated by \(f_D\), and the last by \(f_A\). Outside the extreme breakpoints, the dual objective cannot improve. This proves (A1) for every probability vector, not just a finite numerical grid.

### Exact contraction certificates

Set \(q=15/16\). The following are identities between column coefficient vectors:

\[
\begin{aligned}
q(\tfrac9{10}f_A+\tfrac1{10}f_B)^T-Pf_A^T
&=(0,0,0,\tfrac18)^T,\\
q(\tfrac1{10}f_A+\tfrac9{10}f_B)^T-Pf_B^T
&=0,\\
q(\tfrac2{15}f_A+\tfrac{23}{30}f_C+\tfrac1{10}f_D)^T-Pf_C^T
&=(0,\tfrac3{64},0,0)^T,\\
q(\tfrac{11}{90}f_A+\tfrac{79}{90}f_D)^T-Pf_D^T
&=(\tfrac18,\tfrac{17}{96},\tfrac9{64},0)^T.
\end{aligned}
\tag{A3}
\]

Each mixture is convex and every right-hand side is nonnegative. Multiply by any probability row vector and then take the maximum in (A1). This yields

\[
\boxed{E(\mu P)\le\tfrac{15}{16}E(\mu),
\qquad d_{W_2}(\mu P,\mathcal I)
\le\tfrac{\sqrt{15}}4d_{W_2}(\mu,\mathcal I).}
\tag{A4}
\]

For \(\mu_s=(s,1-2s,0,s)\), \(0<s\le1/4\), direct substitution gives \(E(\mu_s)=s\) and \(E(\mu_sP)=15s/16\). Therefore both displayed factors are sharp. **The factor \(15/16\) is for squared distance, not for \(W_2\).**

The audit independently rediscovered six-face rational contraction certificates by LP, checked them using `fractions.Fraction`, and then independently checked all four shorter identities (A3). Numeric LP is only a certificate-discovery aid; the assertions use exact rational inequalities.

## 4. Optimal coupling, false zeros and nonparacontraction

For the preceding \(\mu_s\), let

\[
\pi_s=(s,1-2s,s,0)\in\mathcal I.
\]

Transport \(0\to0\), \(1/2\to1/2\), and \(2\to1\), preserving their respective masses. This coupling is monotone in the real-line order, hence optimal for squared distance. Alternatively its cost is \(s\), which (A1) proves to be the distance squared to the entire invariant set; this proves both pairwise transport optimality and nearest-invariant optimality without appealing to an unverified coupling claim.

On the state points, the residual is

\[
R=(-1,0,1,1).
\]

It is identical on each of the three coupling pairs. Identity updates also have zero displacement. Consequently

\[
\boxed{\Psi(\mu_s)=0,
\quad d_{W_2}(\mu_s,\mathcal I)=\sqrt{s}>0,
\quad\mu_s\to\delta_{1/2}.}
\tag{A5}
\]

No local gauge vanishing at zero can bound distance to \(\mathcal I\) through this \(\Psi\), even if the outer infimum in its definition is restricted to nearest invariant measures.

### It is genuinely outside paracontraction in measure

Let

\[
\widehat\mu_t=(1/2,0,(1-t)/2,t/2),\quad
\pi_0=(1/2,0,1/2,0),\quad0<t\le1.
\]

The sorted optimal transports before and after one update both give

\[
W_2(\widehat\mu_t,\pi_0)^2
=W_2(\widehat\mu_tP,\pi_0)^2=t/2.
\tag{A6}
\]

Thus strict decrease toward **every** invariant measure fails, while distance to the **set** of invariant measures decreases with (A4). There is no contradiction: the nearest invariant law can change after one step. The equality (A6) directly violates the paracontraction requirement in Luke's Theorem 9.

## 5. Additional audit result: the discrepancy itself IS subregular

This result was not needed by the proposing agent's positive calculation, but is essential to interpreting it correctly.

### Proposition

For this same chain,

\[
\boxed{Z_\Psi:=\Psi^{-1}(0)
=\{(a,b,c,d)\in\Delta_4:a=c+d\},}
\tag{A7}
\]

and

\[
\boxed{d_{W_2}(\mu,Z_\Psi)\le4\Psi(\mu)
\qquad(\mu\in\Delta_4).}
\tag{A8}
\]

Thus \(\Psi\) is globally linearly metrically subregular for zero relative to its actual zero set. This does **not** repair the source's invariant-set error bound, since \(Z_\Psi\supsetneq\mathcal I\).

### Proof

Put \(\delta=a-c-d\) and \(p=1/8\). Every invariant target law \(\pi\) has \(\mathbb E_\pi R=0\), while \(\mathbb E_\mu R=-\delta\). For every coupling, optimal or otherwise,

\[
\int(R(x)-R(y))^2\,d\eta
\ge\int|R(x)-R(y)|\,d\eta
\ge\left|\int(R(x)-R(y))\,d\eta\right|
=|\delta|.
\]

The first inequality uses the integer values \(R\in\{-1,0,1\}\). Therefore

\[
\Psi(\mu)^2\ge p|\delta|.
\tag{A9}
\]

If \(\Psi(\mu)=0\), (A9) forces \(a=c+d\). Conversely, under this equality, send all source mass at \(2\) to \(1\) and retain every other point. The target is \((a,b,c+d,0)\in\mathcal I\), the coupling is monotone and optimal, and it preserves \(R\). Hence its discrepancy is zero, proving (A7).

If \(\delta>0\), move \(\delta/2\) mass from \(0\) to \(1\). There is enough source mass, and the resulting law lies in \(Z_\Psi\); the squared cost is \(\delta/2\).

If \(\delta<0\), move \(-\delta/2\) mass from the combined source class \(\{1,2\}\) to \(0\). There is enough source mass, the new law lies in \(Z_\Psi\), and the squared cost is at most \(2|\delta|\).

Thus always

\[
d_{W_2}(\mu,Z_\Psi)^2\le2|\delta|
\le\frac2p\Psi(\mu)^2=16\Psi(\mu)^2,
\]

which proves (A8).

### What this rules out rhetorically

The assertion “the example disproves necessity of metric subregularity” without specifying *distance to invariants and zero identification* is false as a description of the example. In standard variational-analysis terminology, the displayed discrepancy satisfies metric subregularity. Its zeros simply encode residual-indistinguishability classes, not stationarity.

## 6. Convergence to an individual law, with uniform relative rate

Let \(R=T_\#\). Then \(R^3=R\). For \(a_k=(7/8)^k\) and \(r_k=(3/4)^k\), direct binomial expansion gives

\[
\mu P^k=a_k\mu+\frac{1-r_k}{2}R\mu+
\left(\frac{1+r_k}{2}-a_k\right)R^2\mu.
\tag{A10}
\]

The three coefficients are the probabilities of zero, odd, and positive-even numbers of nonidentity updates. In particular they are nonnegative and sum to one. The limit is

\[
\pi^\mu=\tfrac12(R\mu+R^2\mu)
=((1-b)/2,b,(1-b)/2,0)\in\mathcal I.
\]

This proves convergence, independently of any residual error-bound theorem. It also gives a uniform absolute geometric estimate on the finite space.

A stronger relative estimate follows from (A4). Both selected maps are nonexpansive, so synchronous coupling proves that \(P\) is nonexpansive in \(W_2\). Hence each orbit is Fejér monotone toward every invariant law. Fix \(k\), choose a nearest invariant law \(\bar\pi_k\) to \(\mu P^k\), and let future iterates tend to \(\pi^\mu\). Then

\[
W_2(\pi^\mu,\bar\pi_k)
\le W_2(\mu P^k,\bar\pi_k)
=d_{W_2}(\mu P^k,\mathcal I).
\]

The triangle inequality and (A4) imply

\[
\boxed{W_2(\mu P^k,\pi^\mu)
\le2\left(\frac{\sqrt{15}}4\right)^k
d_{W_2}(\mu,\mathcal I).}
\tag{A11}
\]

Thus there is common one-step Q-contraction to the invariant set and uniform relative R-linear convergence to an individual invariant law, for all initial laws. No trajectory-specific initialization restriction hides the phenomenon.

## 7. What is actually refuted

The following precise claim is false:

> On compact Euclidean state domains, for continuous random updates that are almost firmly nonexpansive in expectation, global uniform one-step Q-contraction toward the invariant set and uniform quantitative convergence to individual invariant laws force the source synchronous invariant discrepancy to satisfy an invariant-identifying error bound.

All premises hold by (A4), (A11), and the expected almost-firm inequality. The proposed conclusion fails by (A5).

The following are **not** refuted:

1. The sufficient theorems in HLS, Luke, or X-FEL, which assume the failed identification/error-bound condition.
2. Luke's paracontractive necessity theorem: (A6) excludes its hypotheses.
3. Necessity of a gauge bound to \(\Psi^{-1}(0)\): (A8) proves that bound here.
4. Necessity restricted in advance to residuals with \(\Psi^{-1}(0)=\mathcal I\).
5. The existence of some other metrically subregular residual in some other appropriate metric. For instance, every genuine one-step set contraction yields a displacement lower bound by the triangle inequality.
6. Every random proximal, block-coordinate, or X-FEL subclass, without verifying its additional structure.

The appropriate claim is therefore **a negative result for necessity of the source's invariant-identifying synchronous-discrepancy certificate**. The original prose is relevant motivation, but is not a license to erase the distinction between identification and ordinary metric subregularity.

## 9. Additional scope theorem: exact zeros on a compact domain already give some gauge

This observation matters for the proposed next strengthening by countably many shrinking blocks. It prevents a new overclaim even if that construction is completed.

Suppose \(G\) is compact, finitely many update maps are continuous, and \(\mathcal I\ne\varnothing\). Then \(\mathscr P(G)\) is compact in \(W_2\); invariant laws form a closed set. The set of triples \((\mu,\pi,\eta)\), with \(\pi\in\mathcal I\) and \(\eta\in C_*(\mu,\pi)\), is closed and compact: marginals vary continuously, the squared transport cost is continuous, and so is \(W_2^2(\mu,\pi)\). The discrepancy integrand is continuous and bounded. Consequently its minimized value \(\Psi\) is lower semicontinuous and its defining minimum is attained.

If in addition \(\Psi^{-1}(0)=\mathcal I\), define

\[
h(t)=\sup\{d_{W_2}(\mu,\mathcal I):\Psi(\mu)\le t\}.
\]

Then \(h\) is nondecreasing, \(h(0)=0\), and \(h(t)\to0\) as \(t\downarrow0\). Otherwise compactness supplies a convergent subsequence of laws bounded away from \(\mathcal I\) with discrepancies tending to zero, contradicting lower semicontinuity and exact zeros. Thus

\[
d_{W_2}(\mu,\mathcal I)\le h(\Psi(\mu)).
\]

A continuous strictly increasing majorant with value zero at zero can also be chosen locally and extended globally. Therefore **no exact-zero compact continuous example can disprove existence of every generalized gauge**. A shrinking-block construction can disprove linear or all positive Hölder gauges, or gauges required to satisfy a specified contraction-compatibility condition; these are stronger, separately meaningful claims. This existence observation supplies no useful explicit modulus and does not make every source convergence condition automatic.

The shrinking-block strengthening was communicated after the initial audit. It has now been independently audited in the addendum below. Its key transport decoupling is proved by stitching bounded local LP dual potentials and checking every cross-block dual constraint; it does not merely assume that transport remains within each block.

## 12. Independent proof audit of the natural projection example

Let \(G=[0,1]^2\), \(\beta=(\delta_0+\delta_1)/2\), and choose

\[
T_j(x,y)=(x,j),\qquad j\in\{0,1\},
\]

with equal probabilities. These are the actual Euclidean projections onto the two closed convex horizontal faces. Each is firmly nonexpansive, continuous, and a self-map of compact convex \(G\). They have no common fixed point. Independence of the new index from the input gives

\[
\mu P=\mu_x\otimes\beta,
\qquad \mathcal I=\{\nu\otimes\beta:\nu\in\mathscr P([0,1])\}.
\]

In particular \(P^2=P\), and every law reaches its invariant limit in one update.

For any pair \((x,y),(x',y')\), the difference of synchronous displacements is \((0,y-y')\), irrespective of the chosen index. Every invariant target has second marginal \(\beta\). Thus every admissible coupling gives a discrepancy cost at least \(W_2(\mu_y,\beta)^2\).

For the matching upper bound, choose invariant target \(\delta_a\otimes\beta\), with any fixed \(a\in[0,1]\). Lift an optimal coupling of \(\mu_y\) and \(\beta\) by the conditional distribution of the original \(x\) given \(y\). Its horizontal transport cost is the coupling-independent number \(\int|x-a|^2d\mu\). It is therefore optimal for the full two-dimensional squared distance as well as for the vertical discrepancy. Consequently

\[
\boxed{\Psi(\mu)=W_2(\mu_y,\beta),\qquad
\Psi^{-1}(0)=\{\mu:\mu_y=\beta\}.}
\tag{B1}
\]

Changing only the \(y\) coordinate through an optimal marginal coupling proves

\[
d_{W_2}(\mu,\Psi^{-1}(0))=\Psi(\mu).
\tag{B2}
\]

Thus the ordinary error bound is exact with constant one, while independence of the coordinates is invisible to \(\Psi\).

For

\[
\mu_t=\tfrac12\delta_{(1/2-t,0)}+\tfrac12\delta_{(1/2+t,1)},
\qquad0<t<1/2,
\]

the discrepancy is zero but the exact distance to \(\mathcal I\) is \(t\). Here is an independent lower-bound proof retaining arbitrary invariant targets and arbitrary couplings. Center the horizontal coordinate, write the source as \((tS,(S+1)/2)\), and an invariant target as \((X,(W+1)/2)\), where \(S,W\) are each symmetric signs and \(X\in[-1/2,1/2]\) is independent of \(W\). Put \(m=\Pr(S\ne W)\). Then

\[
\mathbb E[WX]=0,
\qquad |\mathbb E[SX]|\le\mathbb E[|S-W||X|]\le m.
\]

Every squared transport cost is therefore at least

\[
t^2+\mathbb E[X^2]-2t\mathbb E[SX]+m
\ge t^2+\mathbb E[X^2]+(1-2t)m\ge t^2.
\]

The same-\(y\) coupling to \(\delta_{1/2}\otimes\beta\) attains equality. This invariant target is nearest, so restricting the outer infimum to nearest invariant laws does not remove the false zero. Toward this same invariant anchor the distance is \(t\) both before and after the update; hence the Markov operator is not a paracontraction.

The actual limit \(\mu_tP\) is also a false-zero anchor: among its four quarter-mass atoms, a transport preserving \(y\) costs \(2t^2\). The only competing elementary assignment switches \(y\) and costs \(1/2\). Since \(t<1/2\), the same-\(y\) assignment is optimal. Exact rational enumeration of all six extreme couplings confirms this calculation. Every finite nonempty composition of the selected maps retains the same structural form \((x,y)\mapsto(x,j_{\rm last})\); consequently merely accumulating synchronous displacement differences over finitely many updates cannot see the missing dependence either.

**Scope:** this is a genuine random convex-proximal example under the broad HLS and X-FEL abstract assumptions, but is not a claim about the specific X-FEL likelihood specialization or every literal block-coordinate index convention in Luke's paper.

## 13. Independent proof audit of the countable exact-zero construction

### 13.1 Local blocks and costs

For \(0<e\le1/100\), let \(h=2-e\), and use positions \(0,1/2,1,h\) with the unchanged transition table \((1,1/2,0,1)\). Denote its squared distance to the stationary set by \(E_e\). ### 13.2 Compact state domain, continuity and uniform regularity

Set

t_n=2^{-n},\quad d_n=2^{-3n-10},\quad e_n=d_n^n,

The last inequality follows from \(d_m\le d_n/8\) and \(t_n/d_n=2^{2n+10}\ge4096\). Within a block the largest secant slope is \(1/(1-e_n)\le100/99\); across blocks, the total displacement perturbation is at most \(d_n+d_m\), so (C2) bounds the ratio by \(5/4\). No finite-dimensionality assumption on the *space of measures* has been inserted: the source allows compact state domains, and a compact countable subset of the real line qualifies.

### 13.3 Stationarity and the complete transport dual

Stationarity of each fourth-state singleton forces its mass to be zero. The first and third singleton equations in each block then force equal cyclic endpoint masses. Every midpoint and zero are fixed and have arbitrary masses. Conversely these conditions are plainly sufficient. This determines all invariant probability laws, including laws spread over infinitely many blocks.

Let \(S\) be the union of supports of invariant laws; it is \(G\) with the fourth state of each block removed. Transport targets are supported on \(S\). For a local balance multiplier, an optimum can be selected in

\]

To check this, the extreme breakpoints \(-3/4\) and \(h-1/4\) are dominated, respectively, by \(-1/4\) and \(h-3/4\); outside all breakpoints the dual cannot improve. The source potential is bounded above by \(9/4\), while the destination potential has absolute value at most \(5/4\). Scale these by \(d_n^2\). The destination potential has mean zero under every invariant local law because it is opposite at the two equally weighted cyclic endpoints and zero at the midpoint.

By (C2), for source block \(n\) and destination block \(m\),

\[

Thus the local duals fit together on **\(G\times S\)**, including comparison with zero, where both potentials are zero. \[
\boxed{d_{W_2}(\mu,\mathcal I)^2

Terms with \(m_n=0\) are zero and require no chosen conditional law. The mass at zero is coupled diagonally.

The \(W_2\) factor is \(\sqrt{3125/3267}\); no sharpness is asserted for this deliberately conservative common bound.

### 13.4 Exact discrepancy zeros, including the infinite-block issue

The nonzero displacement labels in block \(n\) are \(-d_n,d_n,d_n(1-e_n)\). They are pairwise distinct both within and between blocks, since consecutive scales differ by a factor eight and \(1-e_n\ge99/100\). The zero label occurs exactly at the fixed states.

### 13.5 Point convergence and finite Wasserstein length

The block table and the fixed point zero give \(T^3=T\) globally. Let \(R=T_\#\), \(\Pi=(R+R^2)/2\), and \(L=5/4\). Then \(R\Pi=\Pi\) and \(\Pi\mu\in\mathcal I\), while \(\operatorname{Lip}\Pi\le L^2\) in \(W_2\).

\[

\le2aL^4 W_2(\mu,\Pi\mu)^2.

If \(a>1/2\), the original binomial mixture gives the same upper bound because its total weight is one, at most \(2a\). For any nearest invariant law \(\pi\),

Consequently

\[
\boxed{W_2(\mu P^k,\Pi\mu)

## 14. Additional corollary: rate-compatible general gauges also fail here

The elementary compactness theorem still supplies some general gauge. It must not be confused with a gauge compatible with the source almost-firm estimate and strict contraction.

HLS equation (20) gives, with \(\tau=(1-\alpha)/\alpha>0\),

\[
\theta(t)^2=(1+\epsilon_f)t^2-\tau[\rho^{-1}(t)]^2.
\]

For fixed \(\epsilon_f>0\), the requirement \(\theta(t)<t\) forces

\[
\rho^{-1}(t)>\sqrt{\epsilon_f/\tau}\,t,
\]

or, equivalently, a local linear envelope
\(\rho(s)<\sqrt{\tau/\epsilon_f}\,s\).
Such an envelope is impossible for the countable model by (C8).

One cannot avoid this by choosing an exact zero violation in a neighborhood of zero. Within any sufficiently small block, compare its fourth point with its third point. The input separation is \(d_n(1-e_n)\), while the nonidentity outputs are separated by \(d_n\). Their expected squared output distance divided by input distance squared is

\[
\tfrac78+\frac1{8(1-e_n)^2}>1.
\]

Every neighborhood of the state zero contains such a complete block. Therefore any valid fixed expected almost-firm inequality on that neighborhood requires positive violation, independent of the chosen \(\alpha\). The third point belongs to an invariant support, so the same obstruction applies to the later source's invariant-support-anchored variant.

Hence the countable example rules out not only linear/Hölder gauges but also **any gauge which, with fixed valid almost-firm parameters, yields strict one-step contraction through the source scalar formula**. This is fully consistent with existence of a slower, noncompatible general gauge.
